# Regulatory & Political Brief: What Can and Cannot Be Moved on the Dulles Rebuild

**Run:** Future-Proofing the Dulles Terminal Rebuild
**Lens:** The federal, state, and local forces that decide which of the design team's "durable vs. volatile" bets are actually theirs to make.
**Core claim:** The two design questions the thesis treats as open — whether screening decentralizes and whether the gate-to-aircraft relationship loosens — are not primarily design questions. They are federal control questions (TSA and CBP), and those agencies move on rulemaking-and-appropriations time, not program time. A 2034 completion plan that assumes the political environment is a variable it can solve is mispricing its biggest risk. The perimeter rule that built Dulles's entire long-haul business case took 58 years to move — and when it finally moved in 2024, it moved *against* MWAA.

---

## 1. Key Findings

- **The landlord is now a political actor with a three-year half-life.** DOT holds title to Dulles; the lease was extended to 2100 in April 2024; and in December 2025 Secretary Duffy opened RFI docket DOT-OST-2025-1887 explicitly driving a ~$22B rebuild to a 2034 target, tied to Executive Order 14344 on federal architecture [Source: https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-launches-new-initiative-revitalize]. The federal sponsor pushing this program is an appointee whose mandate expires with the administration. **Treat federal sponsorship as a volatile input, not a durable one: it must survive the January 2029 transition to reach a 2034 ribbon-cutting.**

- **Distributed screening is a TSA decision, not a design choice — and TSA has scheduled nothing.** Federal law (49 U.S.C. §44901) requires TSA to screen all passengers and property, and the checkpoint configuration is governed by TSA's Checkpoint Requirements and Planning Guide (CRPG, updated May 2025) [Source: https://www.tsa.gov/sites/default/files/checkpoint-requirements-and-planning-guide.pdf]. Self-service/distributed screening exists as a single DHS S&T prototype at one airport (Harry Reid/LAS), PreCheck-only, with TSA stating it will study results "before they announce a timeline for any future deployment" [Source: https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/releases/2024/03/06/tsa-and-dhs-st-prototype-self-service-screening-system-harry-reid]. **Betting built form on decentralized screening by 2035 is betting on an unscheduled federal rulemaking. Overbuild the provisioning; do not draw the building around it.**

- **CBP's design standard hard-constrains international concourse geometry — and CBP staffing, not gates, is the real cap on international growth.** The Airport Technical Design Standards (ATDS, 2021 version, three-year cycle) mandate one-way sterile corridors, physical separation of arriving international passengers, and rate one double primary booth at ~100 passengers/hour [Source: https://www.sskies.org/images/uploads/subpage/PARAS_0002.CBPATDSCompanionGuide.FinalReport.pdf]. But officers are appropriated by Congress; the Reimbursable Services Program only lets airports buy *overtime*, not permanent officer lines [Source: https://www.cbp.gov/border-security/public-private-partnerships/reimbursable-services-program/faqs]. **MWAA can build international gates CBP will not staff. FIS geometry is a 50-year durable constraint; CBP headcount is an annual political one.**

- **The PFC cap has been frozen at $4.50 since the 2000 Aviation Investment and Reform Act, and the 2024 FAA Reauthorization declined to raise it — the fourth failed attempt since 2017** [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/pfc]. Proposals to lift it to $8.50 (Rebuilding America's Airport Infrastructure Act) have never reached a floor vote. **The single largest per-passenger self-help revenue lever for financing "overbuild now" provisioning is capped by statute and will not move inside this program's financing window.**

- **The BIL airport money that could underwrite soft provisioning sunsets September 30, 2026.** Roughly $3.9B/year in Airport Infrastructure Grants and the Airport Terminal Program expires without reauthorization [Source: https://aaae.org/uscta-alerts/appropriations-leaders-unveil-final-fy26-dot/faa-and-dhs/tsa/cbp-spending-bills]. The next FAA reauthorization is due in 2028. **The provisioning decisions that are cheap now (conduit, power, floor loading, riser space) must be financed against a discretionary program that expires before the first concourse is poured.**

- **MWAA is a 17-member board answering to four principals, and it is already leveraging hard.** Seven Virginia, four D.C., three federal, two Maryland appointees, plus two user representatives [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Washington_Airports_Authority]. MWAA plans ~$5.5B of new debt through 2028, pushing debt per O&D enplanement toward ~$400 (from $223 in 2024) and coverage down to ~1.3x, while signatory CPE rises from $9.56 (FY25) toward $12.77 (2026 H1) [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/dulles-rfi-analysis]. Ratings sit at AA-/AA-/Aa3 stable [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/financial-statistics/investor-relations/credit-ratings]. **Every "overbuild for the future" dollar competes against a rising CPE the airlines must sign off on through the use-and-lease agreement, which runs to 2039.**

- **The perimeter rule is the canonical proof that these forces have multi-decade half-lives and can move against you.** The 1966 DCA perimeter rule existed to funnel long-haul traffic to Dulles [Source: https://avgeeks.aero/airports/the-dca-perimeter-rule-explained/]. In 2024, Congress added 10 beyond-perimeter slots over the *unanimous* objection of both home-state delegations (VA and MD senators) [Source: https://thehill.com/homenews/4655051-senate-passes-faa-reauthorization-after-dca-slots-fight/]. **The policy that created IAD's business case took 58 years to erode and eroded against the operator's wishes even with its own senators fighting. Do not plan around a favorable act of Congress.**

## 2. Evidence: Regulation vs. Politics

The thesis's success criterion is a durable-vs-volatile split. The useful regulatory cut is orthogonal to it: **"regulation" moves slowly but predictably through agency process; "politics" can move fast, move against you, or not move at all — and you cannot schedule it.**

### This is regulation (agency rulemaking / design standard; changes on a technical cycle)

**TSA checkpoint form.** §44901 mandates federal screening of all passengers; the CRPG dictates lane geometry, queuing, and equipment envelopes. CT scanners (larger, heavier, more power- and cooling-hungry than legacy AT units) and Credential Authentication Technology are already reshaping the *centralized* checkpoint footprint — deeper divest zones, higher floor loads, more power [Source: https://www.tsa.gov/sites/default/files/checkpoint-requirements-and-planning-guide.pdf]. This is the near-certain design driver, and it points *toward* a larger centralized checkpoint before any distribution. The durable move is to provision structure and utilities for a checkpoint that can both grow (CT) and later thin (self-service), because you cannot predict which TSA delivers first.

**CBP FIS.** The ATDS governs the entire international arrivals chain — gate-to-primary sterile corridor, mandatory separation, baggage hall, secondary — and it is revised on a three-year cycle, so the geometry a concourse is built around today is knowable and stable for a generation [Source: https://www.sskies.org/images/uploads/subpage/PARAS_0002.CBPATDSCompanionGuide.FinalReport.pdf]. This is a genuine 50-year durable constraint and should be treated as fixed built form, not a soft variable.

**NEPA.** FAA Order 1050.1G (effective June 30, 2025) replaced the rescinded CEQ regulations and now caps an EIS at 2 years/150 pages and an EA at 1 year/75 pages, with expanded categorical exclusions [Source: https://esassoc.com/news-and-ideas/2025/07/faas-nepa-update-what-you-need-to-know-about-order-1050-1g/]. On paper this *shortens* the Dulles environmental clock. But it rests on 2025 executive action that the D.C. Circuit is already probing, so the streamlined timeline is itself litigable — the "regulation" here has an unusual political overhang.

### This is politics (Congress required it, could change it, but won't — or will, unpredictably)

**The PFC cap ($4.50, frozen since 2000).** Purely a congressional number. Airports (ACI-NA, AAAE) have pushed $8.50+ for a decade; it failed again in the 2024 reauthorization [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/pfc]. There is no agency path to raise it. Half-life: indefinite.

**CBP officer staffing.** Set by annual DHS appropriations, not by how many gates an airport builds. The binding constraint on international throughput is officers on the primary line, and airports can only *rent* overtime through Reimbursable Services [Source: https://www.cbp.gov/border-security/public-private-partnerships/reimbursable-services-program/faqs].

**DCA slots and perimeter.** Set by statute (FAA Reauthorization Acts) and DOT order; universally acknowledged as inefficient; barely movable, and when moved, moved against MWAA (see case study below).

**The federal lease and sponsorship.** DOT's title and the Duffy-era RFI are the most program-specific political facts in this run: the federal government is simultaneously landlord, funder, regulator, and now activist promoter — a configuration with no analog at other US hubs and no guaranteed continuity past 2028.

## 3. Case Studies: Where a Political Force Decided the Outcome

**Case A — The 2024 DCA slot fight: the home delegation lost.** For 58 years the perimeter rule protected IAD's long-haul franchise. In 2024, Section 502 of P.L. 118-63 added 10 beyond-perimeter slot exemptions; DOT awarded them in December 2024 to five carriers flying DCA nonstop to San Diego, San Antonio, Seattle, Las Vegas, and San Francisco [Source: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12504]. All four Virginia and Maryland senators opposed it and lost [Source: https://thehill.com/homenews/4655051-senate-passes-faa-reauthorization-after-dca-slots-fight/]. MWAA formally advocated to preserve the rule and lost [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/protecting-dca-perimeter]. **Lesson for the rebuild: a program that needs any future congressional favor — a PFC increase, a slot protection, an earmark — is building on ground that shifts against the operator even when its own delegation is fighting for it.** MWAA partially hedged financially (it collects $1M from DCA net revenue per beyond-perimeter pair, but only when DCA's CPE runs $2 below Dulles's), which tells you the sophisticated operator plans for the political loss, not the political win.

**Case B — CBP staffing as the invisible cap.** Preclearance and FIS economics show the same pattern: infrastructure is necessary but not sufficient. CBP precleared 22M+ travelers in 2025 (14.6% of all US-bound air passengers) from just 16 foreign locations, and a single daily international frequency generates 30–50 on-site jobs and $10–15M in regional spending [Source: https://airportscouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Economic-Value-of-US-Preclearance-Final.pdf]. That upside is gated by officer availability, set in appropriations. **A Dulles concourse designed for international growth that CBP will not staff is a stranded asset in the most expensive part of the building.** The gate-flexibility question in the thesis has a federal-headcount answer, not just an apron-geometry answer.

**Case C — The federal RFI as program driver.** The $22B figure, the 2034 date, the plan to extend AeroTrain and retire the mobile lounges, and the eastward/westward expansion of the Saarinen headhouse originate from a DOT-driven RFI (docket DOT-OST-2025-1887), not from MWAA's own board-approved master plan, which had contemplated a more incremental ~$7–9B capital program [Source: https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-05-12/us-plans-22-billion-rebuilding-of-washington-dulles-airport; https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/dulles-rfi-analysis]. **The ambition is federally sponsored and federally timed. If the sponsor changes in 2029, the program's scope, tempo, and financing assumptions are all exposed.** The durable design decisions must be defensible under a scenario where the federal push evaporates and MWAA is left carrying the debt at a CPE the airlines can contest through 2039.

## 5. What This Binds — Mapped to MWAA's Decision Calendar

| Force | Type | MWAA decision it binds | Realistic timeframe to change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TSA CRPG / §44901 checkpoint control | Regulation | Checkpoint footprint at April 2027 concourse construction start; provisioning depth | Unscheduled; CT expansion is near-term certain, distribution is a bet with no date |
| CBP ATDS (FIS geometry) | Regulation | International concourse column grid, corridor, level changes — lock now | 3-year revision cycle; stable, treat as 50-year durable |
| CBP officer staffing | Politics (appropriations) | How much international gate capacity to actually build vs. provision | Annual; will not track your CIP |
| PFC $4.50 cap | Politics | Financing plan for soft provisioning; PAYGO vs. bond mix | Not before the 2028 reauthorization, if ever |
| BIL Airport Terminal Program | Politics | Grant assumptions in the FY26–28 bond issuances | Sunsets Sept 30, 2026 |
| FAA Order 1050.1G (NEPA) | Regulation (with political overhang) | Record of Decision date on the critical path to 2034 | Faster on paper; litigation-exposed |
| DOT lease / federal sponsorship | Politics (program-specific) | Scope, tempo, and financing of the whole $22B program | ~3-year half-life; exposed to Jan 2029 transition |
| DCA slots/perimeter | Politics | IAD long-haul demand base the terminal is sized around | 58 years to move once; moved against MWAA |

**Bottom line for the design and executive teams:** Lock the CBP-driven international geometry and the structural/utility provisioning now — those are the constraints that are stable *because* they are regulatory. Leave the checkpoint deliberately soft and over-provisioned, but do not draw the building as if TSA has decided to decentralize, because it has not. And price every element that depends on a future congressional or appropriations decision — PFC increase, CBP officers, slot protection, sustained federal sponsorship — as a bet against a body that has, for 25 years, reliably declined to act. The economics of this terminal are more forecastable than its politics. Plan the durable form around the regulation, and hedge the politics.
