# Airport CEO Brief — Future-Proofing the Dulles Terminal Rebuild

*Independent Stage 1 research brief. Lens: the chief executive's chair. The design question — durable vs. volatile built form under decentralizing passenger processing — is real. But from this chair it is the second question. The first is whether a $22B, federally-driven program survives the bond covenants, the airline agreement we just signed, and a board none of us fully controls. A terminal that future-proofs the passenger sequence and breaks the credit is a loss.*

---

## 1. Key findings (for a board chair to absorb in one read)

- **We are being handed a program roughly triple what our own board approved, financed almost entirely with debt.** MWAA's July 2025 board-approved Dulles master plan is ~$7B, phased across decades. The federal "revitalization" plan launched by the DOT RFI is ~$22B by 2034, of which **$21.8B is new bonds** and only $1.1B is airport fees [Source: https://www.ffxnow.com/2026/05/12/report-new-plan-to-overhaul-dulles-airport-would-eliminate-mobile-lounges/]. This is not a design decision wearing a finance costume. It is a leverage decision wearing a design costume.

- **The credit math does not clear at $22B.** We already plan ~$5.5B of new debt through 2028, which pushes adjusted debt per enplanement toward ~$400 and drops net-revenue coverage from 1.63x (2024) to ~1.3x [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/financial-statistics/investor-relations/credit-ratings]. That is on ~$4.9B of existing revenue-bond debt. Layering $21.8B on top is not an adjustment to those metrics; it is a different rating category. Our current AA-/AA-/Aa3 is not survivable at that leverage without a rate structure the airlines have not agreed to fund.

- **We just re-papered the airline agreement for a $9B world, not a $22B one.** The new 15-year Use & Lease (effective 1/1/2025, running to 2039) contemplates a **$9B** capital program under a hybrid-residual framework with a 1.40x coverage target and a Majority-in-Interest provision [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement]. A $22B program is outside the envelope the signatories priced. MII means United can slow or reshape what it did not sign up to pay for.

- **Our concentration risk is the single most important number on the page: United is ~68% of flights at IAD; Delta is a distant 4%** [Source: https://simpleflying.com/washington-dulles-5-largest-airlines-market-share/]. Rating agencies flag single-carrier concentration above 50% as a credit consideration [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/airport-bond-ratings]. We are building a 25-year building for one airline's network decision. Pittsburgh built $600M for US Airways on a 30-year lease and watched CPE go from $6 to $13.80 when the carrier dehubbed [Source: https://crankyflier.com/2012/07/17/across-the-aisle-from-pittsburgh-airports-executive-director-on-pittsburghs-rise-and-fall-as-a-hub-part-1/]. That is the tail risk the design conversation keeps forgetting.

- **This program is not ours to fully scope.** The airports sit on a federal lease held by DOT (extended in 2024 to 2100), and three of our seventeen board seats are presidential appointments [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Washington_Airports_Authority]. The $22B push originated with the U.S. DOT, not the authority [Source: https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-launches-new-initiative-revitalize]. A CEO who treats scope as an internal capital-planning question has misread who is actually holding the pen.

- **The federal grant window that would soften the debt is closing, not opening.** The PFC is still capped at $4.50 (unchanged since 2000, and again in the 2024 reauthorization), and the IIJA terminal-grant supplements expire 9/30/2026 [Source: https://aaae.org/uscta-alerts/lawmakers-reach-agreement-on-final-faa-bill]. The one-time federal money that could reduce the bond ask is timing out exactly as the mega-program ramps.

- **The durable/volatile design split is right — and it is the CEO's best hedge.** Overbuilding structure, apron geometry, and utility/comms provisioning (50-year life) while keeping screening, fit-out, and IT deliberately soft (5–15-year life) is the version of this that a rating agency and a signatory airline can both live with. It de-risks the one thing you cannot refinance: poured concrete in the wrong place.

## 2. Evidence

### The bond-rating lens

All three agencies score the same fundamentals — enplanement trend, carrier concentration, cost per enplanement, debt service coverage (DSCR), days cash on hand, and the legal strength of the rate covenant. Fitch's framework runs five factors (Revenue Risk–Volume, Revenue Risk–Price, Infrastructure Development & Renewal, Debt Structure, Financial Profile) and flags single-carrier concentration above ~50% [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/airport-bond-ratings]. Moody's uses a scorecard built on its February 2023 U.S. airport methodology, measuring leverage as debt to O&D enplaned passengers and watching liquidity closely — for context, its SFO downgrade trigger sits at sustained days-cash below 450 [Source: https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/moodys-upgrades-san-francisco-airport-bond-rating].

MWAA today is rated Aa3/AA-/AA- (Moody's/S&P/Fitch), stable, reaffirmed May 2025, on ~$4.9B of outstanding revenue debt [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/financial-statistics/investor-relations/credit-ratings]. The **planned** ~$5.5B issuance through 2028 already takes coverage from 1.63x to roughly 1.3x and adjusted debt per enplanement to ~$400. Fitch specifically credited the system AUL that links DCA and IAD and its cost-recovery approach as a rating strength. That is the asset the $22B program puts at risk.

Precedent that the board should hold in mind: LAX is issuing into a **$15B** capital plan and Moody's expects weaker leverage and coverage but *still within Aa3* [Source: https://www.lawa.org/sites/lawa/files/2025-03/Moodys%20Ratings%20Letter.pdf]. LAX absorbs $15B against ~40M+ O&D enplanements in the nation's second-largest local market. Dulles has ~27.3M enplanements, heavily connecting, on one carrier [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport]. The same dollar of debt does not buy the same rating here. $22B at Dulles is a categorically heavier lift than $15B at LAX.

### Airline use-and-lease structure

The current agreement is a hybrid-residual framework: signatory airlines collectively cover costs allocable to Airline Supported Areas, with an Extraordinary Coverage Protection provision and a settlement mechanism requiring airlines to cure any deficiency within 120 days of fiscal-year close; the coverage target is 1.40x, 15 points above the bond covenant floor [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement]. It runs to **2039** and was negotiated around a **~$9B** program — the board also used it to address the long-running Reagan/Dulles cost imbalance [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-board-approves-use-and-lease-agreement-airlines-addresses-reagan-dulles].

Residual frameworks put the risk on the airport's ability to make airlines whole (lower airport upside, strong bondholder protection); compensatory frameworks fix airline rates and leave residual risk with the airport. MWAA's hybrid is bondholder-friendly *as long as the airlines can and will pay*. That is exactly the clause that fails when one carrier is 68% of the field and decides the CPE has gotten too rich — which is precisely what drove IAD's last cost crisis, when CPE peaked at $26.47 in 2013 after the AeroTrain and international-arrivals build [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport]. The Majority-in-Interest provision gives the signatory group standing on major capital. The next natural renegotiation window is 2039; a $22B program forces that conversation open a decade early, from a weaker position.

### The federal stack

PFC capped at $4.50 since 2000, left unchanged in the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 (signed 5/16/2024, FY24–28); AIP authorized at ~$4B/yr through FY28; the IIJA Airport Terminal Program was $5B over five years, with the supplemental programs expiring **9/30/2026** [Source: https://aaae.org/uscta-alerts/lawmakers-reach-agreement-on-final-faa-bill]. Federal Inspection Services (CBP) and TSA checkpoint decisions run on their own appropriations calendars, not ours — a decentralized-screening design depends on a TSA regulatory posture that does not yet exist. The through-line: this thesis leans on federal authorizations that are *not secured*. The RFI is an invitation, not a check [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/iad-rfi].

### Board governance

Seventeen directors: seven appointed by the Virginia governor, four by the DC mayor, three by the U.S. President, three by the Maryland governor; six-year terms, one reappointment; a chair elected annually [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/about-authority/board-directors]. MWAA is an interstate-compact authority created by Virginia and the District, codified in both codes, operating the airports under a federal lease — it is neither a city department nor a conventional single-jurisdiction operating authority [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Washington_Airports_Authority]. That structure is a strength (insulation from any single mayor or governor) and a specific vulnerability here: the presidential seats and the DOT-held lease give the federal government direct, structural leverage over a program the administration is personally championing [Source: https://www.transportation.gov/testimony/dots-role-regarding-operations-two-metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-airports]. Annual chair rotation means no strategy that requires more than a couple of years of unbroken chair support is safe unless it is embedded in debt documents and the AUL, which outlast any chair.

### The host region and the CEO's calendar

The airline-served business community (the Committee for Dulles and Northern Virginia economic interests) wants the gateway and the 90M-passenger growth vision the master plan describes [Source: https://www.ffxnow.com/2025/07/17/new-master-plan-for-dulles-airport-envisions-growth-to-90-million-annual-passengers/]. That is the natural coalition *for* the program. The organized opposition is quieter and arrives later: bondholders and rating analysts, a signatory airline being asked to backstop coverage on debt it did not size, and the toll-road/transit constituency already leaning on MWAA's balance sheet through the Silver Line. CEO Jack Potter came from outside aviation (former Postmaster General) — the role at this scale is capital allocation, rating-agency and investor relations, airline negotiation, and government affairs far more than terminal operations [Source: https://www.committeefordulles.org/news/john-e-jack-potter-to-receive-the-committee-for-dulles-tower-of-dulles-award]. Peer large-hub CEOs (LAX, SFO, DEN) are spending this cycle on exactly the same thing: financing mega-programs without losing the rating.

### Long-tenure strategic patterns (2000–2025)

- **Got wrong — single-carrier overbuild.** Pittsburgh's $600M US Airways hub on a 30-year lease; the carrier dehubbed in 2004, CPE ran from $6 to $13.80, and the airport nearly went under [Source: https://crankyflier.com/2012/07/17/across-the-aisle-from-pittsburgh-airports-executive-director-on-pittsburghs-rise-and-fall-as-a-hub-part-1/]. Dehubbing leaves stranded, overbuilt gates that airports rarely refill.
- **Got wrong — betting the schedule on unproven technology in the concrete.** Denver's original automated baggage system and, twenty years later, the $1.8B Great Hall P3 that the airport's own CEO took public blame for [Source: https://www.constructiondive.com/news/denver-airport-chief-takes-blame-for-18b-great-hall-p3-debacle/573095/]. Both hard-wired a bet on a technology timeline into fixed built form.
- **Got right — the durable/volatile discipline and phased optionality.** The programs that aged well overbuilt structure, vertical circulation, and utility/apron capacity while keeping the processing regime soft and phased, and did not stake the amortization schedule on a single carrier or a single technology arrival date.

**Documented vs. widely known:** The ratings, the AUL terms, the board composition, the federal lease, and the $22B/$21.8B-bond figure are documented. What is widely understood but unofficial: that the federal program's scope was set for political optics as much as passenger need, that United's willingness to fund a $22B basis is unproven, and that the mobile-lounge replacement is being driven top-down rather than by the master plan's own priorities.

## 3. The case for and against — as the CEO sees it

**The case FOR embracing an ambitious, future-proofed rebuild.** The federal spotlight is leverage we rarely get: a sitting DOT secretary and administration actively want this built, which can unlock federal cover, expedited environmental review, and political air support that no bond issue buys. Dulles has genuine international growth — 11M international passengers, +11% in 2024 [Source: https://simpleflying.com/washington-dc-airport-new-records-2024/] — and the existing facility is functionally obsolete (mobile lounges, Concourse C/D). Building durable structure *now*, while capital is politically endorsed and while the ground is open, is cheaper than retrofitting a half-measure in 2040. The future-proofing thesis, applied to the 50-year layer, is how you spend a once-in-a-generation political window without betting the credit on the 5-year layer.

**The case AGAINST — the version I will defend to bondholders.** A $22B, debt-financed program layered on 68% single-carrier concentration, under an AUL sized for $9B and running only to 2039, is the Pittsburgh trade at national scale. If United reshapes its hub — or simply refuses the CPE the coverage math requires — the residual clause that protects bondholders becomes the clause that forces a rate death-spiral or a downgrade. The federal enthusiasm that opens the window does not sign the bonds, does not co-sign the coverage covenant, and does not survive the next administration; the debt survives all of them for 30 years. **A CEO who delivers a brilliant, de-sequenced, future-proof terminal and loses the rating and the airline relationship has lost.** The design thesis is correct about built form and silent about who pays and who is left holding the amortization when the carrier's network math changes.

**Where the CEO lands.** Take the political window; refuse the leverage. Use federal endorsement to accelerate the *durable* layer and to press for the one thing that actually helps the credit — federal grant participation and PFC flexibility — rather than to justify $21.8B of pure debt. Design for now, provision for later, and explicitly bet *against* fixing scope at a number the airline agreement did not price and the rating cannot carry.

## 4. Data points and precedents a strategist can use

1. **The leverage gap is ~3x, and it is almost all debt.** Board-approved master plan ~$7B vs. federal program ~$22B, of which $21.8B is new bonds and $1.1B airport fees [Source: https://www.ffxnow.com/2026/05/12/report-new-plan-to-overhaul-dulles-airport-would-eliminate-mobile-lounges/]. Frame every design "want" against which layer of that debt stack it sits in.

2. **Coverage is already thinning before the mega-program.** DSCR falls from 1.63x (2024) to ~1.3x and adjusted debt/enplanement approaches ~$400 on just the planned ~$5.5B; the AUL target is 1.40x [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/financial-statistics/investor-relations/credit-ratings]. There is little headroom left to spend.

3. **LAX absorbs $15B and stays Aa3; Dulles cannot assume the same at $22B** — LAX has ~40M+ O&D in the #2 U.S. market; IAD has ~27.3M enplanements, connecting-heavy, one carrier [Source: https://www.lawa.org/sites/lawa/files/2025-03/Moodys%20Ratings%20Letter.pdf; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport]. Use this as the reality check on any "other big airports are doing it" argument.

4. **Concentration is the number: United 68% / Delta 4%, versus the ~50% level agencies flag** [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/airport-bond-ratings; https://simpleflying.com/washington-dulles-5-largest-airlines-market-share/]. Pit it against the Pittsburgh precedent: $6 → $13.80 CPE post-dehubbing [Source: https://crankyflier.com/2012/07/17/across-the-aisle-from-pittsburgh-airports-executive-director-on-pittsburghs-rise-and-fall-as-a-hub-part-1/].

5. **Two hard calendar dates that constrain the thesis:** IIJA terminal supplements expire 9/30/2026, and the AUL runs to 2039 with a 1.40x target and MII [Source: https://aaae.org/uscta-alerts/lawmakers-reach-agreement-on-final-faa-bill; https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement]. Any move that assumes federal grant money or airline consent must land inside these windows or it is a bet, not a plan.

---

*Bottom line for the panel: the future-proofing framework is the right way to spend the political capital — on the durable, un-refinanceable layer — and the wrong justification for the debt. Measure the thesis in coverage ratios, board votes, and the 2039 AUL, not in passenger-processing diagrams. The design can be perfect and still be a strategic loss.*
