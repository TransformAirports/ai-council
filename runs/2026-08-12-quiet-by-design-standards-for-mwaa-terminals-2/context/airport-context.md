# Airport Context Packet — MWAA (IAD and DCA)

**Run:** `quiet-by-design-standards-for-mwaa-terminals-2`
**Assembled:** 2026-08-10
**Scope of packet:** Establish the shared factual base every Council researcher should know before arguing whether MWAA should adopt a common quiet-by-design terminal standard across Washington Dulles International (IAD) and Ronald Reagan Washington National (DCA).

## 1. Decision framing (from the run prompt)

- **Named operator/authority:** Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority (MWAA), a bi-state independent public authority operating IAD and DCA under a federal lease.
- **Named airports:** IAD (Washington Dulles International) and DCA (Ronald Reagan Washington National).
- **Thesis under debate:** MWAA should establish a common quiet-by-design standard for new and substantially renovated terminal spaces at IAD and DCA — acoustics, PA zoning, information systems, and operational controls specified before designs and procurements harden — without collapsing "quiet" into "fewer announcements."
- **Central question:** which requirements should be mandatory across both airports, which must vary by terminal and operating context, and how MWAA should measure compliance and performance.
- **Output requested:** 1,500–2,000-word article, ~400-word executive summary, technical read-ahead framing, with companion executive deck.

**Fields the operator left blank in the run manifest:** `decision_required`, `decision_owner`, `time_horizon`, `approval_path`, `success_measure`, `operator_context`, `source_paths`, `source_material`, `source_library`. **No operator-supplied sources were attached to this run.** Researchers should treat all context below as assembled from public authoritative documents; no internal MWAA memos, board packets, or design-manual excerpts are in scope for this run.

## 2. Governance and delegated authority

- MWAA is governed by a **17-member Board of Directors** with a split appointment: **7 members by the Governor of Virginia, 4 by the Mayor of the District of Columbia, 3 by the Governor of Maryland, and 3 by the President of the United States**. Members serve six-year terms; one reappointment is permitted.
- **2026 officers (one-year terms beginning January 2026):** Mark Uncapher (Chair), Alex Vogel (Vice Chair), Felice C. Smith (Secretary, re-elected). Trent Morse was seated for a six-year term on January 21, 2026.
- MWAA operates IAD and DCA under a **long-term lease from the U.S. Department of Transportation** (the federal government retains ownership; MWAA operates). This gives federal actors — DOT, FAA, Congress — persistent standing in strategic decisions in a way not true of most large-hub authorities.
- The Board delegates project-level authority through the Design Manual and procurement policies (see §7). Standards changes that affect cost, airline recovery, or federal grant eligibility ordinarily require Board action.

## 3. Financial position and debt capacity

- **Current ratings (all stable outlook, most recent reports May 2025):** Fitch AA−; Moody's Aa3; S&P AA−.
- **Debt trajectory:** MWAA plans approximately **$5.5 billion of new debt between 2025 and 2028**. Adjusted debt per origin-and-destination enplanement is projected to **peak near $400 in 2028**.
- **Coverage compression:** Net revenue debt service coverage is expected to fall from **1.63x in 2024 to roughly 1.3x over the next five years** as new debt service phases in.
- **Implication for a standards conversation:** MWAA is entering the tightest coverage window of the current capital cycle at the same time the run asks it to lock in additional design specificity. Standards that raise commissioning cost or airline recovery need an explicit financial defense; standards that reduce late-stage rework are the easier board sell.

## 4. Airline use-and-lease framework

- A **new 15-year Airline Use and Lease Agreement took effect January 1, 2025**, replacing the prior agreement. At execution the agreement carried an approximately **$9 billion capital construction program** (subsequently expanded — see §5): approximately **$6.99 billion at IAD** (new 14-gate concourse, fifth-runway design work) and approximately **$2.39 billion at DCA** (restrooms and passenger amenities, eventual replacement of the Terminal 1 main concourse).
- Majority-in-interest provisions and specific cost-recovery mechanics were not verified from primary documents in this packet; researchers arguing airline economics must not infer confidential MII thresholds from industry convention.
- **Dominant carriers by airport:** United Airlines is the anchor at IAD (the $20B+ transformation announced in July 2026 was co-branded with United and includes 14 new United gates at Concourse E). American Airlines is historically the largest carrier at DCA. Precise share splits for the current period were not verified in primary sources for this packet.

## 5. Capital program and named projects

- **IAD "$20 billion+ transformation"** was announced July 29, 2026 in a joint appearance by the President, the U.S. Transportation Secretary, United Airlines, and MWAA. Public descriptions include:
  - More than **5 million ft²** of new and refurbished facilities.
  - **Replacement of Concourses C and D.**
  - **Extension of the AeroTrain** and **retirement of the mobile lounges.**
  - **Preservation of the Eero Saarinen Main Terminal** (1978 National Register of Historic Places; 1966 AIA Honor Award).
  - Program figure represents a step-up from the roughly **$7 billion** previously scoped for IAD modernization.
- **Concourse E (IAD midfield):** on schedule to open **fall 2026** with 14 new United gates, direct AeroTrain access, and new/enhanced lounges. Related **Tier 2 Concourse East project (~$898.2M)** is targeted for completion in 2026, with approximately **$235.8M grant-funded**.
- **DCA:** ongoing amenity and restroom program plus a longer-horizon **Terminal 1 main concourse replacement** under the use-and-lease capital plan.
- **What this means for a quiet-by-design standard:** the standards decision is not academic. Concourse C/D replacement, DCA Terminal 1 replacement, and every follow-on IAD program element will be scoped, procured, and commissioned inside the next standards window. Standards adopted late get argued as change orders.

## 6. Passenger and operational context (2025 calendar year)

- **System combined:** approximately **54 million passengers**, a record year.
- **IAD:** **29.01 million passengers**, up 6.4% year-over-year; **10.53 million international passengers**, roughly **36%+ international share** — one of the highest international shares among major U.S. airports. Reported as the highest 2025 growth rate among the fifty largest U.S. airports.
- **DCA:** **24.89 million passengers**, down 5.4% year-over-year. MWAA attributed the decline to the aftermath of the January 29, 2025 midair collision, softening government-related travel demand, and the 43-day federal shutdown.
- **Traffic-mix implication for standards work:** IAD is a hub-and-spoke, international-heavy, connection-dominant terminal complex with substantial non-English-speaking and infrequent-traveler populations. DCA is a slot-controlled, high-frequency, largely domestic O&D operation with a business-traveler skew. A single terminal-quality standard has to survive both profiles.

## 7. MWAA Design Manual and standards apparatus

- MWAA maintains a **seven-volume 2020 Design Manual**, plus **Specifications** and **Estimating and Scheduling Standards**, issued by the MWAA Office of Engineering.
- The Design Manual is described by MWAA as a **mandatory guide with the force of law on the airport property**, updated annually, with airport-specific supporting volumes for IAD and DCA.
- Specifications are edited from **MASTERSPEC** for MWAA use. AutoCAD/MicroStation base files, APM tunnel-station signage files, and Authority-Provided Specifications are made available to designers on request.
- **Implication for the run:** any "quiet-by-design standard" the Council recommends should be framed as an amendment or new volume within this existing Design Manual + Specifications + Estimating apparatus, not as a stand-alone policy. That is the vehicle MWAA already uses to bind consultants, contractors, and tenants.

## 8. Regulatory and political stack

- **January 29, 2025 midair collision at DCA** (PSA/American 5342 and a U.S. Army UH-60L) killed 67 people. The February 2026 NTSB final report found multiple failures across FAA, air traffic control, and military operations, including FAA placement of a helicopter route too close to a runway approach.
- **Post-collision regulatory actions:** FAA banned most non-essential helicopter flights near DCA within days; on March 14, 2025 the FAA removed the section of Helicopter Route 4 between Hains Point and the Woodrow Wilson Bridge from charts and restricted runways 15/33 and 4/22 during specific helicopter operations near DCA. These changes affect DCA operational tempo and irregular-operations messaging load.
- **FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024** (enacted May 16, 2024) authorized **10 additional daily beyond-perimeter slot exemptions at DCA** (5 nonstop round-trips). DOT awarded these in December 2024 to Alaska (SEA), American (SAN), Delta (SFO), Southwest (SAT), and United (LAS), with service starts staggered from February through March 2025. DCA slot control and perimeter rule remain otherwise binding.
- **DOT/White House involvement in IAD transformation:** the July 2026 announcement was a coordinated federal-airport rollout, not a routine MWAA capital communication. This is the political altitude at which IAD standards conversations now happen.

## 9. Reference research base for quiet-terminal work

- **ACRP Research Report 175 — Improving Intelligibility of Airport Terminal Public Address Systems** (Transportation Research Board / FAA-sponsored) is the standing U.S. reference for PA intelligibility design guidance for passenger-processing environments. Researchers arguing measurable performance thresholds should treat this as the domestic baseline document.
- **International peer practice frequently cited as "silent" or "quiet" airport policy:** London City, Helsinki, Barcelona El Prat, Copenhagen, Amsterdam Schiphol, and Dubai. These are the peer set named in the run prompt.
- **North American peer:** SFO's Quiet Airport policy is publicly reported to have removed more than 90 minutes of daily announcement time and reduced onsite noise by more than 40%. The measurement methodology behind the reported reduction was not verified in primary sources for this packet.
