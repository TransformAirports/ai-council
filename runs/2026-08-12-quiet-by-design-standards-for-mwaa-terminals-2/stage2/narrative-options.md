## Option 1 — Board-ready: "The cheapest paragraph MWAA will ever write"

**Thesis (one sentence):** MWAA is about to scope, procure, and commission more than $20 billion of terminal space, and the only cheap moment to specify how those buildings sound — acoustics, PA zoning, intelligibility floors, commissioning verification — is before the designs and contracts harden, which means a tiered quiet-by-design volume in the Design Manual this capital cycle, exercised first on the DCA Terminal 1 replacement.

**Communication job:** By the end, the MWAA board and capital-planning leadership should be prepared to direct the Office of Engineering to draft a tiered quiet-by-design Design Manual amendment, because the standard costs almost nothing written now and becomes a change-order fight written later.

**Opening scene:** July 29, 2026. The President, the Transportation Secretary, United's CEO, and MWAA announce a $20 billion-plus transformation of Dulles — more than 5 million square feet of new and refurbished facilities, Concourses C and D replaced, the AeroTrain extended, the Saarinen terminal preserved. The announcement summary in the record mentions concourses, baggage systems, CBP facilities. It does not mention a single acoustic or public-address requirement. Every one of those square feet will be scoped inside the next standards window.

**Argument sequence (6 beats):**

1. *The window is open and it is closing.* The 2025 AUL carries ~$9B of committed capital ($6.99B IAD, $2.39B DCA); the July 2026 announcement steps IAD up to $20B+. Concourse C/D replacement and DCA Terminal 1 replacement will be scoped now. — E-05 cluster (`airport-ceo::ev-f3c118688838`, `infrastructure-economist::ev-525ee186f14c`, `regulatory-political-analyst::ev-6f4ed135cb27`, `quantitative-analyst::ev-6d299a264850`)
2. *MWAA already owns the vehicle.* The seven-volume Design Manual has "the force of law on the airport property," is updated annually, and binds consultants, contractors, and tenants through MASTERSPEC-derived specifications. This is an amendment, not a new bureaucracy. — E-04 cluster (`airport-ceo::ev-e36d706a5fbe`, `regulatory-political-analyst::ev-1660d73cfa9b`, `virtual-chris::ev-d6d475e3fe78`)
3. *The numbers already exist; MWAA just has to adopt them.* ACRP 175 (STI 0.45 minimum, 0.50 target, SNR 10–15 dB(A)), NFPA 72 Ch. 24 (0.45 measurement / 0.50 ADS average emergency floor), IEC 60268-16:2020 as the measurement method. No R&D required — a specification decision. — E-01 (`quantitative-analyst::ev-d1b6bd6f446b`), E-02 (`quantitative-analyst::ev-8cb15085981e`), E-03 (`operations-analyst::ev-ee92641d9233`)
4. *The financial logic is rework avoidance, not new capex.* DSCR compresses from 1.63× to ~1.3× through 2029; debt per O&D enplanement peaks near $400 in 2028. A standard that raises recovery is a hard sell; one that prevents late-stage redesign is the easy one. CII-derived rework framing (design errors 1–9% of project cost, up to 70% of construction rework) supports the direction without pretending precision. — E-05 (`infrastructure-economist::ev-bdbb755826ed`, `ev-75cbcaa2a2a2` as framing only)
5. *One standard, two airports, tiered.* Safety and accessibility floors (28 CFR 35.160, ADA §219/§706, 14 CFR 382, 14 CFR 139, OSHA 1910.95) mandatory at both; intelligibility thresholds mandatory at both; PA zoning and announcement governance mandatory in principle, tiered by context; sensory-quiet zones mandatory in presence, scoped by facility; tenant-audio framework mandatory, enforcement context-dependent. IAD's 36% international connecting flow and DCA's slot-controlled domestic O&D justify the tiering, not an exemption. — E-09 cluster, E-06 (`airline-commercial-strategist::ev-df19b606cf60`)
6. *Exercise it first where the friction is lowest.* The DCA Terminal 1 concourse replacement is the smaller, less-politicized project where the adopted standard governs a real procurement first — as a standing requirement, not a pilot. — evidence-map §2.10, E-05 (`airport-ceo::ev-4be11ef6d9d3`)

**Strongest objection and where it enters:** "How do you know the Design Manual doesn't already require this?" The Council did not verify the Manual's current acoustic content (gap §5.a). Enters at beat 2, stated plainly: the recommendation is conditional in form (new volume vs. amendment) but not in substance — either way the content must be verified and specified before C/D and T1 scope.

**Analogy:** None needed. This frame is strongest played straight; a lateral move would dilute the board voice.

**Signature visual:** A two-band timeline, 2025–2033: top band shows the capital program (AUL execution → Concourse E opening fall 2026 → C/D scoping → T1 replacement → AeroTrain extension 2028–2033); bottom band shows the cost-of-change curve rising as each project passes design freeze. A single vertical line marked "standard adopted here" placed at 2026 vs. 2029 makes the whole argument in one glance.

**Airport decision it leads toward:** Board directs the Office of Engineering to draft a tiered quiet-by-design Design Manual amendment (with ADS mapping, intelligibility floors, commissioning verification), verify current Manual content, and apply the standard to DCA Terminal 1 and IAD C/D scope documents.

**Risk of this frame:** Predictability. It is the report the audience expects from a competent consultancy, and this audience is explicitly tired of that genre. It also under-uses the run's most distinctive material — the post-collision reframing and the intelligibility inversion.

## Option 2 — Counterintuitive: "A quiet terminal is the one you can hear"

**Thesis (one sentence):** The January 29 collision at DCA did not make quiet-by-design politically impossible — it made it necessary, because "quiet" done properly is not fewer announcements but engineered intelligibility, and an airport that broadcasts everything everywhere is an airport where the one announcement that matters cannot be heard.

**Communication job:** By the end, a skeptical airport executive should stop reading quiet-by-design as a passenger-comfort amenity and start reading it as a communications-integrity standard with a safety floor, because the same engineering that lowers ambient noise is what raises emergency speech intelligibility above the NFPA 72 floor.

**Opening scene:** Not an airport. A hospital ICU, circa 2013, where monitoring systems were generating hundreds of alarms per bed per day — so many that nurses stopped hearing any of them. The Joint Commission's response was not "fewer alarms for patient comfort." It was a National Patient Safety Goal (NPSG.06.01.01, phased 2014–2016) forcing every hospital to inventory, rank, and govern its alarms — because indiscriminate broadcast is itself the safety failure. Then cut to an IAD concourse at peak bank, where a gate change, a paging call, a TSA advisory, and two boarding announcements overlap in a space never specified for any of them.

**Argument sequence (7 beats):**

1. *The obvious objection, stated first.* After NTSB AIR-26/02, no MWAA board member wants a headline paraphrasable as "airport cuts safety announcements." Correct instinct. This article agrees — and that agreement is the argument. — E-10 (`airport-emergency-management-director::ev-84be36aecf07`, `regulatory-political-analyst::ev-f4fd32e75644`)
2. *The inversion.* Intelligibility, not volume, is the safety variable. NFPA 72 Ch. 24 requires STI ≥0.45 at 90% of measurement locations, average ≥0.50, per Acoustically Distinguishable Space. High ambient noise and reverberant, overlapping PA are exactly what push STI down. The loud terminal is the unintelligible one. — E-02 cluster (`quantitative-analyst::ev-8cb15085981e`, `airport-emergency-management-director::ev-bf6c2224cb0c`)
3. *The "silent airport" peer set is a category error — and that's liberating.* LCY, HEL, CPH, AMS are single-terminal, short-haul, high-English-proficiency operations with no published intelligibility or missed-boarding measurements in any source the Council found. MWAA doesn't need to import their brand; it needs to out-engineer them. — E-07 cluster (`contrarian::ev-f94112da8250`, `technology-scout::ev-436a5e8bb2a2`)
4. *SFO shows the model and the failure mode at once.* Zoned delivery, discontinued cross-terminal final calls, gate-area retention — a real operating model. But the celebrated outcome figures (90 minutes, 40%) have no published methodology anywhere in the record, and the same record contains documented passenger complaints and missed-flight reporting. Announcement reduction without intelligibility engineering and channel redundancy is subtraction, not design. — E-08 (`virtual-christian::ev-9d246c576cda`; contradiction: `operations-analyst::ev-ccf2fd3ef094`, `contrarian::ev-3840eded78c8`)
5. *The people who most need to hear are the test.* 28 CFR 35.160, ADA §219/§706 assistive listening, 14 CFR 382, plus IAD's 10.53M international passengers — many non-English-speaking, connecting, phoneless, or infrequent. A standard that specifies redundant visual, digital, staffed, and hearing-loop channels is what makes reduced broadcast lawful and humane. — E-09 cluster (`regulatory-political-analyst::ev-9c68fd9606da`, `ev-48c339c22ad1`), E-06
6. *Governance is the technology.* ACI-NA's framing of automated announcement platforms — centralized management, audit logs of every broadcast, elimination of manual-initiation errors — turns "announcement policy" into operational integrity with an enforcement trail. That is what a Design Manual can bind and an auditor can check. Exactly the Joint Commission move: inventory, rank, govern. — (`operations-analyst::ev-08af28494c7c`, `virtual-christian::ev-780eab34e4f2`)
7. *So the standard writes itself as a safety document.* Mandatory both airports: ADS maps, STI floors per NFPA 72/ACRP 175, IEC 60268-16 measurement with verification under representative traffic (the standard itself excludes fluctuating noise — commissioning at 06:00 proves nothing), accessibility floors, announcement governance with audit logging. Context-dependent: zoning tiers, sensory-space scope, tenant enforcement mechanics. Delivered through the Design Manual; exercised first on DCA Terminal 1. — E-01, E-02, E-03 (`contrarian::ev-851bd0537852`), E-04

**Strongest objection and where it enters:** "You're rebranding announcement reduction as safety engineering — the passenger-experience motive is doing the real work." Enters at beat 4, answered by beat 5: the standard is falsifiable (STI numbers, ADS maps, measured verification), and its accessibility floors bind harder than current practice, not softer. If MWAA only wanted ambience it would not be volunteering for measurable compliance.

**Analogy:** The Joint Commission clinical-alarm NPSG (used once, as the opening scene — not repeated). It genuinely clarifies: a national governance body forcing rank-and-consolidate discipline on legacy safety broadcasts, without ever framing it as "fewer warnings."

**Signature visual:** "The intelligibility ladder." A single vertical STI scale showing the IEC 60268-16 qualitative bands (Bad <0.30 → Excellent >0.75), with the NFPA 72 emergency floor (0.45/0.50), the ACRP 175 design band (0.46–0.53, target 0.50) marked — and two annotated arrows: rising ambient noise pushes a terminal down the ladder; quiet-by-design measures push it up. One chart converts "quiet" from vibe to compliance.

**Airport decision it leads toward:** The same Design Manual amendment as Option 1, but framed and titled as a communications-integrity and intelligibility standard — which is the version that survives the post-collision political stack and the AUL conversation.

**Risk of this frame:** Overreach. If the safety framing is pushed one notch too hard, the fact-checker and executive reviewer will correctly note that no ledger record ties terminal PA design to the collision's causes (the NTSB findings are airspace and ATC failures). The frame must use the collision only for political altitude, never for causal linkage. Handled carelessly, it reads as trading on a tragedy.

## Option 3 — Operational: "Commissioning day at Concourse E"

**Thesis (one sentence):** This fall MWAA opens Concourse E — the only building on either airport likely to carry modern acoustic and PA design assumptions — and nothing on the record requires anyone to measure whether the building performs as designed, which is the whole quiet-by-design problem in one 435,000-square-foot example.

**Communication job:** By the end, the COO-level reader should be unable to accept "commissioned" as a synonym for "verified," because a standard without an ADS map and a representative-traffic measurement protocol is a specification no one can enforce.

**Opening scene:** Fall 2026, IAD midfield. Concourse E opens: 14 United gates, AeroTrain-connected, >$500M, lounges, new holdrooms. Somewhere in the closeout documents is an acoustic design basis. The operational question nobody is assigned to answer: at 5:30 p.m., when the international bank connects and every holdroom is full, can a passenger at gate E7 distinguish her boarding call from the one at E9? At 6:00 a.m. on commissioning day, with the building empty, the answer will be yes. That answer is worthless — IEC 60268-16, the measurement standard itself, says it does not cover fluctuating noise, and a full terminal is the definition of fluctuating noise.

**Argument sequence (6 beats):**

1. *The moment.* Concourse E is the one MWAA facility where "as-designed" acoustic performance could go on the record at opening — a baseline every future claim about C/D and T1 performance would be measured against. The run's evidence base flags this as an open gap; no requirement compels the measurement. — (`quantitative-analyst` Concourse E facts; evidence-map §5.h)
2. *Why the empty-building test lies.* IEC 60268-16:2020 defines STI/STIPA but explicitly excludes fluctuating noise. Commissioning acceptance at dawn is necessary, not sufficient; verification must happen under representative traffic. Only one agent in fourteen caught this — which is itself evidence of how easily a board is sold an unenforceable number. — E-03 (`contrarian::ev-851bd0537852`)
3. *Before you can measure, you must map.* NFPA 72's intelligibility floor is defined per Acoustically Distinguishable Space. No ADS map, no auditable compliance. An ADS map is a design deliverable — a Design Manual requirement, not a facilities afterthought. — E-02 (`airport-emergency-management-director::ev-bf6c2224cb0c`), evidence-map §2.4
4. *The podium problem.* At preferential-use gates the holdroom, agent podium, and its PA are effectively the airline's operating domain — United across ~50 jetbridge gates and 67% of IAD traffic, American at ~53% of DCA. A standard that cannot reach tenant audio governs half the soundscape. The enforcement path runs through tenant technology standards and the AUL — different negotiations at each airport. — E-06 (`airline-commercial-strategist::ev-bbe86adff5f4`, `ev-df19b606cf60`)
5. *What operations actually needs.* Zoned, automated, audit-logged announcement delivery (ACI-NA model) that behaves one way on a blue-sky day and reverts deliberately during IROPS — DCA's slot-controlled peaks and post-collision route restrictions make irregular-operations messaging load a design input, not an exception. ACRP 153 gives the IROPS communication framework the standard should reference. — (`operations-analyst::ev-08af28494c7c`, `airport-coo::ev-23ab16b34045`), E-10
6. *Scale the mechanism forward, not backward.* The requirement set proven measurable at Concourse E — ADS maps, STI floors, representative-traffic verification, tenant-audio interfaces, IROPS reversion — enters the Design Manual as standing requirements governing DCA Terminal 1 and IAD C/D. Not a retrofit program, not a pilot: a commissioning-and-verification discipline that every future project inherits. — E-04, E-01, E-05

**Strongest objection and where it enters:** "This is a measurement protocol wearing a standard's clothes — where are the planning, architectural, and procurement requirements?" Enters at beat 6, answered by inversion: requirements that can't be verified aren't requirements, and MWAA's Manual already binds design; what it demonstrably lacks (unverified, flagged honestly) is the verification spine that makes acoustic requirements enforceable. Note the run prompt explicitly excludes pilot-framing — this option must hold the "standing requirement, first exercised" line with discipline.

**Analogy:** Aircraft acceptance flights: no airline takes delivery of an aircraft on the strength of its design certificate alone; it flies the airplane. MWAA takes delivery of terminals on paper. (Use only if it earns its space; the frame works without it.)

**Signature visual:** An annotated plan-view of a generic pier concourse divided into color-coded Acoustically Distinguishable Spaces, with measured-STI callouts at two times of day — 06:00 empty (all zones green) and 17:30 peak (adjacent-zone spillover pushing holdrooms below 0.45). The empty/peak pair is the argument.

**Airport decision it leads toward:** Direct a commissioning-condition acoustic baseline at Concourse E (STI/ambient under representative traffic), adopt ADS mapping and verification requirements into the Design Manual and specifications, and carry both into DCA Terminal 1 and IAD C/D scope.

**Risk of this frame:** Scope shrinkage. The vivid mechanism can crowd out the full tiered standard the run demands (acoustical design, quiet zones, accessibility, tenant governance), leaving the article reading as a COO memo about one building. It also leans on gap §5.h — if Concourse E's acoustic design basis turns out to be undocumented, the opening scene loses its hinge and must soften to "should be on the record."
