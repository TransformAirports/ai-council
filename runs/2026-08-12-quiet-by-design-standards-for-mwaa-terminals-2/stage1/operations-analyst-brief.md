# Operations Analyst Brief — Quiet-by-Design Standards for MWAA Terminals

**Author frame:** senior airport operations analyst reading the run as a throughput, intelligibility, and workload problem — not a branding exercise. The question in front of the Council is not whether to be "quieter." It is whether MWAA can specify a measurable operational performance envelope for terminal audio and information before the next generation of IAD and DCA concourses locks in the physics.

## 1. Key findings

1. **The dominant U.S. reference document already exists and it is quantitative, not aspirational.** ACRP Research Report 175 recommends a minimum Speech Transmission Index (STI) of 0.45 for public-address broadcasts in pier-style departure lounges, a signal-to-noise ratio of 10–15 dB(A) over the ambient, and warns that where terminal ceilings exceed roughly 24 ft it becomes very challenging to achieve an STI of 0.50 without deliberate acoustic treatment [Source: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24839/chapter/5]. Every recommendation in this brief threads that document.

2. **The peer program most often cited in board-room slides — SFO's Quiet Airport Program — is a governance change, not a noise-reduction study.** SFO reports a 40% reduction in announcements at aircraft departure-gate seating areas and estimates that more than 90 minutes of announcements per day were eliminated in the International Terminal alone. The reduction is measured in *announcement minutes*, not in dB(A) or STI; the publicly reported "40%" reduction in on-site noise is a program-level assertion, not a peer-reviewed measurement [Source: https://www.airportxnews.com/sfo-reduces-in-terminal-noise-levels/]. Any MWAA standard that borrows SFO's framing must not borrow SFO's imprecision.

3. **"Silent airport" as practiced overseas is a *displacement* of communication load, not a reduction of it.** Schiphol describes itself as a silent airport with "virtually no announcements" and shifts flight information to FIDS and the mobile app [Source: https://news.schiphol.com/schiphol-in-2025-quieter-aircraft-more-satisfied-travellers-and-solid-financial-results/]. London City Airport's Silent Airport Policy explicitly reserves the PA for emergencies [Source: https://www.londoncityairport.com/at-the-airport/need-to-know/airport-policies/silent-policy]. Both approaches only work because the FIDS network, mobile channels, and gate-agent scripts are engineered to carry the traffic the PA used to. That engineering is what MWAA would be specifying — not silence.

4. **The floor beneath any quiet standard is life-safety intelligibility, and it is codified.** NFPA 72 Chapter 24 (Emergency Communications Systems) requires intelligible audio and visible notification for in-building mass notification, including monitoring of speaker integrity, priority messaging hierarchy, and specified warning tones [Source: https://prod-edam.honeywell.com/content/dam/honeywell-edam/hbt/en-us/documents/literature-and-specs/whitepapers/notifier-us/hon-ba-fire-nf-wp-massnotification-m-wp-mn-05-10.pdf]. European practice adds EN 54-16 for voice alarm certification and UL 2572 for mass-notification hardware [Source: https://www.keenfinity-group.com/xc/en/solutions/public-address-solutions/public-address-and-voice-alarm-systems/]. "Quiet" cannot mean "unintelligible in an evacuation."

5. **The passenger-facing complaint that undermines every silent-airport program is missed boardings — and it lands hardest on the populations MWAA is federally required to serve.** SFO's policy has drawn documented passenger backlash from travelers who missed flights or found the reduced announcement environment confusing [Source: https://simpleflying.com/airport-quiet-policy-frustrating-passengers-sfo/]. Documented incidents of non-English-speaking and elderly passengers boarding wrong flights or missing assistance handoffs — including an 84-year-old woman boarded onto a flight to Malta instead of Athens despite prearranged assistance [Source: https://news.yahoo.com/easyjet-flies-elderly-woman-to-wrong-country-160805128.html] — establish that PA-suppression without a hardened redundant channel is an accessibility risk, not a passenger-experience win.

6. **Employee acoustic exposure is a separate operational file that a quiet-by-design standard actually improves.** OSHA 29 CFR 1910.95 requires a hearing conservation program when employee 8-hour TWA exposures reach 85 dB(A), with a 90 dB(A) permissible exposure limit [Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.95]. Terminal gate agents, CBP officers, TSA screeners, and concession workers are not ramp workers, but announcement-saturated environments where the PA competes with itself across zones raise measured LAeq and drive avoidable exposure. The standard's employee-exposure logic is easier to sell than the passenger-experience logic and it protects MWAA against a hearing-conservation liability the current announcement culture is quietly accumulating.

7. **Accessibility redundancy is not optional and it is measurable.** ADA-driven assistive listening — telecoil-compatible induction loops at podia, ticketing, gate positions, and information desks — is deployed in production at Phoenix Sky Harbor (Terminal 4 gates D11–D18) and Seattle-Tacoma [Source: https://www.skyharbor.com/at-the-airport/amenities/special-needs/] [Source: https://visitseattle.org/explore/accessible-travel-guide/accessible-maps-transportation/airport-accessibility/]. Sensory/quiet rooms for neurodivergent travelers are operating at PHX, SEA, and IND. These are commissioning line items with measurable field-strength and STI tests — not soft amenities — and a common MWAA standard should specify their coverage, testing cadence, and enforcement rather than leaving each concourse to invent the requirement.

8. **The MWAA delivery vehicle is already there.** The seven-volume 2020 MWAA Design Manual is described by the Authority as a mandatory guide with the force of law on airport property, updated annually, with airport-specific supplements for IAD and DCA [see airport-context.md §7]. Any quiet-by-design standard should ride that manual as a new volume or amendment with binding Specifications and Estimating & Scheduling implications, not as a stand-alone policy the Board will forget. A standard that lives outside the Design Manual will be argued out of every project by change order.

## 2. Evidence and reasoning

**The acoustic envelope has a defensible number.** ACRP Report 175 is the domestic reference and it converts the argument from qualitative to quantitative. STI is defined by IEC 60268-16:2020 as a 0–1 scale objective rating of speech intelligibility, measurable in the field with STIPA methods [Source: https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/26771]. The Report 175 recommendation of STI ≥ 0.45 in pier-style lounges and SNR of 10–15 dB(A) is the level below which real passengers stop understanding real announcements [Source: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24839/chapter/5]. In field studies referenced in the acoustic-consulting literature, terminal seating areas commonly measure STI in the 0.32–0.42 range — categorized as "poor" — because ceiling heights, hard finishes, and reverberant volumes overwhelm the PA design [Source: https://sonavyx.com/en/standards/iec-60268-16]. Saarinen's IAD Main Terminal — 600 ft × 150 ft with the signature cable-suspended roof [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport_Main_Terminal] — is precisely the geometry Report 175 flags as the hard case. Any IAD standard has to reckon with the fact that the historically protected volume of the Main Terminal cannot be quieted by turning down the PA; it has to be quieted by treating the reverberant envelope, zoning the sources, and moving load off the PA entirely.

**The peer set the run prompt names is uneven in evidentiary quality.** Schiphol's public reporting supports the shift-to-FIDS-and-app model and shows a demonstrable 2 dB(A) reduction in average air-traffic noise between 2018 and 2025 [Source: https://news.schiphol.com/schiphol-in-2025-quieter-aircraft-more-satisfied-travellers-and-solid-financial-results/] — but that is aircraft-side, not terminal-side, and the two should not be conflated in an MWAA brief. London City's Silent Airport Policy is a real document, publicly posted, with a narrow rule: announcements reserved for emergencies [Source: https://www.londoncityairport.com/at-the-airport/need-to-know/airport-policies/silent-policy]. The frequently cited figures for Helsinki, Copenhagen, Barcelona, and Dubai in industry press are drawn from vendor and trade-media summaries rather than airport-published operational data; the Council should treat those as directional, not as citable evidence for MWAA standard-setting.

**Automation is the missing verb.** Where PA volume comes down, gate-triggered announcement automation — messages fired from AODB flight events at the correct zone with a validated multilingual voice library — is doing the work. Industry vendors including AviaVox and Simpleway have deployed such systems across Amsterdam, Frankfurt, and JFK T4; the ACI-NA industry note on data-driven announcement automation frames the value proposition as reducing manual initiation errors and enabling audit trails of every broadcast [Source: https://airportscouncil.org/2026/02/27/the-data-driven-future-of-automated-airport-announcements-smarter-communication-for-smarter-terminals/]. This matters for a quiet-by-design standard because centralizing broadcast control makes "PA zoning" enforceable in software rather than a training instruction to gate agents. Without that automation layer, PA-zoning becomes a rule that erodes the first time an airline runs a late-boarding push.

**MWAA's operating tempo makes this urgent.** IAD Concourse E, with 14 new United gates, opens fall 2026 [see airport-context.md §5]; the $20B+ IAD transformation announced July 29, 2026 replaces Concourses C and D, extends the AeroTrain, and retires the mobile lounges [see airport-context.md §5]; and the DCA Terminal 1 main concourse replacement is in the current use-and-lease capital plan. Every one of those spaces will be scoped, procured, and commissioned inside the window when a standard would apply. Standards adopted late get relitigated as change orders on airline-recovered cost, which is a MII conversation MWAA does not need in a debt-service-coverage window projected to compress from 1.63x in 2024 toward 1.3x over the next five years [see airport-context.md §3]. The financial case for locking specifications up-front is stronger than the passenger-experience case.

**DCA carries additional load the standard has to name.** The January 29, 2025 midair collision at DCA killed 67 people; the February 2026 NTSB final report cited failures across FAA, ATC, and military operations; the FAA responded with helicopter route changes near DCA [see airport-context.md §8]. That regulatory context has raised DCA's routine irregular-operations broadcast load — reroutings, ground-stop notifications, congressional-delegation movements — and elevated the political cost of any real or perceived failure of emergency communication. A standard that reduces routine PA traffic while *strengthening* the intelligibility and reach of IROPS and life-safety messaging is defensible at DCA. A standard that only reduces PA traffic is not.

## 3. Counterexamples — where quiet-by-design is not the answer

- **True legacy reverberant volumes cannot be operationalized out of.** The Saarinen Main Terminal's protected acoustic geometry is the classic case: absent physical treatment (baffles, absorptive banners, ceiling-plane interventions) that the architectural historian and preservation authorities may resist, no PA re-programming will bring STI to 0.45. In those envelopes the honest answer is that PA becomes a life-safety-only channel and every operational message moves to FIDS, mobile, and staffed positions. That is an infrastructure-plus-technology answer, not an operations-only one.

- **Slot-controlled, high-tempo domestic operations resist announcement-suppression.** DCA's 15-minute Shuttle-style banking, its slot-controlled schedule, and the 10 additional beyond-perimeter exemptions authorized under the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 [see airport-context.md §8] mean gate-hold, gate-swap, and departure-sequencing decisions cascade fast. Peer airports that have most successfully suppressed the PA (LCY, Schiphol) run structurally different operations. DCA's requirements should permit denser gate-zone messaging than IAD international concourses, and the standard should say so.

- **Irregular operations are the stress test the routine day hides.** When wide-scale IROPS hits — the 2025 government shutdown, weather ground-stops, the post-January-2025 helicopter-route disruption at DCA — the PA is the only channel guaranteed to reach every passenger simultaneously without opt-in. A standard that treats the PA as vestigial fails on the worst day. This is why the standard's *emergency* and *irregular-operations* provisions have to be over-specified: intelligibility (STI, coverage), reach (zoning that supports mass call vs. targeted), and redundancy (visual, digital, staffed).

- **International-arrivals sterile corridors have federal broadcast requirements.** CBP-controlled sterile areas and federal-inspection stations carry announcement obligations (declarations, wayfinding, agriculture-inspection cues) that are not optional and not fungible with FIDS. An IAD-wide standard should specifically carve those zones out or subject them to a narrower, federally coordinated variant.

- **The "smartphone-first" fallback fails on the population MWAA legally has to serve.** Digital-first only works for passengers who have a phone, a data plan, an app installed, an account provisioned, notifications enabled, English literacy, and vision. That is not the international-connecting passenger at IAD, the family passenger with small children, the elderly O&D passenger at DCA, or the ADA-accommodated passenger anywhere. The redundant-channel requirement is not a "nice to have" section of the standard; it is the section that keeps it defensible.

## 5. What the standard should require, from an operations lens

Not the Council's job to draft the standard here, but the operations shape of it is:

- **Mandatory across both airports:** a floor STI (0.45 in normal ops, higher in emergency messaging zones); a background-noise ceiling per zone type (holdroom, sterile corridor, concessions concourse); reverberation-time bounds tied to volume and finish; PA-zoning that separates gate-local from adjacent-zone from concourse from emergency; automated broadcast tied to AODB with audit logging; life-safety intelligibility per NFPA 72 Ch. 24; assistive-listening coverage (induction loop or equivalent) at every staffed passenger-touch position; multilingual voice library with defined language set; commissioning STI measurement and post-occupancy re-measurement cadence; tenant audio and TV rules with an enforcement mechanism.

- **Varies by airport and terminal:** allowed density of gate-zone messaging (higher at DCA than IAD international concourses); mobile-notification reliance (higher at DCA business O&D, lower at IAD international connecting); quiet-room and sensory-room count per concourse; historic-envelope acoustic treatment scope (IAD Main Terminal has constraints Concourse E does not).

- **Context-dependent:** directional / parametric loudspeaker use at high-density concessions; muted-TV rules by hold-room type; announcement-script length caps by carrier operational model.

## 6. Evidence gaps and honest limitations

- Peer-airport primary documentation for Helsinki, Copenhagen, Barcelona, and Dubai's quiet policies was not verified from airport-published operational data within budget; trade-press summaries exist but should not be treated as citable measurements in a Board-facing final.
- SFO's "40% on-site noise reduction" figure appears only in program communications; the underlying measurement methodology was not located.
- MWAA's 2020 Design Manual is described publicly as mandatory and seven-volume, but whether it already contains binding acoustic, PA-zoning, STI, or LEQ requirements at what tier was not verified from primary MWAA documents in this brief. The Council's Stage 2 synthesis should flag this: if the standard already partly exists, the question narrows to what to *add*, not what to *invent*.
- The specific FAR, CBP, and TSA broadcast-content obligations in FIS and sterile-area environments were not fully mapped in primary regulatory text in this brief and should be pinned before any final document goes to the MWAA Board.

## 7. Bottom line

The operational case for a common quiet-by-design MWAA standard is not that quiet is nice. It is that (a) intelligibility is a measurable performance envelope with a codified floor, (b) the peer airports the run prompt names have shown that PA-suppression only works when the FIDS-plus-mobile-plus-staff redundancy is engineered in, (c) the MWAA capital calendar makes the next thirty-six months the last cheap window to specify that redundancy, and (d) the Design Manual is the vehicle that binds the whole delivery chain from architect to commissioning agent to tenant. Get those four things right and the "quiet airport" label will take care of itself. Get them wrong and MWAA will spend the next decade litigating change orders about ceiling absorption and speaker layouts in concourses that never should have been designed loud in the first place.
