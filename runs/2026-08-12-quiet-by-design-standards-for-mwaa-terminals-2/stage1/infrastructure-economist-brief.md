# Infrastructure Economist Brief — Quiet-by-Design Standards at IAD and DCA

**Run:** `quiet-by-design-standards-for-mwaa-terminals-2`
**Author role:** Infrastructure economist (senior, 20-year horizon on North American, European, and Asian airport capital programs)
**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Rule of construction:** Independent brief. I did not read any other Stage 1 output. All numbers are cited to primary or authoritative secondary sources in the companion evidence file; professional judgment is marked as such and kept in the prose.

---

## 1. What the economist is being asked

The Council is debating whether MWAA should adopt a common quiet-by-design standard for IAD and DCA terminal spaces — acoustics, PA zoning, information systems, and operational controls specified before designs and procurements harden. The thesis is not "eliminate announcements." It is "specify performance before design is locked."

My job on this run is narrow: does the *economics* of MWAA's current capital program support standards-first, or is this a nice-to-have that shows up on a McKinsey deck and dies in Board finance committee? I argue the economics support it, but only if the standard is written to reduce late-stage change orders and commissioning risk, not to add a new gold-plated compliance layer to an already stretched CIP.

## 2. Key findings

1. **MWAA is entering the tightest coverage window of its current capital cycle at the same time the standards conversation is being held.** Moody's projects net revenue debt service coverage falling from **1.63x in 2024 to roughly 1.3x** over the next five years as approximately **$5.5 billion of new debt** is issued between 2025 and 2028. Adjusted debt per O&D enplanement is projected to **peak near $400 in 2028** [Source: https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/mwaa-goes-to-market]. That is the financial envelope any standards discussion has to live inside.
2. **The US airport capital pipeline is at the largest scale ever measured.** ACI-NA's 2025 Infrastructure Needs Study identifies **$173.9 billion of five-year infrastructure needs**, up **15.1%** from the 2023 study, with **$20.6 billion per year** attributed to large-hub airports [Source: https://airportscouncil.org/press_release/new-aci-na-reports-highlight-economic-impact-and-growing-infrastructure-needs-of-u-s-airports/]. Announced large-hub capital programs total **$130–140 billion through 2030** [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/large-hub-airport-capital-programs-2026]. Standards-driven waste is not rounding error at this scale.
3. **Late design changes are the single largest destroyer of value in airport delivery.** The Construction Industry Institute puts direct rework at approximately **5% of total project cost** on average, with a **2–20% range** by project type; design-related errors account for **1–9% of total project cost in rework alone**, and up to **70%** of construction rework traces to engineering and design errors [Source: https://reworkcost.com/cost-of-rework-in-construction]. The rule of thumb every airport program manager quotes — an error costs 1x on paper, 10x in procurement, 100x once built — is not just a saying; it is the underlying grammar of change orders on the IAD program.
4. **Acoustic and PA scope is exactly the kind of scope that gets value-engineered out in bid, then bought back in commissioning.** Airport PA and voice-alarm systems in transport hubs are typically specified to STIPA ≥ 0.50 to comply with EN 54-16, BS 5839-8, and ISO 7240-19 [Source: https://acousplan.com/blog/iec-60268-16-sti-guide]. STI/STIPA performance is set by loudspeaker density, zone geometry, room absorption, and ambient-noise-driven amplifier headroom — all of which are decided in the architectural and MEP coordination phase. Fixing STI post-commissioning means adding speakers, cutting ceiling, and re-tuning DSP: 100x territory in the 1-10-100 heuristic.
5. **The silent-airport literature is real, but it is a policy and operations story with an infrastructure enabler — not a hardware buy.** SFO's Quiet Airport program reportedly removed **over 90 minutes of daily announcement time** and reduced individual paging events from **492 to 261** (a **47% reduction in occurrences and a 60% cut in cumulative duration**, from 145 to 58 minutes) [Source: https://viewfromthewing.com/san-francisco-airport-removed-90-minutes-of-daily-noise-travelers-say-it-changed-everything/]. London City has run a **silent terminal policy since 2008**, expanded in 2016 [Source: https://www.londoncityairport.com/at-the-airport/need-to-know/airport-policies/silent-policy]. Schiphol has moved important flight information almost exclusively to FIDS and the app [Source: https://www.schiphol.nl/en/assistance/flying-for-the-deaf-and-blind/]. The pattern is not "buy a quiet system;" it is "constrain the announcement source and let the room and PA design already deliver intelligibility where announcements do run."
6. **The bad history of airport programs is a history of specifying too late and re-specifying too often.** Berlin Brandenburg ran from a **€2.83 billion budget to €6.5–7.3 billion** and opened nine years late, with over **100,000 construction defects** documented by 2012 and repeated failures of fire and life-safety systems that are cousins of PA/voice-alarm scope [Source: https://execution-atlas.com/en/articles/berlin-brandenburg-airport/]. DIA's baggage system ran from **$193 million to more than $560 million** with a 16-month schedule slip and was ultimately largely abandoned [Source: https://strategicppm.wordpress.com/2010/06/01/the-failure-of-denver-international-airports-automated-baggage-system/]. Heathrow T5's £4.2 billion terminal delivered on paper but opened to a baggage failure that stranded **42,000 bags and cancelled more than 500 flights in ten days** [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Terminal_5]. Each is a commissioning-and-integration story, not a groundworks story. Quiet-by-design lives on the same seam.
7. **IAD has its own version of this story.** The AeroTrain opened in January 2010 at **~$1.5 billion** — but was built without a station at Concourse D, which is why every Concourse D passenger has ridden a mobile lounge for the 16 years since. The current transformation now budgets **~$3.75 billion for AeroTrain extension and the underground connector**, inside a program envelope of **$20 billion+** and specifically ~**$6.99 billion at IAD** in the underlying 2025 airline use-and-lease commitment [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement]. The historical lesson is that the standard you skip in the original design is the one you re-bid twenty years later at a multiple.
8. **The opportunity cost of a badly specified terminal is a real number and it is comparable to the standards conversation.** MWAA's CPE is currently reported at roughly **$12.77** — cheap by peer standards (ORD ~$28.43 FY2024, projected to $40 by 2035; JFK over $36) [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/airport-finance/large-hub/cpe]. But that CPE is about to rise sharply as the new debt phases in. Every dollar spent buying back a poorly specified audio, FIDS, or acoustic package in year-of-opening is a dollar not spent on the airline recovery buffer that keeps CPE competitive against BWI and, for United's connecting traffic, against alternative hubs.

## 3. Evidence and analysis

### 3.1 MWAA's financial envelope

MWAA holds **Fitch AA−, Moody's Aa3, S&P AA−, all stable outlook as of May 2025** [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/financial-statistics/investor-relations/credit-ratings]. Consolidated system debt service coverage was reported at **2.87x in FY2025** on the master indenture basis and is well above the 1.25x minimum rate covenant [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/dca-slot-expansion-obbba]. But headline coverage is not the constraint. Moody's compressed *net revenue* debt service coverage forecast — **from 1.63x in 2024 to ~1.3x over the next five years** as the ~$5.5 billion of new debt phases in through 2028, with adjusted debt/O&D peaking near **$400** — is the real signal [Source: https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/mwaa-goes-to-market].

Two implications for the standards question:

- Any standard that materially raises design or commissioning cost per gate needs an explicit financial defense to the Board. "Quiet is nice" will not survive finance-committee questioning at 1.3x coverage.
- Any standard that materially reduces the probability or magnitude of late change orders on the ~$20B+ IAD transformation is, at the same coverage level, self-financing. The standard has to be written for the second story, not the first.

### 3.2 US airport capital context

ACI-NA's 2025 Infrastructure Needs Study puts five-year infrastructure needs at **$173.9 billion**, up **15.1% from 2023**, with large hubs at **$20.6 billion/year** and medium hubs at **$6 billion/year** [Source: https://airportscouncil.org/press_release/new-aci-na-reports-highlight-economic-impact-and-growing-infrastructure-needs-of-u-s-airports/]. Announced large-hub programs total **$130–140 billion through 2030**, with roughly **25 of 31 large-hub airports issuing at least one new airport revenue bond series in 2024–2025** [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/large-hub-airport-capital-programs-2026]. MWAA is a representative case, not an outlier, of an industry that has front-loaded a very large amount of terminal-scope capital into a five-year window.

### 3.3 The economics of late design change

This is the part of the argument that most non-engineers underweight. The Construction Industry Institute's long-running benchmarking puts **direct rework at ~5% of total project cost on average**, with **2–20% range** by project type, and **design-related errors at 1–9% of total project cost in rework alone**; up to **70% of construction rework traces to engineering and design errors** [Source: https://reworkcost.com/cost-of-rework-in-construction]. The **1-10-100** cost-of-change principle — a design change costs 1x on paper, 10x in procurement, 100x post-construction — is a stylized rule but it is directionally consistent with the CII data and with every published airport post-mortem [Source: https://assetlifecycle.trimble.com/blog/en-US/article/what-are-change-orders-in-construction].

In practical terms for MWAA: if a common quiet-by-design standard reduces field rework on audio, ceiling, and finish scope by even one percentage point on the ~$6.99 billion IAD envelope in the 2025 use-and-lease, that is on the order of **$70 million avoided**. That is more than the total plausible incremental design cost of writing and enforcing the standard across the program. This calculation is a professional-judgment framing, not a source-quoted number, and belongs in the brief.

### 3.4 The specific technical scope this standard would govern

The ACRP baseline for U.S. terminals is **ACRP Research Report 175: Improving Intelligibility of Airport Terminal Public Address Systems** (2017), which provides design guidance for PA intelligibility across passenger-processing environments and includes data on PA systems, terminal finishes, and background noise levels across participating airports [Source: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24839/chapter/9]. The report is prescriptive enough to be the reference base for MWAA's Design Manual update.

Speech intelligibility is measurable and auditable. **IEC 60268-16:2020** defines STI/STIPA measurement, and **STIPA ≥ 0.50** is the threshold typically required for voice alarm systems to comply with EN 54-16, BS 5839-8, and ISO 7240-19 [Source: https://acousplan.com/blog/iec-60268-16-sti-guide]. That is important because it means "quiet-by-design" can be written in the Design Manual with the same specificity as, say, an FAA runway slope tolerance — as a numerical performance requirement — rather than as a wellness aspiration.

### 3.5 What the peer set actually did

- **London City Airport** ran a silent terminal policy from 2008 and, from August 18, 2016, stopped flight and gate announcements entirely; only essential and emergency announcements remain [Source: https://www.londoncityairport.com/at-the-airport/need-to-know/airport-policies/silent-policy].
- **Helsinki-Vantaa** has been described as a silent airport since 2015, pushing information to passenger devices, retaining gate-area and emergency announcements [Source: https://morepremium.com/helsinki-vantaa-to-be-made-a-quiet-airport/].
- **Schiphol** shifts important information almost exclusively to FIDS and the app and describes itself as a "Silent Airport" [Source: https://www.schiphol.nl/en/assistance/flying-for-the-deaf-and-blind/].
- **SFO's Quiet Airport program** (launched 2018) reportedly cut individual paging occurrences from **492 to 261 daily**, cumulative announcement duration from **145 minutes to 58 minutes**, and reduced overall onsite noise by more than **40%** [Source: https://viewfromthewing.com/san-francisco-airport-removed-90-minutes-of-daily-noise-travelers-say-it-changed-everything/].

Three of these four are governance and operations interventions layered on existing physical plant. That is the honest reading. The infrastructure question — the one MWAA has a chance to get right on Concourse E follow-ons, Concourse C/D replacement, and DCA Terminal 1 — is whether the room, the PA zones, and the FIDS layout make quiet operations *possible and inexpensive* to run, rather than requiring a permanent operational subsidy to maintain.

### 3.6 The bad-history evidence base

- **Berlin Brandenburg (BER):** ~€2.83B initial budget → €6.5–7.3B final (2.3–2.6x), nine years late, opened October 2020, over 100,000 construction defects catalogued by 2012, fire-and-safety and cabling failures at the core of the delay [Source: https://execution-atlas.com/en/articles/berlin-brandenburg-airport/].
- **Denver International Airport baggage system:** ~$193M contract → $560M final cost, 16-month schedule slip, ultimately largely abandoned [Source: https://strategicppm.wordpress.com/2010/06/01/the-failure-of-denver-international-airports-automated-baggage-system/].
- **Heathrow T5:** delivered on budget at ~£4.2B on the terminal, but the opening failed on baggage integration: 42,000 bags stranded, 500+ flights cancelled in ten days [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Terminal_5].
- **JFK New Terminal One:** ~$9.5B terminal within Port Authority's ~$19B JFK transformation, debt load exceeding $6B, opening delayed to at least November 2026 [Source: https://simpleflying.com/6-billion-debt-clock-ticking-new-york-jfk-delayed-new-terminal-1/].
- **IAD AeroTrain:** ~$1.5B when it opened January 2010 (Mitsubishi Crystal Movers, 3.78-mile alignment), built without a Concourse D station — the reason mobile lounges persisted for 16 years and now require a **~$3.75B AeroTrain extension** within the current transformation [Source: https://www.airwaysmag.com/new-post/dulles-20-billion-overhaul-mobile-lounges].

Note the shape of the pattern. In none of these cases was the killer scope the primary structure or the airfield. It was integration scope: baggage, fire-life-safety, cabling, station-to-terminal geometry. Public address, voice alarm, acoustics, and FIDS live in exactly the same seam. That is the operational-economics reason to write the standard now.

### 3.7 Opportunity cost — what else the money could fund

The framing question a Board finance committee will ask is: "if we tighten this standard and it costs incremental design and commissioning, what are we not doing?" A useful reference frame:

- The **10 additional beyond-perimeter DCA slot exemptions** authorized under the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/dca-slot-expansion-obbba] represent tens of millions of dollars a year of incremental non-airline revenue to MWAA at margin. The standards conversation cannot reasonably cost more than the *variance* on one year of that revenue stream if it is going to survive scrutiny.
- On the airline side, MWAA CPE at ~$12.77 is cheap relative to ORD ($28.43 FY2024, projected $40 by 2035) or JFK ($36+) [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/airport-finance/large-hub/cpe]. IAD's competitive position for United connecting traffic against Newark, and DCA's competitive position against BWI, sits partly on that spread. Every avoidable dollar of late-change cost is a dollar the airlines will otherwise see in the recovery formula.

The opportunity-cost argument is not "quiet or slots." It is "an integration standard that avoids a few points of rework is worth many multiples of its cost in preserved CPE competitiveness." That framing is professional judgment.

## 4. Caveats and limitations

- **Primary MWAA financial documents were not read cover-to-cover.** The 2025 numbers cited above are drawn from Bond Buyer reporting of MWAA's May 2025 investor materials and from published summaries of Fitch and Moody's May 2025 rating reports. Where the Fitch/Moody's report itself is quoted, I noted the source. Before publication, the Council should reconcile the specific 1.3x, $400/O&D, and $5.5B new-debt figures against MWAA's 2025 ACFR and the underlying rating agency PDFs.
- **The DIA baggage system numbers are contested in the literature** ($193M contract, $400M–$560M final, 16-month slip). I cited the range that appears in multiple secondary sources but a primary-source verification (state auditor reports, City of Denver post-mortems, GAO/RAND analyses) would strengthen the point.
- **The SFO Quiet Airport reduction figures (90 minutes/day, 40% noise reduction, 47%/60% paging reductions) come from airport-authored materials repeated in trade press.** The measurement methodology behind the reported percentages is not verified in a primary published document I could reach in this pass; the airport-context packet flags the same limitation. Treat these as directionally correct claims, not as auditable outcomes.
- **International peer numbers (LCY, HEL, CPH, AMS)** are drawn from airport websites and trade press. Primary Board-adopted policy documents would need to be pulled before a final report quotes them as design precedent.
- **Cost of a "quiet-by-design" package on a per-gate or per-square-foot basis was not obtainable at this budget.** I looked; the numbers exist in bid documents but are not in the open literature. A single-airport case study (SFO, MSP, or one of the current JFK T1 subs) is the fastest path to a defensible unit-cost range.
- **The "1-10-100" rule is a heuristic, not a peer-reviewed finding.** It is directionally consistent with CII rework benchmarks. The brief uses it as a framing device, not as a calculated result.
- **"Standards-first saves X% of rework" is a professional-judgment claim, not a sourced one.** I flagged it as such in §3.3. Any dollar estimate in the final article should be labeled as an illustrative model, not as measured MWAA data.

## 6. Bottom line for Stage 2 synthesis

The infrastructure economics say **write the standard now, on the Design Manual chassis MWAA already runs, and write it as measurable performance (STI/STIPA, LAeq, PA zone geometry, FIDS density) rather than as aspiration.** The economic case does not depend on quiet being fashionable in Europe. It depends on the fact that MWAA is about to spend a very large amount of terminal-integration money in the tightest debt-coverage window of its cycle, and that history — BER, DIA, T5, IAD AeroTrain's missing Concourse D station — says the scope most likely to fail is precisely the scope this standard governs. The Board sell is "avoided change orders on a $20B+ program," not "quiet is nice." A finance committee will buy the first sentence. It will not buy the second.
