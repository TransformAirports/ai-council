# Airline Commercial Strategist Brief

**Run:** `quiet-by-design-standards-for-mwaa-terminals-2`
**Lens:** Carrier response is the veto. Any standard MWAA writes has to survive United's operating model at IAD and American's slot-fortress model at DCA. If the standard raises the airline recovery bill, touches gate podium autonomy, or increases irregular-operations messaging risk, the carriers will fight it — and the Use and Lease structure gives them the tools to win.

---

## 1. Key findings

1. **Two airports, two carrier concentrations, one contract.** United carried roughly **19.7 million of IAD's 29.0 million 2025 passengers — 67% of the airport's traffic** and operates ~50 jetbridge gates in Concourses C and D. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/united-washington-dulles-hub/] American Airlines held roughly **53% of DCA slots/market by end of 2024** and moves about **14 million DCA passengers a year**. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/american-airlines-washington-dc-routes-analysis-march-2025/] A "common MWAA standard" is in practice negotiated with two counterparties, not "the airlines."
2. **MWAA is entering a compression window.** New debt of ~$5.5B is planned 2025–2028, coverage tightens from **1.63x (2024) toward ~1.3x**, and the AUL commits to a **140% airline-funded debt service coverage target**. [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement] IAD signatory CPE was **$12.88 in FY24 and $9.56 in FY25**; DCA was **$9.05 then $7.49**. [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/airports/iad] [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/dca-slot-expansion-obbba] The system is affordable today. The runway for adding cost is finite.
3. **An airline briefing put the fully-loaded IAD transformation CPE at $90.64 per enplanement**, vs $12.88 today — the number that made Scott Kirby "look disturbed" on stage at the July 2026 announcement, per Cranky Flier. [Source: https://crankyflier.com/2026/08/04/the-plan-for-dulles-is-ambitious-and-ridiculous-and-it-wont-happen/] Any incremental standard that adds recoverable cost enters an arena where the anchor tenant is already publicly resistant to the base program.
4. **The AUL gives airlines real leverage over capital.** MWAA's own release describes the 15-year agreement as recovering all major operating costs with the **140% airline-funded coverage target** and requiring **signature by airlines representing a majority of flights** to take effect. [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement] MII language is universal in hybrid U.S. agreements: it gives "airlines that account for the majority of the traffic at an airport the right to review and accept or veto any capital projects that could lead to significant increases in the rates that they pay." [Source: https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/majority-in-interest-of-airlines-mii] The specific MWAA threshold is not confirmed in the public release — an evidence gap.
5. **Gate-podium PA is often airline territory, not airport territory.** At preferential-use gates the holdroom is the leased premises, and airlines "can create and manage their own automated pre-boarding and boarding sequences through the PA systems." [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/airport-gate-management] [Source: https://www.sita.aero/solutions/sita-at-airports/sita-operations-at-airports/sita-passenger-information/sita-public-address-system/] A "quiet-by-design" standard that regulates gate-podium announcement content, cadence, or scripting is not a design standard — it is a lease amendment.
6. **The domestic reference for measurable PA design already exists.** ACRP Research Report 175 sets **minimum signal-to-noise ratio of 10 dB(A) and minimum Speech Transmission Index (STI) of 0.45** for pier-style departure lounges. [Source: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24839/chapter/12] The right MWAA move is to bind the *acoustic performance*, which airlines rarely fight, and leave *announcement content* to the tenants who own the operational risk.
7. **DCA's operating tempo just got noisier, not quieter.** The January 29, 2025 midair collision, the subsequent FAA restrictions on Helicopter Route 4 and runways 15/33 and 4/22, and 10 additional daily beyond-perimeter slot exemptions awarded December 2024 all increase the irregular-operations messaging load American must carry to its customers. [Source: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12504] [Source: https://aviationa2z.com/index.php/2024/10/17/five-us-airlines-awarded-new-washington-reagan-slots/] A silent-terminal posture at DCA lands on a hub that is being asked to talk more, not less.
8. **Peer "silent" airports differ from IAD/DCA in ways carriers care about.** London City has been silent-terminal since 2008 but is a single-carrier-heavy short-haul O&D operation with **no complex hubbing to communicate around**. [Source: https://www.londoncityairport.com/at-the-airport/need-to-know/airport-policies/silent-policy] SFO's Quiet Airport removed 90+ minutes of daily announcement time and reported a 40% onsite noise reduction — but kept boarding and departure PA **inside the immediate gate area** and discontinued only "final call" airport-wide announcements. [Source: https://viewfromthewing.com/san-francisco-airport-removed-90-minutes-of-daily-noise-travelers-say-it-changed-everything/] The model that survived at SFO was one that did not touch United's gate operations at United's largest hub.

## 2. Evidence — the commercial frame

### 2.1 The 2025 Airline Use and Lease Agreement is the structural fact

MWAA's own release announcing the January 1, 2025 agreement describes a 15-year hybrid arrangement that "continue[s] the ability to recover all major costs associated with operating the airports," carries an airline-funded debt-service coverage target of 140 percent, and had to be signed by carriers representing a majority of the airports' flights to take effect. [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement] The $9 billion capital program cited at execution was split roughly $6.99B IAD / $2.39B DCA. [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/metropolitan-washington-airports-authority-completes-negotiations-new-use-and-lease-agreement]

That single sentence — "recover all major costs" — is the load-bearing airline-commercial fact for this run. Under a hybrid cost-recovery model, incremental standards cost is not absorbed by MWAA's operating surplus; it is passed through to the rate base. A quiet-by-design specification that adds $X million to commissioning and $Y hundred thousand to annual operating cost enters the rate base and shows up in every carrier's monthly airline invoice. If the standard cannot be defended as reducing another airline-visible cost, or as protecting an airline-visible revenue stream, the carriers have both the incentive and the mechanism to resist.

That mechanism is Majority-in-Interest. The generic legal definition — "airlines that account for the majority of the traffic … [have] the right to review and accept or veto any capital projects that could lead to significant increases in the rates" — describes exactly the situation MWAA is walking into. [Source: https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/majority-in-interest-of-airlines-mii] The MWAA-specific MII threshold, exempt categories (safety, security, grant-funded), and cost-triggering thresholds are not in the public release and were not verified for this brief. That is a genuine evidence gap: any Council recommendation that assumes MII will not bite has to be sourced to the executed agreement.

### 2.2 IAD: one carrier, one plan, one veto

United's IAD position is not a "dominant carrier" position in the ordinary sense. It is 67% of the passengers, 50 jetbridge gates, the international gateway anchor, and the co-brand on the $20 billion transformation announced July 29, 2026 in a joint federal-airport-airline event. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/united-washington-dulles-hub/] [Source: https://www.futuretravelexperience.com/2026/08/mwaa-and-united-airlines-unveil-20-billion-transformation-of-washington-dulles-international-airport/] The 14-gate Concourse E opens fall 2026 with a 40,000 sq ft United Club and 46,000 sq ft of concessions — with cost split between MWAA, United, and federal grants. [Source: https://www.internationalairportreview.com/news/232900/united-and-mwaa-celebrate-500-million-concourse-e-expansion-to-enhance-passenger-experience-at-dulles/]

That combination — anchor tenant, capital co-investor, brand co-owner — makes United not one MII vote among many but the counterparty. Any quiet-by-design standard applied to future IAD terminal work is de facto a bilateral negotiation with United. The professional read: United will accept acoustic performance requirements (they reduce customer complaints, they align with United's own product investments in the Polaris and United Club programs), will negotiate hard on tenant-system standards that reach into its clubs and gate podiums, and will veto anything that pushes the transformation CPE higher when the airline briefing pegged the fully-loaded number at $90.64 per enplanement — a 7x escalation from FY24's $12.88 that United will not underwrite quietly. [Source: https://crankyflier.com/2026/08/04/the-plan-for-dulles-is-ambitious-and-ridiculous-and-it-wont-happen/]

The IAD international share (36% of traffic; 10.53M international passengers in 2025) [Source: https://simpleflying.com/united-washington-dulles-hub/] is a second commercial variable. Non-English-speaking connecting passengers do not use the SFO Quiet Airport analogy well — they use apps less, they miss visual cues in unfamiliar terminals, and every missed connection at an international hub is a passenger United reaccommodates, a hotel bill United pays, and a rebooking cost United absorbs. United's willingness to endorse a "reduced-announcement" posture at IAD is meaningfully lower than at a domestic-heavy hub. That is not sentiment; it is the operating cost model.

### 2.3 DCA: fortress hub, slot fortress, messaging fortress

American is the anchor at DCA — 53% of slots, 14M annual passengers, the DCA hub inherited from the 2013 US Airways merger. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/american-airlines-washington-dc-routes-analysis-march-2025/] DCA is described by observers as "one of [American's] most profitable" hubs despite being the smallest of the ten. That profitability rides on slot-controlled scarcity, business-traveler O&D, and a customer base that punishes missed connections harder than a leisure market would. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/american-airlines-washington-dc-routes-analysis-march-2025/]

Three post-2024 events raise DCA's messaging load rather than lower it:

- **January 29, 2025 midair collision** — 67 fatalities; NTSB found multiple FAA/ATC/military failures. [Source: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12504]
- **FAA airspace restrictions** (March 14, 2025 chart update removing Helicopter Route 4 between Hains Point and the Woodrow Wilson Bridge; runway 15/33 and 4/22 restrictions during helicopter operations). [Source: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12504]
- **10 new daily beyond-perimeter slot exemptions** (SEA, SAN, SFO, SAT, LAS) awarded December 2024 with service starting February–March 2025 — long-haul flights that raise irregular-operations exposure and increase the count of long-haul rebook events. [Source: https://aviationa2z.com/index.php/2024/10/17/five-us-airlines-awarded-new-washington-reagan-slots/]

The professional read: at DCA the carrier operating model is telling MWAA it needs *more* redundant communication capacity, not less. A "quiet-by-design" standard that reads to American as constraining PA availability during IROPs will be resisted through the airline lease and — if that fails — through the airline's own product design, which will simply route around any airport-side quiet mandate via app push and gate-agent behavior. That is not compliance; that is workaround.

### 2.4 What carriers actually do with PA — and why airport rules rarely reach the gate

At preferential-use gates the holdroom (including the agent podium and its PA infrastructure) is the airline's leased premises. Airlines "can create and manage their own automated pre-boarding and boarding sequences through the PA systems," and vendors like SITA and Simpleway explicitly build gate-controlled PA around airline scripts. [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/airport-gate-management] [Source: https://www.sita.aero/solutions/sita-at-airports/sita-operations-at-airports/sita-passenger-information/sita-public-address-system/]

That means an MWAA quiet-by-design standard that regulates *whether* boarding announcements occur, *what they say*, or *when the airline may override the airport script* is not a design specification — it is a change to the airline lease. It will be renegotiated on that footing, and the specific carriers with veto power (United at IAD; American at DCA) already have every reason to preserve autonomy over gate-agent workflow.

The workable envelope is narrower: MWAA can bind the acoustic performance of the airport-provided PA zones (ambient dB, reverberation time, STI, adjacent-zone spillover), the FIDS and digital-communication baseline, quiet-space geometry, and the tenant *systems standard* that airline-installed PA must meet at the gate. ACRP 175 provides the domestic reference floor: SNR 10 dB(A), STI 0.45 in pier-style lounges, with additional cautions against text-to-speech in reverberant or non-native-language-heavy spaces. [Source: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24839/chapter/12] Standards written to this envelope are largely uncontested by carriers because they lower the airline's own complaint volume without touching the airline's operating autonomy.

### 2.5 Peer signal, honestly read

The SFO Quiet Airport program is the case most often cited domestically. The program removed 90+ minutes of daily airport-wide announcement time and reported a >40% onsite noise reduction. [Source: https://viewfromthewing.com/san-francisco-airport-removed-90-minutes-of-daily-noise-travelers-say-it-changed-everything/] The mechanism that made it politically survivable at United's largest West Coast hub is that boarding and departure announcements were *kept* at the gate — the program discontinued airport-wide "final call" announcements and pushed music/media reduction. It did not tell United when to close a boarding door. That is a design choice a hub carrier will accept. It is also the ceiling of what airport-side quiet policy has demonstrably shipped at a large U.S. hub.

London City has been silent-terminal since 2008 [Source: https://www.londoncityairport.com/at-the-airport/need-to-know/airport-policies/silent-policy] but is not a hub, not slot-controlled by U.S. standards, not international-transfer-heavy, and not encumbered by a hybrid AUL. Its precedent value for MWAA is limited to demonstrating that visual/digital primacy is *possible* — not that it is transferable.

## 3. Case studies of carrier response to airport-side decisions

**Case: SFO Quiet Airport (2019–present).** SFO wrote its policy in a way that United could accept without operational cost — gate-area PA retained, "final call" airport-wide announcements removed, music and background media reduced. [Source: https://viewfromthewing.com/san-francisco-airport-removed-90-minutes-of-daily-noise-travelers-say-it-changed-everything/] Public United statements opposing the policy do not exist because the policy did not require United to change what United does. *Read-across for MWAA:* the survivable envelope is airport-controlled zones and airport-controlled scripts. The moment the standard crosses into gate podium behavior it becomes a use-agreement fight.

**Case: The IAD $20B transformation announcement (July 2026).** Public co-branding masked a private dispute. The airline briefing referenced by Cranky Flier put the fully-loaded transformation CPE at $90.64 — a ~7x escalation from FY24's $12.88. [Source: https://crankyflier.com/2026/08/04/the-plan-for-dulles-is-ambitious-and-ridiculous-and-it-wont-happen/] United's implied strategy per Snyder: "I'm sure he's using this as a way to get some of the work done that he thinks is most important, and then he figures when a new president is in place, the rest can be shelved." *Read-across:* the anchor tenant will strategically endorse a program in public while retaining the tools to phase-shift or veto components. A quiet-by-design standard added mid-program without carrier alignment is a change-order fight the airport will lose.

**Case: DCA post-crash operating environment.** The FAA's March 14, 2025 removal of a Helicopter Route 4 segment and its runway restrictions during helicopter operations near DCA [Source: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12504] force American to run more schedule disruption at its most profitable-per-passenger hub. The commercial response has been to double-down on messaging density — app push, gate-agent scripts, more IROP PA use — not less. A "quieter DCA" landing on this operating environment risks reading to American as constraining its recovery toolkit at exactly the moment the airline needs more of it.

## 5. Evidence gaps and honest limits

- The **specific MII threshold, cost-triggering thresholds, and exempt project categories** under the 2025 MWAA AUL were not verified from the executed agreement for this brief. Any Council claim about airline consent mechanics has to be sourced to the agreement or to MWAA's bond disclosures on EMMA, not to the press release.
- The **current airline share of IAD and DCA rate base** (as opposed to passenger share) was not disaggregated in the public financials pulled here. United's leverage is likely higher on rate base than on passengers.
- The **degree to which the existing MWAA Design Manual already contains binding acoustic, STI, or PA-zoning language** was not verified in primary sources for this run. If it does, "quiet-by-design" is a version increment, not a new document — a materially easier board and MII conversation.
- **Peer-airport primary documentation** (SFO airport commission minutes on the Quiet Airport program, LCY board records) is not in the evidence base and would need direct sourcing before being cited as precedent in the article.

## 6. The Council-facing veto question

For any recommendation the Council writes, apply this test: **what will United do at IAD, and what will American do at DCA, when they see this line in the design manual?** If the answer touches gate-podium autonomy, adds recoverable cost without carrier-visible benefit, or constrains IROP messaging capacity, the standard will be renegotiated, workaround-ed, or vetoed. If the answer is "they will accept it because it lowers their complaint rate and does not touch the gate," the standard will ship. That is the airline-commercial gate the quiet-by-design standard has to pass.
