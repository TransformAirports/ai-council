# Regulatory and Political Brief — Quiet-by-Design at IAD and DCA

**Analyst:** Regulatory & Political
**Run:** `quiet-by-design-standards-for-mwaa-terminals-2`
**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Purpose:** Tell the Strategist what a quiet-by-design terminal standard at MWAA can and cannot bind, on what realistic timeframe, and which apparent choices are actually foreclosed by federal code, civil-rights law, historic preservation, or the airline agreement.

---

## Key findings

1. **NFPA 72 sets an intelligibility floor no MWAA policy can undercut.** Where emergency voice communication is required and quantitative measurement is performed, at least 90 percent of measurement locations must achieve STI ≥ 0.45 with an average STI ≥ 0.50 (CIS ≥ 0.70) in each acoustically distinguishable space. "Quiet-by-design" is a specification for the *routine* announcement floor. It is not a license to lower the *emergency* floor. Any Council recommendation that reads "silent" gets bounced by the fire marshal at commissioning.

2. **MWAA is a public entity under ADA Title II. 28 CFR § 35.160 obligates it to furnish auxiliary aids and services, and to give "primary consideration" to the disabled traveler's requested communication method.** Smartphone-first is a design *convenience*, not a legal *substitute*. Any standard that assumes an English-reading, app-equipped traveler as the design baseline is a Title II complaint waiting to be filed.

3. **14 CFR Part 382 (Air Carrier Access Act) extends the same duty to airline processes on airport premises** and requires accessible kiosks (until 25% of installed post-2016 machines at each airport are accessible), accessible primary websites, and equivalent trip-information access at gate, ticket, and customer-service positions. The airline gate podium is not out of scope.

4. **The Passenger Facility Charge cap has been $4.50 per boarded passenger since 2001; the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 (P.L. 118-63, May 16, 2024) left it unchanged.** MWAA's discretionary passenger-funded envelope for terminal-interior work — including acoustic finishes, PA rework, and FIDS — is fixed until Congress acts. Four industry attempts to raise the cap since 2017 have failed. Assume this does not move on the standards-adoption timeline.

5. **The Dulles Main Terminal has been determined eligible for the National Register of Historic Places since March 1978.** Every federally-funded or federally-permitted undertaking that could affect the Saarinen terminal or the surrounding Historic District triggers Section 106 consultation under 54 U.S.C. § 306108. The Silver Line's Refined LPA drew an "Adverse Effect" finding for landscape displacement at Saarinen Circle. Acoustic retrofit inside the Main Terminal — ceiling treatments, PA-loudspeaker placement, glazing changes — will consult with the Virginia SHPO before it hangs. Budget 6–12 months on the critical path.

6. **AIP dollars are not available for the exclusive-use terminal interior where most of this standard will live.** FAA AIP Handbook Order 5100.38D excludes exclusive-use, revenue-producing, and most terminal-interior improvements. Acoustic finishes in gate holdrooms and concourse retail are funded from PFCs or airline-recoverable capital — which routes them through the 2025 Use and Lease Agreement, not through federal grants.

7. **The 2025 Airline Use and Lease Agreement is the real gate.** MWAA has a new 15-year agreement effective January 1, 2025 with an approximately $9B initial capital program (subsequently expanded). Amendments to the Design Manual that raise airline-recoverable cost trigger majority-in-interest (MII) review. A quiet-by-design standard that adds specification cost without airline economic buy-in gets stalled in the airline consultation, not in the boardroom.

8. **Federal actors have persistent standing at MWAA that peer authorities do not have.** The airports sit under a long-term DOT lease. Three of 17 Board seats are Presidential appointments. The July 29, 2026 IAD $20B+ transformation was announced jointly by the White House, DOT, United, and MWAA. Design decisions that would be routine at DFW or ATL are now, at IAD, potentially political events. Standards worded in ways that read as constraint on the announced transformation invite federal re-scoping. Standards worded as enabling the announced transformation are politically load-bearing in the *right* direction.

## Evidence — what actually binds

### Regulation (cannot be moved on project timelines)

**Fire alarm intelligibility.** NFPA 72 *National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code* requires that emergency voice communication systems achieve, where quantitative measurement is performed, an average STI ≥ 0.50 (CIS ≥ 0.70) and STI ≥ 0.45 at not less than 90% of measurement locations in each acoustically distinguishable space [Source: https://www.suppressionsystems.com/voice-intelligibility-in-mass-notification-systems/]. Enforcement runs through the adopted building code (Virginia USBC at IAD; DC Construction Codes at DCA), and the Authority Having Jurisdiction signs the commissioning documents. This is the code that governs the *emergency* audio path — not the routine paging path — but the two share the same loudspeaker infrastructure. A design that thins out or dampens loudspeaker coverage for a quieter routine environment will be re-tested for the emergency intelligibility floor at commissioning and, if it fails, the retrofit is charged to whoever specified the acoustic treatment.

**Effective communication under ADA Title II.** MWAA is a public entity. 28 CFR § 35.160 requires it to ensure communications with people with disabilities are "as effective as" communications with others, and to give "primary consideration" to the individual's requested auxiliary aid or service [Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-35/subpart-E/section-35.160]. Auxiliary aids include "qualified interpreters, notetakers, real-time computer-aided transcription services, written materials, telephone handset amplifiers, assistive listening devices, assistive listening systems, telephones compatible with hearing aids, closed caption decoders, open and closed captioning" and equivalent. The regulatory implication for a quiet-by-design standard is unavoidable: reducing the *audible* channel raises the required *visual, tactile, and staffed* channel.

**Air Carrier Access Act. 14 CFR Part 382** binds airlines on airport premises and requires accessible information at gate, ticket, and customer-service positions and accessible airline-provided kiosks [Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-II/subchapter-D/part-382]. The airline podium, the airline monitor, and the airline kiosk are not carve-outs from the standard. MWAA cannot enforce Part 382 on carriers, but it can — through the Use and Lease Agreement and tenant technology standards — require that carrier equipment installed on MWAA premises meet the accessibility redundancy the standard assumes.

**Part 139 alerting capability.** 14 CFR § 139.325 requires the Airport Emergency Plan to include a means to alert; § 139.339 requires public protection procedures. Whatever the routine audio design, the airport must retain the capability to broadcast to any part of the certificated air-operations area. Zoning the PA is compliant; disabling it is not.

**Historic preservation.** The Saarinen Main Terminal was determined NRHP-eligible in March 1978. Federally-funded undertakings that could affect the terminal or the Historic District trigger Section 106 [Source: https://www.achp.gov/sites/default/files/2024-02/2023_FAA_Section3_ProgressReport.pdf]. The Silver Line project drew an "Adverse Effect" finding at Saarinen Circle. Acoustic ceiling treatments, wall panels, or repositioned PA loudspeakers in the historic Main Terminal will require Section 106 consultation with the Virginia SHPO and potentially the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation. This is the schedule risk the standard document should call out by name.

**FIS space at IAD.** IAD carried 10.53 million international passengers in 2025 (roughly 36% of enplanements). Federal Inspection Services spaces are built to CBP's Airport Technical Design Standards. Queue-calling PA, announcement scripts, and secondary-inspection paging in FIS are CBP-controlled, not MWAA-controlled. A quiet-by-design standard applied inside FIS is a *negotiation* with CBP, not a specification MWAA can unilaterally adopt.

**TSA checkpoint.** TSA sets checkpoint design and controls its own equipment. CT scanners, Credential Authentication Technology, and queue instruction generate their own acoustic signature. MWAA can specify the acoustic environment *around* the checkpoint — soft ceilings, absorptive walls, decoupled queuing — but not inside it.

### Politics (moves only on named legislative or contractual windows)

**PFC cap.** Held at $4.50 in the 2024 FAA Reauthorization [Source: https://aaae.org/uscta-alerts/lawmakers-reach-agreement-on-final-faa-bill]. The next legislative vehicle is the FY2028 reauthorization. Assume no relief on the standards-adoption horizon. MWAA's terminal-interior capital for this standard is airline-recoverable capital or PFC — with a fixed PFC unit rate.

**DCA slot and perimeter regime.** The 2024 Reauthorization added 10 daily beyond-perimeter slot exemptions at DCA — the fifth expansion since 2000. Each expansion has been fought by the Virginia/Maryland/DC delegation and by American Airlines. The perimeter/slot regime is the canonical case of "everyone agrees it is inefficient and no one can move it outside a reauthorization vehicle." The Strategist should not treat DCA growth as an assumption of this standard; the standard should be written for the DCA operating profile that exists today and the one produced by the current 5-round-trip exemption class.

**2025 Airline Use and Lease Agreement.** MWAA and its signatory carriers agreed on a 15-year framework effective January 1, 2025 with an approximately $9B initial capital program. Precise MII thresholds and cost-recovery categorization are not on the public record and should not be inferred from industry convention. What is knowable: standards changes that raise airline-recoverable cost need airline economic buy-in before board adoption or they become the subject of the next MII dispute.

**Post-collision political altitude at DCA.** The January 29, 2025 PSA/Army UH-60L midair killed 67. The February 2026 NTSB final report faulted FAA, air traffic, and the Army. FAA removed Route 4 between Hains Point and the Wilson Bridge and restricted runways 15/33 and 4/22 during helicopter operations. The operational effect is more irregular-ops messaging, not less. Standards that assume DCA can tighten its announcement floor without a matching investment in irregular-ops visual and staffed communication miss the actual operating condition of the airfield after the collision.

## Case studies where the regulatory or political force determined the outcome

**SFO Quiet Airport policy — read the fine print.** SFO reports removing more than 90 minutes of daily announcement time and reducing on-site noise by more than 40 percent. Neither figure appears in a published methodology accessible to a peer reviewer. California operates under its own Title 24 acoustic code and its own AHJ interpretation of NFPA 72. SFO's carrier mix, its terminal geometry, and its state code baseline are not IAD's or DCA's. Cite SFO for *policy precedent*, not for *performance-standard evidence*. Any MWAA number lifted from an SFO press release will be flagged by the evidence prosecutor and pulled by the fact-checker.

**Dulles Silver Line, Section 106.** FTA's Refined Locally Preferred Alternative for the Silver Line was found to have an Adverse Effect on the Dulles Airport Historic District through displacement of landscaping at Saarinen Circle. The finding did not stop the project; it triggered a Memorandum of Agreement and mitigation. Precedent for a Council recommendation: budget for a Programmatic Agreement covering acoustic and PA modifications inside the Historic District *up front*. Retrofitting a project-by-project Section 106 process into a design-manual amendment adds calendar risk you can avoid.

**DCA Round 5 slot expansion, 2024.** The 10-slot beyond-perimeter addition passed inside the Reauthorization because it was in the Reauthorization. Standalone DCA slot legislation has never passed. Any Strategist recommendation that depends on DCA growth, DCA traffic reduction, or DCA schedule redistribution is an assumption about the *next* Reauthorization, not a variable MWAA controls. Write the standard for the DCA that exists.

**PFC cap since 2001.** Held flat across four Congresses despite consistent airport-industry advocacy [Source: https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/airports/pfc/pfc_updates/pfc_72_19_Reauthorization.pdf]. In 2024 the reauthorization added PFC application-streamlining but no rate relief. If the standard's business case relies on a higher PFC to fund the standard, the business case fails on Day One at the Aviation Subcommittee.

## The Strategist's actual decision calendar

- **Design Manual annual update cycle.** MWAA updates the seven-volume 2020 Design Manual on an annual cadence. Any new quiet-by-design volume or amendment routes through this vehicle, not through a standalone board policy. Miss the annual update window and the standard is a year late to procurement.
- **Concourse E opens fall 2026.** The acoustic and PA baseline is being set in real time. Standards adopted *after* Concourse E opens are, for Concourse E, retrofit — not baseline.
- **IAD Concourse C/D replacement, in scoping.** This is the largest window. Standards adopted before the design contract is scoped will bind the architect; standards adopted after will be change orders.
- **DCA Terminal 1 concourse replacement, earlier phase.** Same logic, longer runway.
- **2025 Use and Lease Agreement, 15-year term.** Airline economic buy-in on cost-recovery changes has to be earned inside this window. Design Manual amendments that raise airline-recoverable cost without airline sign-off create MII exposure.
- **FAA reauthorization, FY2028.** The only realistic window for PFC or DCA slot/perimeter movement. Standards should be written for the current federal envelope, not for the one advocacy hopes for.
- **NEPA/Section 106 clocks on any federally-funded Concourse C/D or Historic District work.** 6–24 months, item-dependent. This is the clock the Strategist most often ignores and the one that most reliably slips a program.

## What the Strategist should stop assuming is a variable

- **DCA capacity or DCA slot allocation.** Fixed until the next Reauthorization.
- **PFC rate.** Fixed until the next Reauthorization.
- **AIP funding for terminal-interior acoustics.** Not eligible in exclusive-use terminal areas.
- **Federal preemption of local political input on IAD.** Reversed by the July 2026 White House rollout. IAD is now more politically exposed, not less.
- **Substitution of smartphone communication for staffed, visual, and audible redundancy.** Foreclosed by 28 CFR § 35.160 and 14 CFR Part 382.
- **A "silent" terminal.** Foreclosed by NFPA 72 emergency-voice intelligibility, Part 139.325 alerting, and Part 139.339 public protection.

The standard the Council can defensibly recommend is: a specified, measurable *routine* ambient and announcement floor, with the *emergency* intelligibility floor preserved and the *accessibility* redundancy expanded — adopted through the Design Manual, priced into the 2025 Use and Lease framework, sequenced ahead of the Concourse C/D and DCA T1 scoping windows, and cleared through Section 106 for any work touching the Saarinen envelope.

Anything more ambitious than that is a political argument, not a design standard, and it will lose the political argument on this reauthorization cycle.
