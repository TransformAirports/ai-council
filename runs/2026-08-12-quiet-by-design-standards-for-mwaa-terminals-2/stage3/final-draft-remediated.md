# The Loud Terminal Is the Unintelligible One

**A communications-integrity standard for MWAA terminals — argued as safety engineering, delivered through the Design Manual.**

*Run: quiet-by-design-standards-for-mwaa-terminals-2 · Final draft (Stage 3) · 2026-08-11*

---

## Executive Summary

MWAA holds approximately $6.99 billion of committed capital at IAD and $2.39 billion at DCA under the fifteen-year Airline Use and Lease Agreement effective January 1, 2025.[^1] On July 29, 2026, the President, the U.S. Transportation Secretary, United's CEO, and MWAA jointly announced a Dulles transformation described at more than $20 billion, provisional pending Board action.[^2] Every square foot of that work passes through the seven-volume MWAA Design Manual — "a mandatory guide with the force of law on the airport property."[^3] The recommendation: a tiered communications-integrity standard added to the Design Manual this capital cycle, issued by the Office of Engineering under delegated standards-setting authority, brought to the Board as an information item at the next Business Administration Committee, and escalated to a resolution only if AAAC consultation surfaces material airline objection or an AUL cost-center reclassification.

After the January 29, 2025 midair collision and the February 2026 NTSB final report, no board member should sign a policy paraphrasable as "reduced safety broadcasts."[^4] The standard is defensible only as the opposite: a floor that raises the probability the announcement that matters reaches the passenger who needs it. The emergency-communications chapter of the National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code sets intelligibility floors — a Speech Transmission Index of at least 0.45 at 90% of measurement locations in each Acoustically Distinguishable Space, average 0.50[^5] — because the loud, reverberant terminal is precisely what fails them. ACRP 175 targets STI 0.50 with 10–15 dB(A) signal-to-noise in pier-style departure lounges;[^6] IEC 60268-16:2020 supplies the measurement method.[^7]

Design-time acoustic treatment runs roughly two to four times below retrofit at commercial-benchmark midpoints.[^8] The enforcement vehicle already exists: the Design Manual binds consultants and contractors today, and extending it into tenant audio is the design objective, contingent on verification of the 2025 AUL's majority-in-interest provisions.[^9] The window is narrow: debt-service coverage compresses from 1.63× in 2024 to roughly 1.3× over the five-year planning window as some $5.5 billion of new debt phases in,[^10] which is why the amendment must be scoped to work still in programming, not programs already in design.

## The Loud Terminal Is the Unintelligible One

Hospital intensive-care units produce so many simultaneous alarms that clinical staff stop hearing them. The Joint Commission's response was not to reduce alarms in the name of patient comfort — it was National Patient Safety Goal NPSG.06.01.01, phased in 2014 and 2016: every accredited hospital had to inventory its alarms, rank them, govern them, and prove it.[^11] Indiscriminate broadcast was the safety failure. Airports face the same pattern, at lower stakes — and the same logic applies.

Picture the IAD international connecting bank in the late-afternoon peak: a gate change pages overhead, a TSA advisory follows, two boarding calls run in adjacent bays, a courtesy page cuts through. On paper, operations serving the passenger. In a reverberant holdroom shared with adjacent gates, it is the alarm no one hears. IAD moved 29.01 million passengers in 2025, 10.53 million of them international — the highest 2025 growth rate among the nation's fifty largest airports.[^12] DCA traffic fell in 2025 following the January collision, government-travel softening, and a forty-three-day federal shutdown. Every one of those passengers expected the announcement that mattered to reach them.

MWAA is about to specify, from the studs up, an enormous run of new terminal space. The July 29 announcement covers more than 5 million square feet: Concourses C and D replaced, AeroTrain extended, mobile lounges retired, Saarinen's Main Terminal preserved.[^2] The committed AUL figures are firm — roughly $6.99 billion at IAD and $2.39 billion at DCA.[^1] A Cranky Flier analysis cited an airline briefing placing fully loaded IAD transformation cost per enplanement at $90.64, against $12.88 today.[^13] That reluctance strengthens the case for a Design Manual amendment. A standard that lives in the Manual survives program shrinkage, re-scoping, or a change of administration.

The obvious objection first. After NTSB AIR-26/02, no board member wants a policy paraphrased as "airport cuts safety announcements."[^4] That instinct is correct — and it is the argument. The National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code's emergency-communications chapter sets an intelligibility floor for each Acoustically Distinguishable Space because a terminal is not one acoustic environment.[^5] Fail that floor by being loud, reverberant, and cross-broadcast: precisely the condition an unspecified terminal defaults to. High ambient noise pushes STI down the qualitative bands of IEC 60268-16.[^7] The louder the terminal, the less intelligible the emergency announcement.

The peer set usually cited is a category error. London City has run a silent policy since 2008.[^14] Helsinki-Vantaa (since 2015) and Copenhagen moved information to screens and apps; Schiphol layered a reduced-announcement regime onto dense flight-information displays.[^15] None of the four has published measured intelligibility, missed-boarding, or channel-redundancy data.[^15] They are policy analogues, not design precedents. MWAA does not need to import their brand. It needs to out-engineer them.

SFO is the closest North American precedent, and it shows both the model and the failure mode. SFO reports its Quiet Airport program removed roughly 90 minutes of daily announcement time, cut individual paging from 492 to 261 occurrences per day, and reduced onsite noise by more than 40%.[^16] The same record holds documented passenger complaints and missed-flight anecdotes.[^17] Borrow the operating model: zoned delivery, discontinued cross-terminal final calls, gate-area retention. Quote the outcome numbers as SFO reports them, not as facts. Announcement reduction without intelligibility engineering and channel redundancy is subtraction, not design.

The recommendation must pass one test above all: the people who most need to hear. 28 CFR 35.160 obligates MWAA to ensure effective communication with people with disabilities.[^18] The 2010 ADA Standards §219 and §706 require assistive-listening systems in each assembly area where audible communication is integral to use.[^19] 14 CFR Part 382 sets a floor requiring at least 25% of shared-use automated airport kiosks in each location to meet accessibility design specifications at airports with 10,000-plus enplanements.[^20] ACRP Report 239 treats digital-first substitution as partial, not sufficient, for travelers with disabilities and older adults.[^21] A standard that mandates redundant visual, digital, staffed, and hearing-loop channels binds MWAA harder than current practice, not softer. One schedule note: the Saarinen Main Terminal was determined National Register-eligible in 1978, and Section 106 consultation with the Virginia SHPO and ACHP runs six to eighteen months, longer with a Memorandum of Agreement[^22] — reason to keep the amendment's first applications outside the eligible-district envelope.

Governance is the technology. Automated announcement platforms can be specified in Design Manual terms: centralized management, audit logs of every broadcast, elimination of manual-initiation errors, templated multi-language delivery.[^23] This is the Joint Commission move applied to airports: inventory, rank, govern, log — run by a standing Announcement Governance Committee reporting annually to the Board's Business Administration Committee.

Which raises the tenant question. At preferential-use gates, the podium PA is effectively airline-owned. United moves about 67% of IAD traffic; American holds roughly 53% of DCA market share.[^24] A standard that cannot reach tenant audio governs half the soundscape. The amendment, once verified against the 2025 AUL's majority-in-interest provisions, should be written to reach it.[^9]

The financial case rests on rework avoidance, not new capital. Commercial pricing guides put new-construction acoustic ceiling treatment at $5–$15 per square foot and retrofit panel treatment at $18–$45 — non-airport, medium-confidence directional benchmarks, but they point the same direction: design-time treatment runs roughly two to four times below retrofit at the midpoints.[^8] An order-of-magnitude range for the full stack of $12–$40 million sits between 0.17% and 0.57% of the committed IAD program.[^8] Added specification cost inside programs already in design cuts against debt-service coverage, which is why scoping to programming-phase work is the difference between the easy Board sell and the wrong one.[^10]

The standard writes itself as a two-tier document. Mandatory at both airports: an Acoustically Distinguishable Space map as a design deliverable; intelligibility floors per the emergency-communications chapter of the National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code and ACRP 175;[^6] measurement per IEC 60268-16:2020, commissioned under representative traffic with a cure period rather than a certificate-of-occupancy gate;[^7] accessibility floors per 28 CFR 35.160, ADA §219/§706, and 14 CFR Part 382; hearing conservation per 29 CFR 1910.95;[^25] audit-logged announcement governance; and concessionaire audio held within an authority-defined ambient limit. Tiered by context: PA zoning depth, sensory-quiet zone scope (a low-stimulation benchmark of 35 dB(A) in designated areas),[^26] tenant-audio enforcement mechanics, and the physical toolkit for a Saarinen-eligible head-house versus a new-build midfield concourse versus a Pelli-era DCA terminal.

Exercise it first where friction is lowest. Subject to project phase — which the Council did not verify — the DCA Terminal 1 concourse replacement is the natural choice: smaller, less politicized, less encumbered by the July 2026 political stack around Dulles. If Terminal 1 has passed scope definition, the reference application shifts to the earliest eligible IAD C/D element still in programming. Either way, Concourse E — opening this fall with fourteen new United gates[^27] — carries the first commissioned STI and ambient baseline under representative traffic.

The strongest counter-case is redundancy: existing federal standards already set the floors that matter. Half-right. The emergency-communications chapter binds emergency voice; it does not require ADS mapping as a design deliverable, does not govern routine PA, does not compel governance of manual-initiation error. ADA §219 sets receiver counts, not intelligibility floors for daily announcements. ACRP 175 is guidance, not code. The amendment's marginal content — the ADS map at each milestone, representative-traffic commissioning, audit-logged governance, channel-redundancy floors, the IROPS override, media limits, a tenant-audio boundary tied to the AUL — is exactly what the existing floors do not require.

Return to the ICU. The Joint Commission did not tell hospitals to reduce alarms. It told them to prove they knew which ones mattered, that those would be heard, and that the machinery for governing the rest was on the record. That is what a communications-integrity standard buys an airport. The Board question is not whether to fund a program. It is whether to write the paragraph.

## Airport Decision Card

**Owner and instrument.** MWAA President and CEO sponsors. Vice President for Engineering drafts and issues the amendment under delegated standards-setting authority exercised for annual Design Manual revisions. The Board receives it as an information item at the next Business Administration Committee. A formal resolution is initiated only if AAAC consultation surfaces material airline objection or the 2025 AUL cost-center analysis[^1] requires majority-in-interest treatment.

**External framing convention.** Internal engineering name: "quiet-by-design." External policy name in all Board papers, press releases, DOT correspondence, and airline briefings: "MWAA Communications-Integrity Standard."

**Airline-consultation lane (parallel to drafting, on the critical path).** Weeks one through three: confidential briefings to United and American executive station managers and capital counsels. Week four: formal AAAC briefing. Weeks four through eight: thirty-day comment window on draft amendment language. Week twelve: written MWAA position on cost-recovery categorization keyed to the 2025 AUL's[^1] cost-center matrix. No amendment language enters any scope document until that position letter is on the record.

**First application and baseline.** Subject to project phase: DCA Terminal 1 concourse replacement (preferred) or earliest eligible IAD C/D element still in programming. Concourse E baseline STI and ambient measurement commissioned within ninety days of opening, benchmarked against ACRP 175 and the emergency-communications chapter of the National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code.[^27]

**Cost and funding.** Capital stack $12–$40 million one-time,[^8] allocated roughly 60/40 IAD/DCA proportional to committed program footprint. Funding candidates: program contingency inside committed AUL work; PFC amendment for mobile notification platform; GARB only if a stand-alone capital element requires it. Airline recoverability characterized in the Week 12 position letter under the 2025 AUL's[^1] cost-center matrix. Operating tail (platform license, audit-log staffing, commissioning verification): to be scoped during drafting.

**Dependencies.** 2025 AUL majority-in-interest and cost-recovery text;[^1] tenant technology standards apparatus;[^9] Section 106 consultation with Virginia SHPO and ACHP on any Saarinen-adjacent application (six to eighteen months, longer with MOA);[^22] Fire Marshal AHJ concurrence at each airport; FAA Part 139 emergency-communications compliance baseline; represented-workforce bargaining posture on any new duties.

**Leading indicators.**
1. Amendment language published in the next Design Manual annual revision and cited in the first eligible major terminal-package scope documents.
2. Commissioned STI and ambient readings at Concourse E under representative traffic within ninety days of opening, benchmarked against ACRP 175.[^27]
3. Missed-boarding and ADA-effective-communication complaint rates at amendment-governed facilities, benchmarked against non-amendment facilities.

**Failure modes / stop conditions.**
- If the current Design Manual already contains equivalent acoustic and PA-zoning content, the recommendation contracts to a governance-and-verification amendment.
- If the 2025 AUL majority-in-interest text materially constrains tenant-audio enforcement, the tenant tier drops from mandatory to recommended pending renegotiation.
- If the Concourse E baseline shows existing MWAA design already meets ACRP 175 and the emergency-communications chapter targets under representative traffic, mandatory application contracts to governance and IROPS behavior.
