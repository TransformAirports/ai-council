# Contrarian Brief: The Case Against a Visitor Pass Program at Houston Airports

**Run:** Visitor Access at Houston Airports (IAH / HOU)
**Agent:** Contrarian — strongest honest case *against* the thesis
**Position in one line:** A visitor pass program is a goodwill gesture dressed as an operational initiative, and Houston is close to the worst-sited airport system in the country to attempt it. The benefits are small, soft, and structurally unavailable when demand peaks; the costs and risks land on the exact bottleneck Houston already cannot manage.

My job is not to pretend the program is dangerous or that peer airports are reckless. About 20 airports run these programs and none has produced a reported mass-casualty or catastrophic breach. The honest contrarian case is narrower and harder to dodge: **the marginal load falls on a saturated checkpoint, the marginal benefit is a rounding error, and the whole thing is contingent on a TSA that could not screen Houston's actual passengers in 2026.** If the Council recommends this, it should do so knowing exactly how thin the case is.

---

## 1. The strongest version of the argument against

- **Houston is the wrong system for this, specifically.** IAH and Hobby posted among the *highest* TSA officer callout rates in the nation in 2026 — 42.4% at IAH and 47.4% at HOU — with waits of two to four hours and at least half the security lanes closed [Source: https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/25/us/houston-bush-airport-tsa-wait-times] [Source: https://www.khou.com/article/news/local/bush-hobby-airport-tsa-shortage-shutdown-secruity-wait-times/285-d1d9faa7-56cd-48fe-99f0-02cca3997a70]. You do not add discretionary bodies to the queue that already can't clear the ticketed passengers who paid to be there.

- **Visitors load the slowest lanes.** Every operating program routes non-travelers through *general* screening — PreCheck and CLEAR are explicitly unavailable to pass holders [Source: https://flytulsa.com/travel/amenities/tul-visitor-pass-program/]. So the incremental demand doesn't spread across the fast lanes; it concentrates on the standard checkpoint that is Houston's binding constraint.

- **The pioneer quit and never came back.** myPITpass (Pittsburgh, 2017) was the celebrated first mover that "led the way." It ended in 2020 and did not restart [Source: https://www.futuretravelexperience.com/2017/08/pittsburgh-international-airport-to-re-introduce-public-airside-access/]. The "21 airports now do it" narrative is survivorship framing that hides the fact that the flagship abandoned the program and stayed out for years.

- **The programs run at token scale and vanish at peak.** SEA caps visitors at 300/day and suspended the pass across multiple June–July 2026 dates for the World Cup and summer travel; it reserves the right "in its sole discretion to cancel the program or modify program rules at any time without prior notice" [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs]. DTW caps at 75/day and has issued ~25,000 passes since October 2023 — roughly 40 a day [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-news/detroit-metropolitan-airport-extends-dtw-destination-pass-program]. The benefit is engineered to disappear on exactly the holidays, summer weekends, and big-event days when families most want to walk someone to a gate.

- **The "avoided escort labor" line in any cost model is likely near-zero.** The escort categories that actually consume airport and airline labor — unaccompanied minors, reduced-mobility passengers, badged employees — are airline-run or SIDA-controlled and do not convert to a public visitor pass. The visitor pass serves a *want* (greet a traveler, plane-watch, dine), not an operational cost driver. Any lifecycle model that books escort-hour savings against program cost is manufacturing an offset.

- **The security floor is "no rules."** TSA "has no official stance or rules on how airports' guest pass programs should operate, as long as they comply with security rules" [Source: https://deeparrival.com/news/airport-guest-pass-non-flyers-2026/]. Secure Flight vets the applicant, but the airport owns every residual risk TSA declines to opine on: reconnaissance by a self-selected long-dwell population, sterile-side hand-offs and staging, insider-assistance surface, and — critically — headcount with no PNR and no airline of record to account for during an evacuation or IROPS.

- **The program's foundation is a TSA that failed Houston in 2026.** The entire concept rides on TSA voluntarily screening non-travelers. During the 2026 shutdown, TSA could not staff screening for paying passengers at IAH/HOU. A program you launch in good times becomes the first thing suspended in bad times — and creating a public expectation and then yanking it is worse for customer trust than never offering it.

- **Opportunity cost and optics.** Kiosks, application processing, identity verification, comms, training, legal review, and — above all — scarce checkpoint capacity spent on 40–300 non-travelers a day is capacity not spent on the passengers standing in a two-hour line. "We let plane-spotters past security while ticketed passengers missed flights" is a headline and a board question, not a customer-experience win.

## 2. Evidence

**Houston's checkpoints are already saturated.** CNN reported Bush Intercontinental among the worst TSA wait times in the country, with lanes closed and loads funneled through a fraction of capacity [Source: https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/25/us/houston-bush-airport-tsa-wait-times]. KHOU reported IAH callout at 42.4% and HOU at 47.4% — the highest in the nation — with 100% of spring-break load pushed through under 50% of lanes and waits reaching three-plus hours [Source: https://www.khou.com/article/news/local/bush-hobby-airport-tsa-shortage-shutdown-secruity-wait-times/285-d1d9faa7-56cd-48fe-99f0-02cca3997a70]. This is the checkpoint any visitor program would feed.

**Every program routes visitors through general screening.** SEA directs pass holders through general screening at a designated checkpoint between 5 a.m. and 10 p.m.; TUL states PreCheck and CLEAR "cannot be used" with the visitor pass [Source: https://flytulsa.com/travel/amenities/tul-visitor-pass-program/]. SEA itself notes more than a third of daily passengers arrive before 9 a.m., straining checkpoints [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/live-estimated-checkpoint-wait-times].

**The scale is deliberately trivial and peak-suppressed.** SEA: 300/day cap, day-of or up-to-seven-days-ahead application, TSA approval, and explicit suspension on June 18–22, 25–26, 28–29, July 5–6, 9–10, 2026 for peak volume and the World Cup [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs]. DTW: 75/day cap, ~60 applicants/day, ~25,000 passes since October 2023; participant surveys show the dominant use is meeting an arriving/departing traveler, with some plane-watching or dining [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-news/detroit-metropolitan-airport-extends-dtw-destination-pass-program]. These are goodwill volumes, not operational ones.

**The pioneer's exit is documented.** myPITpass launched September 2017 with weekday 9-to-5 airside access and a 24-hour background-checked clearance; it ended in 2020 and did not restart, with construction cited as the reason it stayed dark [Source: https://www.futuretravelexperience.com/2017/08/pittsburgh-international-airport-to-re-introduce-public-airside-access/]. Whatever the stated reason, the first mover ran the program for under three years and then chose not to bring it back for years.

**The regulatory posture puts risk on the airport.** Secure Flight vets non-travelers seeking sterile-area access via airport-issued gate passes, but TSA takes "no official stance or rules" on program design [Source: https://deeparrival.com/news/airport-guest-pass-non-flyers-2026/]. Programs already build in enforcement language — removal for anyone who "presents a potential security concern" or disrupts operations — and reserve the right to revoke access [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/VisitorFAQs]. That language exists because the operating airport, not TSA, absorbs the incident.

**Passes are free to the visitor, which is the tell.** The passes cost the visitor nothing at most airports [Source: https://deeparrival.com/news/airport-guest-pass-non-flyers-2026/]. A free program with a 40–300/day velvet rope is a marketing and community-relations product. There is nothing wrong with that — but it should be evaluated as marketing spend, not as an operational or revenue initiative, and it should compete against other marketing dollars, not hide inside a security-operations business case.

## 3. Where this contrarian position is wrong (the honest concessions)

I would be dishonest to argue the program is indefensible everywhere. Here is where the anti-thesis breaks down, and the strategist should exploit these:

- **Hobby is a different airport than Bush.** The saturation case is strongest at IAH. HOU is smaller and point-to-point; a hard-capped, off-peak-only, heavily blacklisted pilot at Hobby could carry near-zero marginal checkpoint load while capturing most of the community value. If the recommendation is "pilot at HOU, not IAH," my strongest objection weakens considerably.

- **A well-bounded post-security, non-SIDA trough program adds little load.** If visitors are capped hard (say 50/day), barred during the pre-9 a.m. peak and all blackout periods, and screened only in genuine lulls, the incremental checkpoint demand can be a rounding error even at IAH. The saturation argument assumes the airport won't discipline the caps and hours. A serious program design can neutralize it.

- **The narrow, high-value use cases are real.** Military and USO greetings, and assistance for elderly or first-time travelers, are genuine, sympathetic, and politically potent. Narrow eligibility could capture perhaps 80% of the goodwill at 5% of the volume and risk. "Everyone gets a pass" is weak; "Gold Star families, USO, and credentialed elder-assist" is hard to argue against.

- **The absolutist security objection is not supported.** ~20 airports operate these programs and I found no evidence of a resulting catastrophic breach. Incremental risk over an already-Secure-Flight-vetted, TSA-screened body is real but small. My honest position is cost/benefit and Houston-fit, **not** "this is a security hazard." A strategist who tries to kill the program on raw danger will lose; one who kills it on saturation, scale, and opportunity cost is on solid ground.

- **Concession and community value are real, just small.** DTW and SEA participants do dine and shop. If Houston Airports explicitly values the goodwill and the modest concession spend, and books it honestly as such, the program can clear a low bar. It fails only when it is sold as ROI it cannot produce.

## 4. Data points a strategist must address or concede

1. **"IAH and HOU had the nation's highest TSA callout rates — 42.4% and 47.4% — with waits of two to four hours and half the lanes closed in 2026."** [Source: https://www.khou.com/article/news/local/bush-hobby-airport-tsa-shortage-shutdown-secruity-wait-times/285-d1d9faa7-56cd-48fe-99f0-02cca3997a70] Any recommendation must explain why you add discretionary throughput to *this* checkpoint, and what happens to the program the next time TSA staffing collapses.

2. **"myPITpass — the first and most celebrated program — ran for under three years, ended in 2020, and did not restart."** [Source: https://www.futuretravelexperience.com/2017/08/pittsburgh-international-airport-to-re-introduce-public-airside-access/] The bandwagon count (21 airports) must be reconciled with the pioneer's exit.

3. **"SEA caps visitors at 300/day, suspends the program for the 2026 World Cup and summer peaks, and reserves the right to cancel at any time without prior notice."** [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs] The strategist must concede that the benefit is structurally unavailable on the highest-demand days and defend building a customer expectation the airport plans to revoke on cue.

4. **"DTW caps at 75/day and has averaged roughly 40 visitors a day since October 2023."** [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-news/detroit-metropolitan-airport-extends-dtw-destination-pass-program] At that volume, the "avoided escort hours," concession revenue, and efficiency savings in any lifecycle model are negligible; the strategist must show the numbers or drop the operational-ROI framing.

5. **"TSA has no official stance or rules on how airports' guest pass programs should operate, and visitors clear only general screening — not PreCheck or CLEAR."** [Source: https://deeparrival.com/news/airport-guest-pass-non-flyers-2026/] [Source: https://flytulsa.com/travel/amenities/tul-visitor-pass-program/] The airport owns the residual security and evacuation-accountability risk, and the marginal load lands on the slowest lanes. The recommendation must place that liability and that congestion somewhere explicit.

---

## Bottom line for the Council

The visitor pass is not scary and it is not novel. It is *small* — deliberately, structurally small — and it is being contemplated for the one large US system that currently cannot process the passengers it already has. If the Council recommends it, the defensible version is a hard-capped, off-peak, blackout-heavy pilot at Hobby with narrow high-goodwill eligibility, sold honestly as community relations rather than operational return. The version that fails is any program at IAH sold as efficiency, cost savings, or revenue. The strategist's burden is to show why Houston should spend scarce checkpoint capacity and administrative effort on 40–300 non-travelers a day while ticketed passengers wait two hours — and to be honest that on peak days, the program will simply be switched off.
