# Airport COO Brief: Visitor Access at Houston Airports (IAH / HOU)

*Independent Stage 1 research brief. Written from the operator's chair — the person who has to make IAH and HOU work at 5:45 AM on the Monday of a peak-week push, regardless of what the strategy deck says.*

---

## Framing: the one thing every visitor-pass deck gets wrong

A visitor pass program is not an airport amenity that happens to touch the checkpoint. It **is** a checkpoint program. Every benefit in the thesis — greeting travelers, escorting elderly passengers, concessions dwell, community goodwill, avoided escort labor — is delivered by moving a non-traveler through the single most capacity-constrained, most staffing-fragile, least airport-controlled asset on the property: the TSA screening lane.

That is the frame. The operator does not own the lane. TSA owns the lane. The airport owns the *permission slip* and the *floor space on either side of it*. Any honest operational case has to start there, because it determines which levers I actually control (cap, hours, checkpoint selection, blackout dates, marketing throttle) and which I only get to ask nicely about (lanes open, officers assigned, callout rate on a shutdown Friday).

---

## 1. Key findings

- **Every operating peer program is a rationed program, and the ration is small.** DTW caps at 75/day, MSY at 50 weekday / 100 weekend, PHL Wingmate at 100/day, SEA at 300/day. These are not timid numbers — they are the numbers that survived contact with a real checkpoint. Against IAH's ~40M+ annual passengers, a 100–300/day cap is a rounding error on volume but a live variable on a peak-hour queue. [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs] [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs]

- **The program is the first thing suspended when the airport gets busy or breaks.** SEA suspended the pass on selected 2026 summer/World Cup peak dates and again after a cyberattack. myPITpass — the program that started the whole US trend in 2017 — is currently suspended. This tells the operator something important: peers treat visitor access as a *sheddable load*, not a committed service. Design it that way from day one. [Source: https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-tacoma-international-airportspot-saver-and-the-sea-visitor-pass-program-unavailable-on-certain-dates-this-summer-2026-world-cup-in-seattle-games-summer-travel] [Source: https://www.wpxi.com/news/top-stories/pittsburgh-international-to-allow-non-ticketed-visitors-access-to-airside-terminal/599351950/]

- **The security-risk debate is largely settled and largely a red herring; the throughput debate is the real fight.** Every peer runs visitors through the *same* TSA screening, same prohibited-items rules, same watch-list vetting (TSA, not the airport, adjudicates PHL applications). Incremental security risk versus a ticketed passenger is marginal. The genuine operational cost is not a bomb in a bag — it is 40 extra bodies in the Terminal C line at 6:10 AM. [Source: https://www.phl.org/wingmate/FAQ] [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs]

- **The checkpoint is the binding constraint, and I don't control it.** IAH ran ~29-minute average waits with Terminal E hitting 45 minutes on bad days in early 2026 — during a $1.46B redevelopment that added checkpoint capacity. Visitor throughput competes directly with the resource TSA staffs to a budget, and TSA staffing is exposed to shutdowns and callout spikes (frontline absence rose to ~6% during the recent shutdown vs. ~2% baseline). A program whose success depends on lanes I can't open is a program I have to be able to throttle unilaterally. [Source: https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/03/18/tsa-security-wait-times-at-bush-intercontinental-airport-iah-terminal-e-sit-at-45-minutes/] [Source: https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/16/us/airport-wait-tsa-delay-agents-quit]

- **Advance application with off-airport vetting is the operating model that works; same-day kiosks are the one that bites you.** PHL and MSY require 1–7 day / 24-hour advance application with TSA adjudicating before arrival. DTW and legacy myPITpass allowed same-day kiosk sign-up at the departures level — which pushes ID check, vetting, and queue management onto terminal staff at the worst possible moment and location. For a hub of IAH's size, advance-only is the only defensible design. [Source: https://www.phl.org/newsroom/wingmate-update] [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/news/msy-guest-pass-allows-non-ticketed-passengers-post-security]

- **The avoided-escort-labor benefit is real but narrow, and it is not where the volume is.** Escort reduction matters for discrete, authorized post-security activities (events, tours, meet-and-assist for the elderly). It does not offset the marginal staffing the program *adds*: queue monitoring, wayfinding, incident response for lost/unattended visitors, and emergency accountability for people who are not on a manifest. The labor math is a swap, not a pure savings, and honest modeling should show both sides.

- **Houston's timing cuts both ways.** IAH is closing out the ICP redevelopment (new checkpoints, new bag system certification in early 2026) and HOU is mid-West Concourse expansion — meaning fresh checkpoint capacity to absorb visitors, but also a 2026 FIFA World Cup host-city demand shock that is exactly when you'd blackout the program. Launch timing has to thread that. [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/articles/item/2025-a-year-of-transformation-at-houston-airports/] [Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/lake-houston-humble-kingwood/transportation/2026/01/29/iah-to-wrap-up-146b-terminal-redevelopment-program-in-early-2026/]

## 2. Evidence

**The benchmark programs — actual operating parameters, not brochure copy.**

*SEA Visitor Pass (Seattle):* 300 visitors/day cap, single dedicated checkpoint (Checkpoint 4), entry 5 AM–10 PM (last entry 9 PM), 4-hour visit limit, TSA no-fly vetting, one adult may escort up to five minors, commercial use prohibited. Suspended on select peak/World Cup dates and after a cyberattack. This is the largest US cap and it is still confined to *one* checkpoint. [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs] [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/sea-visitor-pass-terms-use]

*DTW Destination Pass (Detroit):* 75 visitors/day, both terminals, 4 AM–9 PM daily, same-day kiosk enrollment on the departures level, same ID and screening rules as passengers, minors escorted. Made permanent after a pilot — proof the model can stabilize. [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs] [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-news/detroit-metropolitan-airport-extends-dtw-destination-pass-program]

*PHL Wingmate (Philadelphia):* 100 visitors/day, online application 1–7 days in advance (no same-day), **TSA — not the airport — reviews every application**, digital pass with a two-hour entry window, 6-hour visit limit, valid at all non-PreCheck checkpoints, 4 AM–9 PM entry. This is the cleanest "advance-vetted, airport-throttled" model in the peer set. [Source: https://www.phl.org/newsroom/wingmate-update] [Source: https://www.phl.org/wingmate/FAQ]

*MSY Indulge Guest Pass (New Orleans):* 50 weekday / 100 weekend cap, 24-hour advance sign-up (no walk-ups), one visit per participant per month, 11 AM–8 PM, general boarding lanes with standard TSA screening. Note the *demand-management* tools: monthly per-person limit plus a mid-day window that avoids both peaks. [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/news/msy-guest-pass-allows-non-ticketed-passengers-post-security] [Source: https://flymsy.com/msy-guest-pass/]

*myPITpass (Pittsburgh):* The 2017 originator. Weekdays 9 AM–5 PM only, same-day kiosk on the ticketing level, no-fly vetting, standard screening, and — critically — **airport staff actively monitored checkpoint flow and cut off myPITpass access when it threatened ticketed-passenger throughput.** That throttle is the single most important operational feature any Houston program must copy. The program is currently suspended. [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/pittsburgh-intl-opens-airside-areas-non-ticketed-visitors/] [Source: https://www.wpxi.com/news/top-stories/pittsburgh-international-to-allow-non-ticketed-visitors-access-to-airside-terminal/599351950/]

**The operator's constraint — the checkpoint I don't own.**

The consistent design choice across all five programs — off-peak hours, hard daily caps, single-checkpoint confinement, real-time throttle authority — is not customer-service caution. It is every operator independently concluding that visitor load must yield to passenger throughput. IAH's own numbers make the point: ~29-minute average waits and Terminal E at 45 minutes even *with* the new ICP checkpoints online. [Source: https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/03/18/tsa-security-wait-times-at-bush-intercontinental-airport-iah-terminal-e-sit-at-45-minutes/]

And the resource that clears those lines is not mine. TSA staffs lanes to a federal budget. During the recent shutdown, frontline callouts jumped to ~6% (from ~2%), agents quit, and checkpoints closed. [Source: https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/16/us/airport-wait-tsa-delay-agents-quit] A program that adds bodies to a queue whose service rate is set by someone else's budget is a program I have to be able to switch off in a single phone call.

**The professional pushback is real and it's from labor.** When myPITpass launched, the flight attendants' union called it a "bad idea" that would "clog already frustratingly long TSA security lines." That's not NIMBYism — it's the frontline workforce naming the exact operational failure mode. [Source: https://www.foxnews.com/travel/flight-attendants-blast-pittsburgh-airports-new-tsa-rules-its-a-bad-idea]

**Houston-specific facts.** IAH: ~40M+ passengers/year; $1.46B Terminal Redevelopment Program (International Central Processor, new ticketing hall and checkpoints) closing out with TSA bag-system certification in early 2026. HOU: $470M West Concourse expansion, seven new gates, complete 2027. Both airports are gearing up for 2026 FIFA World Cup demand. [Source: https://communityimpact.com/houston/lake-houston-humble-kingwood/transportation/2026/01/29/iah-to-wrap-up-146b-terminal-redevelopment-program-in-early-2026/] [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/hou/redevelopment/west-concourse-expansion/]

## 3. The operational case — for and against, as the COO sees it

**The case FOR (why I'd sign, with conditions):**

1. *It's genuinely sheddable, so my downside is bounded.* Every peer proves I can cap it, blackout it, confine it to one checkpoint, and kill it in an afternoon when a runway closes or TSA loses two lanes. A benefit I can withdraw the instant it costs me is a benefit worth having.
2. *The vetting burden lands on TSA, not me — if I insist on the PHL model.* Advance application with TSA adjudication means I am not standing up a new identity-verification bureaucracy at the departures curb. I am issuing a floor pass to people the federal government already cleared.
3. *There is real, discrete escort labor to reclaim.* Military send-offs, meet-and-assist for elderly and first-time travelers, sanctioned tours and community events currently consume staff escort hours. A structured pass with defined use cases converts ad-hoc escorting into a managed, auditable process.
4. *Houston has fresh checkpoint capacity, right now, uniquely.* The ICP just added screening capacity at IAH. There is no better moment in a decade to absorb a modest, capped, off-peak visitor load than the year the new checkpoints come online.

**The case AGAINST (where this thesis dies in execution):**

1. *It competes for the one resource I don't control and can't buy.* Adding demand to a TSA-staffed, budget-constrained, shutdown-exposed lane is asymmetric risk: I own the political blame for the line, TSA owns the staffing. On a bad morning the visitor pass makes my worst asset worse and I can't fix it by spending money.
2. *Same-day enrollment is a trap and it's the seductive one.* DTW/myPITpass kiosks are the "easy launch." They put ID checks, vetting exceptions, and queue confusion on my terminal staff at the departures level during the push. Reject it. If the program can't run advance-only, it shouldn't run.
3. *Emergency accountability for non-manifest bodies.* In an evacuation or a security event, sterile-area population that isn't on any airline manifest complicates head-count and reunification. It's manageable with a live pass registry and hard caps — but it's a real IROPS wrinkle that decks never mention.
4. *Blackout dates are exactly the days the public wants in.* Holidays, spring break, World Cup — the emotionally resonant "greet grandma at the gate" moments are the peak days I must suspend the program. That gap between the marketing promise and the operational reality is a customer-experience liability if not messaged honestly up front.

**The COO's bottom line:** Yes, but only as a capped, advance-vetted, single-checkpoint, off-peak, unilaterally-throttleable pilot — and only with an explicit written throttle protocol that lives in the operations center, not in a policy binder. The program is defensible precisely *because* it can be small and sheddable. The moment anyone tries to scale it into a headline amenity or a revenue line, it stops being operable.

## 4. Concrete examples that frame the thesis

**Example 1 — The myPITpass throttle is the whole ballgame.** Pittsburgh's operators reserved the right to cut off visitor access in real time when checkpoint flow was threatened, and staff actively watched the lines to do it. That single feature is the difference between a program that serves passengers and one that fights them. Houston's model must hard-code an OPSCOMM-triggered throttle: when Terminal C wait exceeds a defined threshold, visitor entry pauses automatically, no approvals required. [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/pittsburgh-intl-opens-airside-areas-non-ticketed-visitors/]

**Example 2 — SEA's World Cup blackout is the Houston preview.** Seattle suspended its pass on selected summer 2026 and World Cup dates. Houston is *also* a 2026 World Cup host city closing out its checkpoints the same year. If Houston launches without a pre-published blackout calendar tied to demand forecasts, it will be improvising suspensions in front of angry families in June 2026. Publish the blackout logic before launch. [Source: https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-tacoma-international-airportspot-saver-and-the-sea-visitor-pass-program-unavailable-on-certain-dates-this-summer-2026-world-cup-in-seattle-games-summer-travel]

**Example 3 — Confine it to one checkpoint, like SEA.** SEA runs 300/day but only through Checkpoint 4. Single-checkpoint confinement lets me put a known, staffable load on a lane I've sized for it, keeps visitor wayfinding simple, and contains the throttle to one place. For IAH, pick one checkpoint in one terminal for the pilot — not "all non-PreCheck lanes" as PHL does. Start contained; expand only on evidence. [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs]

**Example 4 — The escort-labor swap, made honest.** A military family send-off today might pull a staff escort for two hours through a sterile area. Under a structured pass, those same people self-serve through screening — real escort hours reclaimed. But the same program adds a lost-visitor call at 7 PM, a queue-monitor position at the dedicated checkpoint, and an accountability line item in the evacuation plan. The lifecycle model must show *both* columns. Anyone who shows only the savings is selling, not operating.

**Example 5 — The advance-vetting firewall (PHL) vs. the curbside kiosk (DTW).** PHL routes every application through TSA 1–7 days out; the airport never touches adjudication and never manages a vetting exception at the curb. DTW's same-day kiosk is more convenient and pushes exactly that burden onto terminal staff during operations. Same benefit to the visitor; radically different operational load on my people. Houston takes the PHL firewall. [Source: https://www.phl.org/newsroom/wingmate-update] [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs]
