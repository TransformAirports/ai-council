# Stage 1 Brief — Director of Public Safety

**Run:** Visitor Access at Houston Airports (IAH / HOU)
**Chair:** Director of Public Safety (sworn police, ARFF/fire, and 911/dispatch under one command)
**Lens:** Whether a visitor pass program changes what our first responders can actually *deliver* — not whether it passes a TSA audit.

---

## Framing: the trap this thesis walks into

Most airport executives evaluate a visitor pass program the way they evaluate a concessions RFP — throughput, revenue, customer experience, with "security" handled by a checkbox that says "everyone gets TSA-screened, so risk is unchanged." That checkbox is where the reasoning goes wrong. TSA screening is a compliance control. It tells you the person carrying no prohibited item is unlikely to carry one. It tells you nothing about whether Houston Police Department (HPD) and Houston Fire Department (HFD) can absorb the *marginal population and marginal workload* the program adds — during a normal Tuesday, during an irregular-operations (IROPS) meltdown, and during the 80 seconds of a mass-casualty event.

The single most important fact about public safety at IAH and HOU is structural, and it reframes the entire question: **Houston Airports does not own its police or its fire department.** Sworn law enforcement is provided by HPD's Airport-IAH and Airport-HOU Divisions [Source: https://www.houstontx.gov/police/divisions/airport_iah/index.htm]. Aircraft Rescue and Firefighting (ARFF) is provided by HFD out of Stations 92, 96, and 54 at Bush Intercontinental [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/press-releases/item/houston-fire-department-opens-new-arff-92-station-at-iah/]. And 911 calls route to the Houston Emergency Center (HEC), the city's consolidated PSAP, which dispatches HPD and HFD units citywide [Source: https://www.houstontx.gov/hec/about.html]. Houston Airports is a *tenant* of its own public safety apparatus. Every officer, firefighter, and dispatcher a visitor pass program consumes is drawn from a city budget and a city chain of command that the Director of Aviation negotiates with — but does not control.

That is the gap between the org chart and what shows up at 3 AM.

## 1. Key findings

- **The incremental sterile-area risk from a vetted visitor is genuinely modest — and the program's advocates are right about that.** SEA runs Secure Flight watch-list checks on every applicant, caps access at 300 passes/day, and denies any name that pings the list [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/sea-visitor-pass-program]. A vetted, screened, government-ID-verified visitor is arguably a *lower* individual risk than a self-selected ticketed passenger, who gets the same screening with less advance vetting. Rejecting the program on "a bad actor could get in" grounds ignores that bad actors already buy $39 Spirit tickets. Be honest about this or lose credibility.

- **But the risk that matters is not identity — it's density and accountability, and the program does nothing good for either.** Visitors are not on a flight manifest. During an evacuation or mass-casualty event, they are an unknown denominator: people who don't know the building, aren't counted anywhere, and complicate headcount, reunification, and the "is everyone out?" question that governs when we let responders stand down.

- **The highest-consequence airport attacks happen in the public, pre-security areas a visitor pass never touches.** At Fort Lauderdale (FLL) in January 2017, the shooter — a *ticketed passenger* — retrieved a handgun from checked baggage and opened fire in the Terminal 2 baggage claim, a public zone requiring no credential of any kind: 5 killed, 6 wounded, in under 80 seconds [Source: https://www.policinginstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Ft.-Lauderdale-Hollywood-International-Airport-AAR.pdf]. A visitor pass program hardens nothing here. Selling it as a security improvement is a category error.

- **Self-evacuation onto the airfield is the recurring failure mode, and every added body makes it worse.** At FLL, roughly 90 minutes after the shooting, a phantom "second shooter" rumor triggered a chaotic self-evacuation with travelers pouring onto restricted tarmac [Source: https://viewfromthewing.boardingarea.com/2017/04/23/man-shot-passengers-baggage-claim-fort-lauderdale-airport-response-giant-cluster/]. At LAX in 2013, thousands streamed through fire-exit doors onto the ramp, forcing an immediate shutdown of flight operations [Source: https://www.newsweek.com/lax-mass-shooter-airport-passengers-evacuate-1643725]. Non-ticketed visitors in the sterile concourse add to this crowd without adding anyone who knows where the exits go.

- **"Adequate" law-enforcement staffing is a federal requirement that lags the program by 12–18 months.** 49 CFR 1542.217 requires LEOs "in the number and manner adequate to support" the security program, with full arrest authority [Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/1542.217]. A visitor program raises checkpoint volume, terminal population, and calls for service (medical, lost persons, theft, disputes). But you cannot conjure sworn officers — HPD recruiting, academy, and field training run roughly a year to eighteen months. The program can launch in 90 days; the staffing to support it cannot.

- **The existing programs already treat capacity and security as the binding constraint — and are willing to shut off.** myPITpass, the original 2017 program, is currently suspended while Pittsburgh works with TSA on resumption [Source: https://parkingnearairports.io/blog/view/164]. SEA blacks out its Visitor Pass on peak-demand dates, including around 2026 World Cup matches [Source: https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-tacoma-international-airportspot-saver-and-the-sea-visitor-pass-program-unavailable-on-certain-dates-this-summer-2026-world-cup-in-seattle-games-summer-travel]. The operators who run these programs treat a hard cap and a kill switch as non-negotiable. Houston should copy that discipline, not the marketing.

- **Houston's ARFF clock does not care about visitors — but the population it must account for does.** 14 CFR 139.319 requires ARFF to reach the midpoint of the farthest runway within 3 minutes; IAH's widebody international fleet drives a high ARFF index sustained by three staffed HFD stations [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/press-releases/item/houston-fire-department-opens-new-arff-92-station-at-iah/]. That capability is sized to *aircraft*, not terminal crowds. A structural fire, a medical surge, or an evacuation in a concourse now holding more non-ticketed people stresses the *structural and EMS* side of HFD, which is the same city apparatus and is not indexed to the visitor program at all.

## 2. Evidence

**Houston's public safety is a city function, not an airport function.** HPD staffs the Airport-IAH and Airport-HOU Divisions 24/7, patrolling checkpoints, gates, and terminals, with dedicated criminal investigations, tactical bike patrol, and explosive-detection canine units [Source: https://www.houstontx.gov/police/divisions/airport_iah/index.htm]. HFD provides ARFF and structural coverage; ARFF-92 opened in February 2026 as a 21,000-sq-ft facility with direct airfield access to hold the FAA response-time standard, one of three HFD stations at IAH [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/press-releases/item/houston-fire-department-opens-new-arff-92-station-at-iah/]. The HEC is the primary PSAP, consolidating 911 call-taking and HPD/HFD dispatch for the city [Source: https://www.houstontx.gov/hec/about.html]. Practical consequence: when a visitor calls 911 from a jet bridge or a concourse, the call lands at HEC and is routed to the airport division — the same queue as the rest of the fourth-largest city. During a citywide event (hurricane, flooding, a major incident downtown), airport calls compete with everything else.

**The federal framework the program must live inside.** 49 CFR Part 1542 governs airport security programs; 1542.217 requires the airport operator to provide LEOs with arrest authority "in the number and manner adequate" to support screening and the security program [Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/1542.217; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-XII/subchapter-C/part-1542]. A visitor pass program is an amendment to the TSA-approved Airport Security Program and requires TSA concurrence — SEA states access is "subject to TSA approval" and conditioned on Secure Flight watch-list checks [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/sea-visitor-pass-program]. This is the compliance layer. It is necessary and it is not sufficient.

**What the comparable programs actually do.** SEA: 300 passes/day, first-come, advance application up to 7 days, REAL ID required, watch-list vetting, denial on any database match [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/sea-visitor-pass-program]. DTW's Destination Pass piloted in 2019 and became permanent in 2020 [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass]. PIT's myPITpass — the 2017 originator — is currently suspended pending TSA coordination [Source: https://parkingnearairports.io/blog/view/164]. The through-line for a public safety chair: **caps, blackout dates, and suspension authority are standard equipment, because the operators know the program's safe operating envelope is narrow.**

**The mass-casualty case history.** FLL 2017: ticketed passenger, gun in checked baggage, baggage-claim public area, 5 dead / 6 wounded in under 80 seconds; the after-action review documented the phantom-shooter panic, the self-evacuation onto restricted tarmac, and reunification chaos [Source: https://www.policinginstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Ft.-Lauderdale-Hollywood-International-Airport-AAR.pdf; https://viewfromthewing.boardingarea.com/2017/04/23/man-shot-passengers-baggage-claim-fort-lauderdale-airport-response-giant-cluster/]. LAX 2013: fire-exit self-evacuation onto the ramp, flight operations shut down on the affected side [Source: https://www.newsweek.com/lax-mass-shooter-airport-passengers-evacuate-1643725]. Newark: a false threat and one shouted "evacuate" produced a panic evacuation, and the person who triggered it was detained [Source: https://www.foxnews.com/travel/newark-airport-passengers-evacuate-active-shooter]. The pattern is unambiguous: the deadliest failures are crowd-behavior and communication failures in and around *public* space, and they are worsened by density and by people who don't know the building.

## 3. The public safety case — for and against the thesis

### For (a well-controlled program is defensible, and may be net-positive on safety)

- **Vetting plus screening makes the marginal identity risk small.** Secure Flight checks and TSA screening mean the incremental sterile-side risk of a capped, vetted visitor is lower than the risk we already accept from every walk-up ticketed passenger [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/sea-visitor-pass-program]. Intellectual honesty requires conceding this.
- **A credentialed, capped, badged visitor population is *more* observable than the uncontrolled meeter-greeter crowd it partly replaces.** Today, curbside and baggage-claim crowds — the FLL zone — are entirely unvetted. A program that pulls some of that activity into a vetted, monitored channel could modestly *reduce* landside congestion at the exact points that are hardest to protect.
- **Escort-hour recovery is real officer time.** Authorized events, VIP meets, and unaccompanied-minor / special-assistance escorts currently burn HPD and airport-staff hours. A structured visitor channel can convert ad-hoc escorts into a managed process — genuine capacity returned to patrol.
- **The controls that make it safe already exist and are proven.** Daily caps, advance application, blackout dates, and a suspension switch are off-the-shelf [Source: https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-tacoma-international-airportspot-saver-and-the-sea-visitor-pass-program-unavailable-on-certain-dates-this-summer-2026-world-cup-in-seattle-games-summer-travel].

### Against (where the thesis ignores real response capability)

- **The safety benefit is claimed on the sterile side; the real risk lives landside, and the program doesn't touch it.** FLL happened in baggage claim. Nothing in a visitor pass hardens the highest-consequence zone [Source: https://www.policinginstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Ft.-Lauderdale-Hollywood-International-Airport-AAR.pdf].
- **It adds population and workload to departments Houston Airports does not command.** Marginal officers, firefighters, and dispatchers come from HPD, HFD, and HEC — city budgets, city priorities, city queues [Source: https://www.houstontx.gov/police/divisions/airport_iah/index.htm; https://www.houstontx.gov/hec/about.html]. "Adequate" LEO staffing under 1542.217 is a legal requirement that a 90-day program launch cannot meet, because sworn hiring runs 12–18 months.
- **Accountability degrades in exactly the scenarios that kill people.** Visitors aren't manifested. During a self-evacuation — the documented failure mode at FLL and LAX — every additional non-ticketed person is a body that complicates headcount, reunification, and the runway-incursion risk of crowds on the AOA [Source: https://www.newsweek.com/lax-mass-shooter-airport-passengers-evacuate-1643725].
- **More visitors means more unattended-bag reports, more false alarms, more terminal closures.** Each is a public-safety and IROPS event that consumes the same finite units. The volume scales with foot traffic.
- **Kill-switch discipline erodes under commercial pressure.** The cap and the blackout are only as good as the executive willingness to enforce them on the busiest, most profitable day — which is precisely when the security case for shutting off is strongest and the business case for staying open is loudest.

**My verdict as the public safety chair:** I do not oppose the program. I oppose *launching it as a compliance exercise*. A capped, single-location, vetted pilot with a hard public-safety veto is defensible and probably net-neutral to slightly positive on risk. A program sized to customer-experience ambition, without a funded staffing MOU and without accountability built into the Airport Emergency Plan, is a bet that nothing goes wrong on a peak day — and that is the bet that gets people hurt.

## 4. Operational realities a strategist can use

1. **The jurisdictional fact that reframes the debate.** Houston Airports doesn't employ its cops, firefighters, or dispatchers — HPD, HFD, and the Houston Emergency Center do [Source: https://www.houstontx.gov/police/divisions/airport_iah/index.htm; https://www.houstontx.gov/hec/about.html]. Any visitor pass business case that doesn't include a signed staffing/cost MOU with those city departments is booking a benefit while externalizing the cost onto agencies it can't direct.

2. **FLL 2017 is the counter-argument to "screening = safe."** Ticketed passenger, gun in checked baggage, public baggage claim, 5 dead in under 80 seconds, then a phantom-shooter self-evacuation onto the tarmac [Source: https://www.policinginstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Ft.-Lauderdale-Hollywood-International-Airport-AAR.pdf]. Use it to kill the idea that TSA screening addresses the risk the program actually changes.

3. **The federal staffing clause is a schedule constraint, not a footnote.** 49 CFR 1542.217 requires "adequate" LEO coverage with arrest authority [Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/1542.217]; the ARFF 3-minute standard under 14 CFR 139.319 is fixed [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/press-releases/item/houston-fire-department-opens-new-arff-92-station-at-iah/]. Sworn hiring is 12–18 months. That mismatch is the reason "phase it" is a safety requirement, not a preference.

4. **The comparables already tell you the safe envelope: cap it and be willing to turn it off.** SEA caps at 300/day with watch-list vetting and blacks out peak dates; PIT's originating program sits suspended [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/page/sea-visitor-pass-program; https://parkingnearairports.io/blog/view/164]. Houston should adopt a hard daily cap, IROPS/threat-level blackouts, and a Director-of-Public-Safety kill switch written into the program governance, not left to discretion.

5. **Build accountability in before launch, not after an incident.** Visitors must be integrated into the Airport Emergency Plan's evacuation headcount and reunification procedures, with a real-time roster of who is inside the sterile area on a given pass. Without it, the first mass-evacuation turns "is everyone out?" into an unanswerable question — the exact failure the FLL and LAX after-actions warned about [Source: https://www.newsweek.com/lax-mass-shooter-airport-passengers-evacuate-1643725].
