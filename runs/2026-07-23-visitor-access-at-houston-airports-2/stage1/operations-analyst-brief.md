# Operations-Analyst Brief — Visitor Access at Houston Airports

**Run:** visitor-access-at-houston-airports-2
**Lens:** Airport operational performance — throughput, checkpoint capacity, staffing workload, congestion, escort labor, and irregular-operations exposure.
**Bottom line up front:** The operational feasibility of a visitor pass program is not in doubt — six US airports run one today, and the annual visitor volume is a rounding error against passenger throughput. The entire operational question is *where, when, and how many*. Every functioning program is governed by a hard daily cap and a time window engineered to keep visitors out of the peak-hour checkpoint bank. Houston's decision is not "can we absorb visitors" (trivially yes) but "which checkpoint, which hours, and what cap protect peak throughput at a two-airport system already running 30–60 minute security waits during a multi-year construction program."

---

## 1. Key Findings

1. **Every operating program rations access with a hard daily cap — the cap is the whole design.** Realized caps: SEA 300/day, DTW 75/day, PHL Wingmate 100/day, MSY 50 weekday / 100 weekend, TPA 6 guests per 2-hour block per airside. The caps exist explicitly to protect checkpoint and circulation throughput, not for administrative convenience [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs] [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs] [Source: https://www.phl.org/wingmate/terms] [Source: https://flymsy.com/msy-guest-pass/] [Source: https://stuckattheairport.com/2020/01/13/tampa-intl-airport-expands-gate-pass-program/].

2. **At any realistic cap, visitors are statistically invisible airport-wide but concentrated at one pressure point.** A 100/day cap yields ~36,500 visitor-entries per year — 0.075% of IAH's 48.45 million 2024 passengers. The operational load is not the annual total; it is the handful of extra bodies arriving at *one checkpoint* during *specific hours*, which is why SEA confines its entire program to a single checkpoint (Checkpoint 4) [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs] [Source: https://roadgenius.com/statistics/airports/usa/george-bush-iah-airport/].

3. **Programs are deliberately scheduled around the peak departure bank — the time windows are an operational tell.** MSY opens at 11 a.m. (skipping the 4–7 a.m. bank); myPITpass ran 9 a.m.–5 p.m. weekdays only; TPA meters entry in two-hour blocks. IAH already runs 30–45 minute peak waits and up to ~60 minutes early Sunday, so any Houston program must blackout the morning banks rather than optimize around them [Source: https://flymsy.com/msy-guest-pass/] [Source: https://www.ifly.com/airports/george-bush-intercontinental-airport/wait-times].

4. **Screening cost is federal; the airport's marginal labor is issuance and incident response — and it scales with the staffing model chosen.** TSA absorbs the screening. The airport pays for identity verification, pass issuance, wayfinding, and IROPS accountability. Automated models (DTW kiosks; SEA/PHL digital passes) minimize recurring labor; staffed-desk models (MSY Customer Service Desk) do not [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs] [Source: https://flymsy.com/msy-guest-pass/].

5. **Realized uptake runs well below cap — demand is modest, which cuts both the congestion risk and the benefit case.** PHL logged "more than 10,300" Wingmate visitors cumulatively since November 1, 2023 — a fraction of the ~36,500/year its 100/day cap permits. The congestion downside is smaller than critics fear; the concession/experience upside is also smaller than boosters claim [Source: https://www.internationalairportreview.com/news/256744/over-10300-people-use-philadelphia-international-airports-wingmate-guest-pass-since-launch/].

6. **These programs are the first thing cut under operational stress — and IAH is currently in exactly that state.** Near-universal COVID suspension; Orlando (MCO) paused and only relaunched December 29, 2025 "to sort out staffing and operations"; SEA knocked offline by a 2024 cyberattack; and Pittsburgh — the program's *inventor* — never restarted because the terminal went into construction. IAH's multi-billion-dollar terminal redevelopment makes it structurally the wrong moment to add sterile-area population [Source: https://deeparrival.com/news/airport-guest-pass-non-flyers-2026/] [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/pittsburgh-intl-opens-airside-areas-non-ticketed-visitors/].

7. **Escort-labor savings are real but not the headline, and no IAH-specific figure is public.** A visitor pass converts some *airport-hosted* escorted access (tours, community events, meet-and-greet) from badged-escort supervision into self-screened unescorted access. But the largest category of non-ticketed access today — airline gate passes for unaccompanied minors and passengers needing assistance — is airline-run, not airport labor, so the airport's escort savings are bounded. Any quantified "avoided escort hours" claim must be built from Houston's own event/tour logs, which are not in the public record.

8. **Houston is two very different operational problems, not one.** IAH: 48.45M passengers, nine checkpoints across five terminals, heavy international/connecting complexity. HOU: 14.6M passengers, a single 24-hour terminal with two concourses. HOU's simpler, single-terminal geometry is the far easier and lower-risk pilot candidate; IAH's connecting banks and construction footprint are the harder case [Source: https://roadgenius.com/statistics/airports/usa/george-bush-iah-airport/] [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/press-releases/item/houston-airports-shatters-passenger-record-with-631-million-travelers-in-2024/] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Hobby_Airport].

## 2. Evidence

### Benchmark program mechanics (the operational template)

The six programs relevant to this run converge on a common architecture: pre-registration or same-day issuance, TSA screening identical to ticketed passengers, a hard daily cap, defined hours, and a visit-duration ceiling.

- **SEA Visitor Pass (permanent):** 300 passes/day, first-come-first-served, **Checkpoint 4 only**, 5 a.m.–10 p.m. seven days, last entry 9 p.m., **4-hour visit limit** measured from first screening, apply same-day or up to 7 days out. No dedicated or expedited lane — visitors use standard lines and cannot use PreCheck or CLEAR. The FAQ's own congestion advice: "at peak travel times, we recommend two or more hours before you want to be post-security" [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs].
- **DTW Destination Pass (permanent):** **75 passes/day**, 4 a.m.–9 p.m. daily, fully automated via **Departures-level kiosks**, same-day use, both McNamara and Evans terminals [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs].
- **PHL Wingmate:** **100 passes/day**, 4 a.m.–10 p.m. (entry to 9 p.m.), **no same-day** — apply 1–7 days in advance, digital pass accepted at five checkpoints (Terminals A-West, A-East, B, D/E, F; **Terminal C excluded**), 6-hour visit limit. "More than 10,300 people" cumulative since November 1, 2023 [Source: https://www.phl.org/wingmate/terms] [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/phls-free-wingmate-guest-pass-program-now-even-easier-to-use-digital-passes-accepted-at-five-philadelphia-international-airport-checkpoints/].
- **MSY Guest Pass:** **50 weekday / 100 weekend**, 11 a.m.–8 p.m., register ≥24 hours in advance, pick up at the **staffed Customer Service Desk** on the baggage-claim level, guests 13+ need a pass [Source: https://flymsy.com/msy-guest-pass/].
- **TPA All Access (relaunched Nov 2025):** **6 guests per 2-hour block per airside**, pickup 9 a.m.–7 p.m., advance online registration. TPA states the cap is "to help reduce traffic in the trams and airsides" — an explicit throughput-protection rationale [Source: https://stuckattheairport.com/2020/01/13/tampa-intl-airport-expands-gate-pass-program/] [Source: https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2025/11/03/no-boarding-pass--you-can-now-visit-the-airside-terminals-at-tampa-international-airport-].
- **myPITpass (pioneer, Sept 5, 2017; suspended):** weekdays 9 a.m.–5 p.m., no advance sign-up, valid day-of only; the program reserved the right to halt or limit issuance "if the program caused congestion." Ended 2020 for COVID and never restarted because the airport entered terminal construction [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/pittsburgh-intl-opens-airside-areas-non-ticketed-visitors/].

### The throughput arithmetic

The airport-wide load is negligible; the localized load is the whole risk. IAH handled **48.45 million passengers in 2024** across nine checkpoints [Source: https://roadgenius.com/statistics/airports/usa/george-bush-iah-airport/]. A 100/day cap (PHL-style) is ~36,500 entries/year, or **0.075%** of IAH throughput. Even SEA's more generous 300/day ceiling is ~109,500/year — well under one-quarter of one percent of a large hub's traffic. No program threatens annual capacity.

What the cap protects is the peak *hour* at a *single* checkpoint. A standard TSA lane clears roughly 150–250 passengers/hour; a checkpoint already sitting at 30–45 minute waits has no slack to donate. That is why SEA corrals its entire program to Checkpoint 4 and why TPA meters six-person blocks: the design goal is to keep visitor arrivals in the trough between departure banks, never on top of one. IAH's documented peak waits of 30–45 minutes (up to ~60 on early Sunday) mean a Houston program must **blackout the morning banks** and confine visitors to designated checkpoints and midday/evening windows [Source: https://www.ifly.com/airports/george-bush-intercontinental-airport/wait-times].

### Staffing and workload

TSA bears the screening cost. The airport's recurring operational burden is (a) application intake and identity verification, (b) pass issuance, (c) wayfinding and visitor management inside the sterile area, and (d) incident and IROPS accountability. The staffing model is the single biggest lever on recurring cost: DTW's kiosk automation and SEA/PHL's digital passes push near-zero marginal labor per visitor, while MSY's staffed Customer Service Desk adds a fixed labor line every operating hour [Source: https://www.metroairport.com/about-us/dtw-destination-pass/faqs] [Source: https://flymsy.com/msy-guest-pass/]. For a system the size of Houston's, an automated/digital issuance model is the only defensible choice on workload grounds.

### Realized demand

PHL's "more than 10,300" cumulative visitors since November 1, 2023 is the most useful public uptake figure [Source: https://www.internationalairportreview.com/news/256744/over-10300-people-use-philadelphia-international-airports-wingmate-guest-pass-since-launch/]. Against a 100/day cap (36,500/year theoretical), this implies realized daily averages in the low tens — the program routinely runs far below its ceiling. Operationally this is reassuring on congestion and sobering on benefits: the concession and community upside is proportional to a small, self-selecting flow, not a terminal full of new foot traffic.

### Fragility under stress

The program class has a consistent failure mode: it is discretionary and gets suspended first. COVID suspended nearly all of them; Orlando relaunched only on **December 29, 2025** "after a pause to sort out staffing and operations"; SEA's system went offline in a 2024 cyberattack; and Pittsburgh's original program never came back because the terminal entered construction [Source: https://deeparrival.com/news/airport-guest-pass-non-flyers-2026/] [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/pittsburgh-intl-opens-airside-areas-non-ticketed-visitors/]. This is directly load-bearing for Houston: IAH is in the middle of a major terminal redevelopment, and the Pittsburgh precedent is a warning that a visitor program and active terminal construction do not coexist well.

## 3. Counterexamples — where operations cannot solve the problem

Intellectual honesty requires naming the cases where scheduling, technology, and process design run out of road and the constraint is genuinely physical or categorical.

1. **Peak-hour checkpoint capacity is a hard physical limit.** During the morning departure bank, a constrained IAH checkpoint at 45–60 minute waits has no throughput to give. No coordination trick manufactures lane-hours that don't exist. The only "operational" answer is rationing — a hard cap plus blackout windows — which is rationing, not optimization. If the program's value proposition depends on morning access, operations cannot deliver it; the honest answer is "not at that checkpoint, not at that hour."

2. **Active terminal construction compresses the very space the program consumes.** IAH's redevelopment reduces available sterile-area square footage and circulation width in phases. Adding non-ticketed bodies to a construction-compressed concourse is the exact condition that kept Pittsburgh's program shut permanently [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/pittsburgh-intl-opens-airside-areas-non-ticketed-visitors/]. This is a physical-footprint constraint, not a process problem — the right answer during peak construction phases may be "wait," or "HOU first."

3. **Emergency and irregular-operations accountability is a known-population problem.** In a ground stop, evacuation, or medical/security event, every body in the sterile area must be accounted for and moved. Ticketed passengers exist on manifests; visitors do not. A daily cap contains this population but does not eliminate the accountability gap, and no scheduling design makes an unticketed visitor appear on a flight manifest. This is a categorical limit that public-safety and emergency-management colleagues should size independently.

4. **Distribution across checkpoints cannot be fully "operationalized away."** SEA's decision to confine its entire program to one checkpoint is an admission that visitor flow cannot be spread across all lanes without losing control of it. Concentration protects the rest of the airfield but turns the chosen checkpoint into the binding constraint — a trade the operator must accept, not engineer around [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs].

## 4. Data points and quotes a strategist can use verbatim

1. **The cap is the design.** SEA runs "300 passes allotted per day," available at Checkpoint 4 only, with a strict 4-hour visit limit — the reference template for a large-hub program [Source: https://www.portseattle.org/SEAvisitorpassFAQs].

2. **Airports say out loud that caps exist to protect throughput.** TPA limits access to "only six guests… per two-hour block in each airside," explicitly "to help reduce traffic in the trams and airsides" [Source: https://stuckattheairport.com/2020/01/13/tampa-intl-airport-expands-gate-pass-program/].

3. **Demand runs modest.** PHL logged "more than 10,300" Wingmate visitors cumulatively since its November 1, 2023 launch — implying daily averages in the low tens against a 100/day cap [Source: https://www.internationalairportreview.com/news/256744/over-10300-people-use-philadelphia-international-airports-wingmate-guest-pass-since-launch/].

4. **These programs are fragile and get cut first.** Orlando's Experience MCO pass relaunched December 29, 2025 only "after a pause to sort out staffing and operations," and Pittsburgh's pioneering program "ended in 2020 due to Covid-19 and didn't restart… because the airport was undergoing construction on a new terminal" [Source: https://deeparrival.com/news/airport-guest-pass-non-flyers-2026/] [Source: https://airportimprovement.com/article/pittsburgh-intl-opens-airside-areas-non-ticketed-visitors/].

5. **Scale check for Houston.** IAH handled 48.45 million passengers across nine checkpoints in 2024; HOU handled 14.6 million through a single terminal; the two-airport system set a record at 63.1 million. A 100/day visitor cap is 0.075% of IAH's annual throughput — invisible in aggregate, decisive only at the chosen checkpoint during the peak hour [Source: https://roadgenius.com/statistics/airports/usa/george-bush-iah-airport/] [Source: https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/press-releases/item/houston-airports-shatters-passenger-record-with-631-million-travelers-in-2024/] [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Hobby_Airport].

## Operational recommendation (for the Strategist to weigh)

If Houston proceeds, the operations-defensible design writes itself from the benchmark record:

- **Pilot at HOU first,** not IAH. Single terminal, simpler geometry, no active large-terminal construction footprint of IAH's scale, and a cleaner accountability boundary. Prove the operating model where the downside is smallest.
- **Automated/digital issuance** (DTW/PHL/SEA model), not a staffed desk — recurring labor is the cost that compounds.
- **One or two designated checkpoints, never all of them** — SEA's single-checkpoint concentration is the control mechanism.
- **Hard daily cap in the 50–100 range** with real-time remaining-count display, and the explicit right to halt issuance under congestion (myPITpass's original safeguard).
- **Blackout the departure banks.** Midday-to-evening windows only; no early-morning access. MSY's 11 a.m. start is the model.
- **Sequence around construction.** Do not layer a visitor program onto IAH terminals in active redevelopment phases; Pittsburgh is the cautionary precedent.

**Honest gaps in this brief:** No public figure exists for IAH/HOU current escorted-access or event-escort labor hours, so the "avoided escort hours" benefit cannot be quantified from open sources and must be built from Houston's own operations logs. Per-lane throughput figures used above (150–250 pax/hour) are industry-standard planning ranges, not IAH-measured values; a Houston decision should use the airport's own checkpoint throughput data by hour and checkpoint before setting caps and blackout windows.
