# Red Team critique — Revised draft A (revisions/v1)

**Scope of this review.** The reader feedback contained one substantive point: the program's core potential saving is redirecting event-related visitors — inaugural flights and the like — through the pass instead of burning operations staff time on escorts. This critique evaluates (A) whether revised-draft-a.md actually addressed that point, and (B) what the revision broke or weakened in the process. The revision touched seven locations: Executive Summary point 8, the "Demand is modest" section (three rebuilt paragraphs), the counter-case escort paragraph, the concessions list, the Director's "strongest case for," the eligibility bullet, and footnote [^33].

---

## Part A — Did the revision address the feedback?

**1. Location:** Whole document, all seven touch points.
**Issue:** Verdict on responsiveness: yes, and more thoroughly than the one-line feedback strictly required. The prior draft dismissed escort savings outright ("close to fictional," "not an operational cost driver"); the revision splits the ledger into a non-converting half (unaccompanied minors, reduced-mobility, badged employees — airline-run or SIDA-controlled) and a converting half (inaugural delegations, route launches, USO send-offs, sanctioned tours), and carries the split consistently through the exec summary, the argument, the counter-case, the concessions, and the recommendation. The split itself is legitimate: the Airport COO brief (item 3, "there is real, discrete escort labor to reclaim") and the Director of Public Safety brief ("Escort-hour recovery is real officer time... Authorized events, VIP meets... currently burn HPD and airport-staff hours") both support the converting half. The prior draft under-used those two briefs; the reader caught what this Red Team should have caught in Stage 2.
**Recommendation:** Accept the structural response as adequate. The problems are in execution, below.

**2. Location:** "Demand is modest," second and third paragraphs (revised); concessions list.
**Issue:** The revision also correctly preserved the Contrarian's discipline. The Contrarian brief demanded the Strategist "show the numbers or drop the operational-ROI framing," and the revision complies by keeping the saving qualitative — "it sizes a benefit, it does not balance a budget" — and by updating the concession from "largely fictional" to a two-part concession that matches the new argument. No internal inconsistency between the concession list and the claims it concedes. This is done right.
**Recommendation:** None. Noted so the Strategist knows which parts to leave alone in the next pass.

**3. Location:** Eligibility bullet, Director's recommendation.
**Issue:** The feedback's operational point is now reflected in the program design itself — "inaugural-flight and route-launch delegations your staff escort by hand today" was added to eligibility, which is the correct place for it, not just the rhetoric. The feedback asked for a savings argument; the revision converted it into a design feature. Good instinct.
**Recommendation:** Keep, subject to items 5 and 6 below, which show the design feature and the site selection now fight each other.

**4. Location:** Footnote [^33].
**Issue:** The footnote was rewritten to carry the new claim, and it honestly discloses that "no IAH/HOU-specific escort-hour figure is in the public record." That disclosure is necessary and present. But see item 7: the footnote's sourcing label is now doing work it cannot support.
**Recommendation:** See item 7.

## Part B — What the revision broke or weakened

**5. Location:** Eligibility bullet ("Capture most of the goodwill — and the one real escort-labor saving — at a fraction of the volume and risk") and Director's "strongest case for." **The Hobby/Bush mismatch — the most serious new break.**
**Issue:** The escort-labor saving the revision now touts lives mostly at Bush. Inaugural international flights, route-launch ribbon-cuttings with consuls and mayoral deputies, the civic events "an airport of Houston's civic weight hosts" — that is Bush's calendar. Hobby is 93-plus percent Southwest running domestic and near-international point-to-point; its route-launch and dignitary-event tempo is a fraction of Bush's. The report recommends a Hobby-only pilot and then claims that pilot captures "the one real escort-labor saving." It cannot. The pilot can prototype the *mechanism*; it will realize almost none of the *hours*, because the hours are at the airport the report explicitly excludes. The prior draft did not have this problem because it did not make the claim. The revision introduced a benefit whose geography contradicts its own site selection and never acknowledges the tension — in a document that is otherwise obsessive about acknowledging tensions (it flags its own Hobby-construction inconsistency, its own Hobby-callout-rate inconsistency).
**Recommendation:** Say it straight, in the site-selection discussion or the eligibility bullet: the event-escort saving concentrates at Bush; the Hobby pilot proves the conversion mechanism at low volume, and the saving is realized only if the program later expands to Bush on the evidence the pilot generates. This actually *strengthens* the "bank the argument" logic in the Pittsburgh section — the escort saving becomes a second reason a Bush expansion might eventually clear the bar. The revision left that connection on the table.

**6. Location:** "Demand is modest," third paragraph ("hours returned to operations and to patrol"); Director's pitch ("real hours returned to operations and patrol"); versus the counter-case ("staffing to support it cannot [launch in 90 days]") and the 90-day list (HPD/HFD staffing understanding as precondition).
**Issue:** The revision now claims the program *returns* HPD patrol hours while the rest of the document argues the program *adds* law-enforcement burden severe enough to require a written staffing understanding before launch and to implicate the 1542.217 adequacy determination. Both can be true — escort hours saved on event days, coverage hours added on ordinary days — but the draft asserts both without ever netting them or even noting they point in opposite directions at the same department. A skeptical HPD commander reads "hours returned to patrol" as a claim on his ledger and will ask for the arithmetic the report admits does not exist.
**Recommendation:** One sentence of reconciliation where the patrol claim first appears: the saving is episodic event relief; the cost is standing coverage; the net is unknown and is precisely what the written HPD/HFD understanding must price. Do not let "returned to patrol" stand unqualified in the Director's pitch.

**7. Location:** Footnote [^33] and the paragraph it supports ("staged an event: dignitaries, a consul or a mayor's deputy, local press, a water-cannon salute... escorted today by airport operations staff, sometimes with an HPD officer attached").
**Issue:** Sourcing sleight of hand. The footnote label — "Structural operational analysis" — presents the claim as analyst work product, but the inaugural-flight specifics appear in no Stage 1 brief (I checked: zero hits for "inaugural," "route launch," "water-cannon," or "delegation" across all eight briefs). The COO and DPS briefs support "sanctioned events and VIP meets burn escort hours" generically; the vivid inaugural-flight scene came from the reader — an airport operations executive relaying firsthand knowledge. That is a *better* source than a web brief, but the document hides it. A fact-checker matching [^33] against the briefs will find the specifics unsupported, and this revision was made *after* the Stage 3 fact-check, so nothing has audited these sentences.
**Recommendation:** Amend [^33] to state the actual basis: peer-brief support for the general category (COO brief item 3; DPS brief escort-hour finding) plus direct operator input on the Houston event-escort pattern. And run the fact-checker over the revision's new material before this version ships — it currently carries the only never-fact-checked paragraphs in the document.

**8. Location:** Footnote [^33] versus the Director of Public Safety brief.
**Issue:** A quiet overruling of a brief cited for support. The DPS brief lists "unaccompanied-minor / special-assistance escorts" among the *recoverable* categories; the revised [^33] and the argument text put them firmly in the *non-converting* half. The draft's position is the correct one — those are airline-run — but the document is now leaning on the DPS brief's escort-hour finding while silently rejecting half of that same finding. If the DPS chair reads the final report, he will notice.
**Recommendation:** One clause acknowledging the adjudication: the DPS view that special-assistance escorts convert is overruled because those escorts are the airline's program, not the airport's.

**9. Location:** "Why the counter-case is insufficient" → security paragraph, and the conversion claim generally ("a managed, auditable, self-service channel").
**Issue:** "Self-service" overreaches for the exact examples chosen. A consul at a route launch will be escorted regardless — that is protocol, not process failure. Local press shooting a water-cannon salute needs ramp access, which is SIDA territory the report's own definition (first section: the pass "is not escorted access... emphatically not" ramp-side) says the pass can never grant. So for the marquee example — the inaugural — the pass converts *some* delegation members (community guests, families, USO parties) and leaves the escort-intensive core (dignitaries by protocol, press by SIDA) exactly where it is. The saving is real but thinner than "converts that ad-hoc escorting into a managed self-service channel" implies.
**Recommendation:** Downgrade the verb. The pass *thins* the escorted party, it does not eliminate it: guests and families flow through the pass channel; protocol and ramp escorts remain. The claim survives at the right size and becomes harder to attack.

**10. Location:** "Demand is modest," third paragraph, closing sentences ("the tell that it is real is that the eligibility this report recommends... is precisely the list of activities that burn escort hours today").
**Issue:** Circular reasoning dressed as corroboration. The eligibility list was written (in this very revision) to match the escort-burning activities; that the two lists match is authorship, not evidence. The prior draft's version of the recommendation contained no inaugural/route-launch eligibility — it was added in the same edit that added the savings argument. Presenting the match as a "tell" invites the first skeptical reader to check the version history, and the version history convicts.
**Recommendation:** Cut "the tell that it is real is..." and replace with the honest causal direction: the eligibility list was *chosen* to target the activities that burn escort hours, which is why the narrow program is also the one aimed at the savings that exist. Same conclusion, no circle.

**11. Location:** "Demand is modest," second paragraph ("the skepticism the counter-case brings to the 'avoided escort labor' line is earned against this half of the ledger").
**Issue:** Forward reference to a section the reader has not reached. "The counter-case" is introduced as a named structure four sections later; here it arrives without antecedent and reads as an editing seam — visible evidence that this paragraph was patched in response to feedback rather than written into the argument.
**Recommendation:** Drop the reference: "and skepticism about the 'avoided escort labor' line is earned against this half of the ledger." The counter-case section already makes its own version of the point; it does not need advance billing.

**12. Location:** Executive summary, point 8, added final sentence.
**Issue:** The new sentence runs roughly 80 words through two em-dash pivots and a four-item list before landing — the longest sentence in the executive summary by a wide margin, in the one section built for a reader who will read nothing else. It also smuggles the entire nuanced argument (converts, self-service, hours returned, does-not-shrink-with-volume) into a summary bullet, where it cannot carry the caveats the body attaches to it.
**Recommendation:** Two sentences, one caveat: the route-launch delegations, USO send-offs, and sanctioned tours that operations staff escort by hand today convert into a managed pass channel — the one saving that does not shrink with walk-up volume, though it is episodic and unquantified.

**13. Location:** Repetition across the document.
**Issue:** The list "inaugural-flight delegations / route-launch events / USO send-offs / sanctioned tours" now appears in substantially identical form in seven places: exec summary point 8, twice in "Demand is modest," the counter-case, the concessions list, the Director's case-for, and the eligibility bullet. Likewise "the one saving/benefit that does not shrink with (walk-up) volume" appears three times verbatim-adjacent. One feedback point generated seven echoes; by the fourth occurrence the reader hears the patch, not the point.
**Recommendation:** Full treatment once ("Demand is modest"), design once (eligibility bullet), and compress the other five to a clause or a cross-reference. The exec summary and Director's pitch may each keep one short mention; the counter-case and concessions need only the concession.

**14. Location:** "Demand is modest," first paragraph, final sentence ("The escort-hour savings are the exception, and they deserve to be pulled out of that sentence rather than buried in it").
**Issue:** Writerly self-commentary — the document narrating its own paragraph structure. This is the third such meta-move in the vicinity ("Watch what happens," "Say it plainly," now this), and this one is purely about the draft's own sentences. It is the tell of a revision responding to feedback rather than an essay that always knew this.
**Recommendation:** Delete the sentence. End the paragraph at "self-selecting trickle — with one exception," and let the next paragraph be the exception.

**15. Location:** Document-wide, verification status.
**Issue:** Everything else in this draft survived a Stage 2 red team, a Stage 3 edit, a humanizer pass, and a fact-check. The revision's new material — roughly 400 words across seven locations, including a new factual scene about Houston event operations and a rewritten footnote — has survived nothing. It is currently the least-tested prose in the most-read sections (exec summary, Director's pitch).
**Recommendation:** After the Strategist addresses items 5–14, run the fact-checker over the delta at minimum before this version is packaged.

---

## Verdict

The revision did what the feedback asked, sourced the general claim more honestly than it first appears (the COO and DPS briefs were always there to support it), and kept the concession structure coherent. But it bolted a Bush-shaped benefit onto a Hobby-shaped recommendation without noticing (item 5), claimed hours returned to a police department the same document says must be paid more (item 6), and dressed operator testimony as analysis (item 7). Items 5–7 must be fixed before this version ships; items 8–14 are a half-day of Strategist work; item 15 is process hygiene. None of it requires retreating from the reader's point — the escort argument is real. It just has to be told at its true size, in its true place, with its true source.
