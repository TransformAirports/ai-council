# Procurement & Contracts Brief — Data Centers at Airports: Airside Risks

**Author's chair:** Senior procurement and contracts officer, top-tier US airport authority
**Thesis under test:** Data centers near approach/departure paths should pass an independent-evidence test for compatibility with aircraft operations and preservation of future airfield capacity — not merely clear current obstruction surfaces.
**My lens:** A safety thesis becomes real only when it survives the instrument that actually authorizes the building. For a data center on airport land, that instrument is not a construction procurement — it is a **land transaction** (sale or ground lease), gated by FAA approval and grant-assurance flow-downs. The contract is what happens; the compatibility study is what you *hope* happens. If the operating restrictions are not recorded in the deed or lease, they do not exist.

---

## 1. Key findings

1. **A data center near a runway is bought and sold as real estate, not as construction — and MWAA has already done it once.** In 2018 the Airports Authority sold 424 acres of Dulles "Western Lands" to Digital Realty for **$236.5 million**; the land had been assembled in 2005–2007 to build a fourth runway and support facilities [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-announces-sale-424-acres-western-lands-dulles-international]. The precedent that governs this thesis is a fee-simple land sale, and a sale is the *weakest* instrument for enforcing operating restrictions over a 30-year asset life.

2. **The federal test the thesis wants already exists in law, but it triggers on FAA consent, not on the airport's own review.** Under 49 U.S.C. § 47107 grant assurances, federally acquired/conveyed airport land stays in airport use until the FAA approves or consents to a change; the FAA's January 8, 2024 land-use policy limits releases to actual sale/disposal and conditions consent on "current and future aeronautical need" and impact on "safe and efficient operation of aircraft" [Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/08/2023-27017/policy-regarding-processing-land-use-changes-on-federally-acquired-or-federally-conveyed-airport]. The independent-evidence test is a federal gate; the airport's job is to build the record that gate demands.

3. **Fair market value is mandatory, and it works against the safety thesis.** Grant assurances require non-aeronautical land to be leased/sold at FMV per an FAA-standard appraisal (CGL 2018-03) [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/airport_compliance/compliance_guidance/cgl-2018-03-appraisal-standards]. The pressure to capture Loudoun-County data-center land value ($236.5M for 424 acres) is precisely the pressure that shortens compatibility review. Name the trade: revenue maximization and airside-conservatism pull in opposite directions on the same parcel.

4. **A land sale forfeits the airport's pen; a ground lease keeps it.** Once title transfers fee-simple, operating covenants (generator-test windows, plume monitoring, lighting spec, wildlife management) survive only as recorded deed restrictions or easements — hard to monitor, harder to claw back. A long-term ground lease with reopeners and a capacity-reservation clause keeps enforcement, reversion, and future-runway optionality in the airport's hands. This is the single most consequential procurement decision in the thesis.

5. **DBE and Buy America are dormant until an AIP dollar touches the deal — then they wake up.** A pure land sale carries no 2 CFR 200 procurement flow-down. But if the airport funds the compatibility studies, access roads, or utility relocations with AIP grant money, Brooks Act QBS, Davis-Bacon, Buy America Build America, and 49 CFR Part 26 DBE goals all attach [Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/47107]. The thesis should decide *up front* whether the enabling infrastructure is grant-funded, because that choice binds the procurement toolkit.

6. **The realistic date from board direction to a defensible, FAA-consented instrument is 18–30 months, not the 6–12 months a real-estate pro forma assumes.** Appraisal + compatibility studies (aeronautical study, wildlife hazard assessment, thermal-plume CFD, EMI) run 9–15 months; FAA land-use consent, ALP update, and Part 77 airspace determination run 6–12 months (partly concurrent); NEPA on new ground disturbance adds 6–12 months. No developer notice-to-proceed should precede the FAA airspace determination.

7. **The audit trail the thesis needs is the audit trail the DOT OIG already grades.** OIG has repeatedly cited airports for below-FMV non-aeronautical leases and weak documentation — LAWA left up to ~$558,000 on the table on land leases FAA could not show met FMV [Source: https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/FAA%20Oversight%20of%20LAX%20Revenue%20Use%20Inadequate.pdf]. A data-center deal that skips the independent compatibility record produces exactly the finding pattern OIG audits are built to catch.

## 2. Evidence by sub-question

### A. The instrument: this is a real-estate disposition, not a CMAR/Design-Build decision

The thesis is framed as an operational-risk review, but procurement discipline asks a prior question: *what document authorizes the building, and who signs it?* For a data center on airport property, the authorizing instrument is a **land disposition** — either a fee-simple sale or a long-term ground lease — executed by the airport's real-estate function, not its construction-procurement function. The developer builds under its own private contracts; the airport is a landlord/grantor, not an owner-builder. That inversion changes everything about leverage:

- In a construction contract, the airport writes the spec and holds retention, liquidated damages, and bonds.
- In a land disposition, the airport's only durable leverage is what it records in the deed or writes into the lease **before** it signs. After closing, a fee-simple owner is largely free of the airport's operational preferences except where a recorded covenant or easement binds the land.

MWAA's own precedent is a **sale**: the Western Lands went to Digital Realty in fee simple for $236.5M, with the press release promising only that "conditions of the transaction ensure that the land will be used for airport-compatible purposes and will comply with current zoning" [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-announces-sale-424-acres-western-lands-dulles-international]. "Airport-compatible" and "current zoning" are not the same as an enforceable, monitored covenant governing generator plumes over a departure corridor. The Digital Dulles campus has since grown to a planned 14 buildings, ~11.7M sq ft, and up to 1 GW across six substations [Source: https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/cloud/article/11429999/dulles-land-buy-gives-digital-realty-runway-for-data-center-expansion] — a scale whose cooling and standby-generation footprint dwarfs what any 2018 land-sale condition contemplated.

### B. The federal land/funding stack and what each dollar binds

1. **Grant assurances (49 U.S.C. § 47107).** By accepting AIP grants, the airport promises FAA that airport land serves airport purposes; Assurance 5 (preserving rights and powers), 19 (operation and maintenance), 21 (compatible land use), 22 (economic nondiscrimination), 25 (airport revenues), 29 (airport layout plan), and 31 (disposal of land) all bear on a data-center parcel [Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/47107]. Assurance 29 means the parcel and its use must appear on an **FAA-approved Airport Layout Plan** before development — the ALP update is itself a gate.

2. **FAA land-use-change consent (effective Jan 8, 2024).** The FAA consents to non-aeronautical use only after weighing "current and future aeronautical need" and impact on safe/efficient aircraft operations, and generally releases federal obligations only on actual sale [Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/08/2023-27017/policy-regarding-processing-land-use-changes-on-federally-acquired-or-federally-conveyed-airport]. This is where the thesis's independent-evidence test is legally *cashed*: the airport must document compatibility to earn FAA consent.

3. **Revenue Use Policy + FMV (64 FR 7696; 49 U.S.C. § 47133).** Non-aeronautical land must transact at fair market value on an FAA-standard appraisal (CGL 2018-03), and proceeds must be used for the airport [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/airport_compliance/compliance_guidance/cgl-2018-03-appraisal-standards]. For MWAA specifically, land-sale revenue at Dulles must be spent on aeronautical purposes at Dulles and cannot be diverted to other business lines — a constraint that flows from MWAA's federal lease and the Virginia–DC interstate compact directing the Authority "solely to operate and improve" the two airports [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Washington_Airports_Authority].

4. **Airspace/obstruction review (14 CFR Part 77; FAA Form 7460-1).** Even a compliant land use requires an aeronautical study of structures, and — critically for this thesis — the Part 77 obstruction-surface review does *not* evaluate thermal plume, exhaust, lighting, or EMI. Those risks fall outside the standard 7460 determination, which is exactly the gap the thesis identifies. The airport must commission supplemental studies because the federal form will not.

5. **BABA / Davis-Bacon / Brooks Act / DBE — conditional.** None attach to a pure land sale. All attach if AIP or BIL/IIJA dollars fund the compatibility studies, enabling infrastructure, or utility work. That is a live choice at Dulles, where DOT's December 2025 RFI to "revitalize" the airport explicitly opens both aeronautical and "non-aeronautical land development opportunities" and routes engagement through federal channels [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/iad-rfi].

### C. Transaction method as a strategic decision (the delivery-method analog)

The delivery-method equivalent here is **sale vs. long-term ground lease vs. developer RFP**, and the federal rules narrow the toolkit less than they *shape* it:

- **Fee-simple sale** (the Digital Realty model): maximizes upfront cash, triggers a full Assurance 31 release, and — decisively — **surrenders the airport's ongoing operational leverage**. Appropriate only where the airport has already permanently written off the parcel for aeronautical use.
- **Long-term ground lease** (40–50 year terms are standard for airport development): keeps reversion, keeps the airport as landlord with enforcement rights, and lets compatibility covenants live in the lease with monitoring and cure provisions. FAA consent runs concurrent with the lease term and the land reverts to aeronautical use at expiry [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/resources/publications/orders/compliance_5190_6/order-5190-6c-compliance-chapter-12]. **For a use whose airside risk is still being characterized, the lease is the correct vehicle** — it preserves the ability to tighten operating restrictions at renewal and to reclaim land if future airfield capacity demands it.
- **Competitive developer RFP with land disposition**: the defensible path when multiple bidders exist. It builds the procurement-integrity record (documented FMV, open competition) that defeats both protests and OIG findings. An unsolicited single-developer proposal — the way many data-center deals actually arrive — should be converted to a competitive solicitation or supported by a rigorous sole-source justification, or it becomes the audit headline.

The strategic point for the Strategist: MWAA reached for the fastest, highest-cash instrument (a sale) on the Western Lands. A thesis that wants an independent-evidence test and preserved airfield capacity is implicitly arguing for the *slower, lower-cash instrument* (a covenant-heavy ground lease). Name that cost.

### D. The studies MWAA must require — and how to contract them

The screening framework the run asks for is, in procurement terms, a set of **procurement gates**, each tied to a study the airport pays for and controls (never the developer alone, whose CFD model will find compatibility):

| Gate | Study | Contracting vehicle |
|---|---|---|
| Obstruction | Part 77 / Form 7460-1 aeronautical study | FAA process; airport monitors |
| Thermal/aerodynamic | Independent CFD plume & turbulence study over approach/departure corridors | Specialized A/E — Brooks Act QBS if AIP-funded |
| Wildlife | Wildlife Hazard Assessment per AC 150/5200-33B (stormwater ponds attract birds) | Qualified wildlife biologist, on-call IDIQ |
| Lighting/EMI | Photometric + electromagnetic interference study vs. NAVAIDs/ILS | Specialty engineer, likely sole-source expertise |
| Environmental | NEPA (CatEx vs. EA) for new ground disturbance | A/E QBS; FAA is lead agency |

Two procurement notes. First, these are **qualifications-based selections**, not low-bid — the Brooks Act (40 U.S.C. ch. 11) governs A/E selection on federally funded scopes, and the airport should not sealed-bid a plume study to the cheapest modeler. Second, the airport should fund and own these studies **or** contractually require developer-funded studies performed by an airport-selected, airport-directed consultant, so the independent-evidence standard is real and not developer-marked homework.

### E. Contract structure — the two or three terms MWAA must hold the pen on

If the thesis produces a ground lease, three clauses determine whether the safety argument survives to operation:

1. **Operating covenants with monitoring and enforcement, baked into the recorded instrument.** Generator-testing windows keyed to wind/operational conditions, continuous plume/thermal monitoring, lighting shut-off obligations, and a wildlife-management plan — each with reporting, inspection rights, cure periods, and step-in remedies. A covenant with no monitoring clause is decorative.

2. **A capacity-reservation / reopener clause protecting future airfield growth.** The Western Lands were literally the fourth-runway land bank; Dulles still carries a future-fifth-runway reservation in its D2 planning [Source: https://www.flydulles.com/d2-projects-future-fifth-runway]. Any instrument near the airfield must reserve the airport's right to reclaim, restrict, or require modification if future capacity or a new compatibility finding demands it — with a pre-agreed valuation mechanism so the reopener is exercisable, not theoretical.

3. **Indemnity and insurance for interference with aircraft operations.** Standard commercial-lease indemnity does not contemplate an operator's exhaust plume causing a go-around or its lighting degrading an ILS. The airport should hold the pen on an aviation-specific indemnity and insurance stack, plus liquidated-damages calibration for defined interference events.

### F. Protest, integrity, and audit posture

- **Protest/integrity risk** concentrates in the *disposition*, not a construction award. The exposures are below-FMV sale, sole-source disposition without justification, and an unsolicited-proposal process that never tested the market. Competitive solicitation plus a documented FMV appraisal is the defense.
- **MWAA's rulebook** governs on top of the federal frame: the Contracting Manual (Fifth Edition, updated to v5.3 effective June 26, 2025) and MWAA's own Office of Inspector General. Board approval is required for concession contracts and leases generating over **$3 million annually** in revenue — a data-center ground lease clears that threshold easily, so this is a board-level action with a board-level record [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/sites/mwaa.com/files/2022-01/Contracting_Manual-Fifth_Edition-01-2022.pdf].
- **Audit posture.** DOT OIG's recurring findings — below-FMV non-aeronautical leases, thin documentation, weak sole-source support — map directly onto data-center land deals [Source: https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/FAA%20Oversight%20of%20LAX%20Revenue%20Use%20Inadequate.pdf; https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/library-items/Final%20Report%20-%20Airport%20Revenue%20Diversion%20-%20AV2024010%20-%20508%20compliant.pdf]. A clean file is: FAA-standard appraisal, competitive or justified disposition, FAA land-use consent on record, ALP updated, and the compatibility studies in the grant/lease file.

## 3. The schedule-honest verdict

A real-estate pro forma will assume a data-center parcel near Dulles can move from board interest to developer notice-to-proceed in **6–12 months**. The procurement-and-approval math does not support that when the parcel sits near approach/departure paths and the airport intends a genuine independent-evidence test:

- Scope, FMV appraisal, and disposition-method decision: **3–6 months**
- Independent compatibility studies (aeronautical, wildlife, plume CFD, EMI): **9–15 months**
- FAA land-use consent + ALP update + Part 77 airspace determination: **6–12 months** (partly concurrent with studies)
- NEPA on new ground disturbance (CatEx best case, EA if plume/wildlife/noise are non-trivial): **6–12 months**
- Competitive disposition/negotiation + board approval (>$3M/yr lease): **3–6 months**

**Realistic defensible instrument execution: 18–30 months from board direction.** Developer NTP should *follow* the FAA airspace determination and land-use consent, not lead it. If the thesis assumes a sub-12-month "just lease the surplus land" timeline, it is assuming away the independent-evidence test it claims to require.

## 4. The trade map — each instrument and its binding obligation

- **AIP grant history on the parcel →** the land is bound by Assurances 5/19/21/29/31; you cannot dispose of it without FAA consent and an ALP update. *Cost: no unilateral speed.*
- **FAA land-use-change consent (2024 policy) →** you must document compatibility and future aeronautical need to the FAA's satisfaction. *Cost: the safety study is a gate, not a formality.*
- **Revenue Use Policy + FMV (64 FR 7696; § 47133) →** you must take fair market value and spend it on the airport. *Cost: revenue maximization pressure works against airside conservatism on the same parcel.*
- **MWAA federal lease + interstate compact →** Dulles land proceeds must serve aeronautical purposes at Dulles; the Authority exists "solely to operate and improve" the airports. *Cost: no diversion of data-center cash to unrelated ventures.*
- **Any AIP/BIL dollar in the enabling work →** Brooks Act QBS, Davis-Bacon, BABA, and 49 CFR Part 26 DBE goals attach to that work. *Cost: the funding choice binds the procurement toolkit before design begins.*
- **Fee-simple sale (Digital Realty model) →** full Assurance 31 release and maximum cash, but the airport loses ongoing operational leverage. *Cost: the compatibility covenants must be perfect at closing because you cannot tighten them later.*
- **Long-term ground lease →** slower, lower upfront cash, but preserves enforcement, reversion, and future-runway optionality. *Cost: revenue timing and complexity, in exchange for keeping the pen.*

## 5. Pull-ready findings (quote verbatim)

1. "A data center near a runway is not a construction project the airport controls — it is a land disposition the airport signs once and lives with for thirty years; if the compatibility restrictions are not recorded in the deed or lease, they are unenforceable aspirations."

2. "MWAA has already run this play: in 2018 it sold 424 acres assembled for a fourth runway to Digital Realty for $236.5 million — the strongest available evidence that airfield land banks get converted to data centers when the revenue is large enough and the airside test is soft enough."

3. "The FAA's Part 77 obstruction review evaluates whether a building pokes through an imaginary surface — it says nothing about thermal plume, exhaust, lighting, or electromagnetic interference, which is precisely why the airport must commission the studies the federal form will never require."

4. "Fair market value is not optional — grant assurances force the airport to take top dollar for non-aeronautical land, which means the same rule that funds the runway also creates the pressure to shortcut the compatibility review that protects it."

5. "The honest procurement timeline from board direction to a defensible, FAA-consented instrument is 18 to 30 months, and no developer should turn a shovel before the FAA airspace determination is signed — any faster schedule is borrowing certainty from a study that hasn't been done."
