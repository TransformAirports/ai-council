## 2. Governance and authority

- **Ownership:** Jointly owned by the Cities of Dallas and Fort Worth.
- **Board:** 12 members. 11 voting seats appointed by the two cities' councils in proportion to ownership: 7 Dallas-appointed, 4 Fort Worth-appointed. A 12th, non-voting seat rotates annually among Irving, Grapevine, Euless, and Coppell.
- **Delegated authority:** The Board may enter contracts without city-council approval. City councils must approve the annual budget, bond sales, and comparable measures.
- **CEO:** Chris McLaughlin, appointed by the Board following a global executive search (DFW newsroom).
- **Board meeting cadence:** First Thursday of each month, 8:30 a.m., 2400 Aviation Drive.

## 3. Financial posture

- **Capital Improvement Program:** $11.3 billion through FY 2030 (per DWU Consulting summary of DFW disclosures). Terminal F is one component of a larger program that includes Terminal C and Terminal A renewals.
- **Debt authorization:** The 70th Supplemental Bond Ordinance authorizes up to $3.0 billion in new debt between March 1, 2025 and February 28, 2026.
- **Ratings:** KBRA rated the 2025A-1 (AMT), 2025A-2 (AMT), and 2025B (Non-AMT) Joint Revenue Refunding and Improvement Bonds AA, Stable (August 2025). Prior published ratings reported: Moody's A1 (positive), S&P AA-, Fitch A+, KBRA AA.
- **Revenue model:** DFW operates under a residual-hybrid Use and Lease Agreement structure with its signatory airlines. Detailed MII (majority-in-interest) thresholds and current cost-per-enplanement figures are not confirmed in the sources reviewed for this packet — flagged as a gap for the airline-commercial-strategist and airport-CEO agents.

## 4. Airline use and lease context

- **Prior agreement (2023):** 10-year Use and Lease Agreement between DFW and American Airlines with preapproved capital investments, including a Terminal F planned as a 15-gate facility at approximately $1.6 billion.
- **Current agreement (May 2025):** DFW and American announced acceleration and expansion of Terminal F to 31 gates at approximately $4 billion, with the Use and Lease Agreement extended through 2043. American is the sole occupant of Terminal F.
- **Hub concentration (2025):** American Airlines carried 82.6% of DFW's passengers (70.8 million of 85.7 million) and operated 82% of departures (secondary sources citing DFW statistics).
- **Connecting share:** Two competing figures are in play. DFW-level secondary reporting places connecting share at about 60%. American's own DFW hub materials describe DFW as the airline's largest connecting complex; the run prompt asserts "two-thirds of American's DFW customers connect." Council agents should note the distinction between airport-level and carrier-level connecting share and cite the version they use.

## 5. Terminal F program facts

- **Scope:** 31 gates, sole American Airlines occupancy, approximately $4 billion.
- **Timeline:** First phase scheduled to be operational in 2027; full program targeted for 2030.
- **Named amenities in the joint announcement:** state-of-the-art amenities; expanded shops and restaurants; public art installations; a new centralized Skylink Station; a walking connection to Terminal D; expanded parking garage designed with built-in curbside circulation; dedicated areas for ticketing, check-in, security screening, and baggage claim.
- **Construction method:** Modular. Gates are being built offsite and installed on site. DFW and secondary sources report approximately 30% cost savings versus conventional construction. The same modular method is being applied to Terminal C and Terminal A renovations, which are adding 14 gates by spring 2026.
- **Gate mix (widebody vs. narrowbody), floor-plate dimensions, and specific lounge/retail square footage inside Terminal F:** Not publicly detailed in the sources reviewed. Flagged as a gap for chief-engineer, operations-analyst, and airline-commercial-strategist agents.

## 6. American Airlines' premium repositioning at DFW (2026 actions)

- **Flagship Check-In:** Announced in Terminal D near the D30 security checkpoint, opening later in 2026. Serves Flagship First / Flagship Business and eligible AAdvantage elites via a separate entrance with dedicated processing.
- **Admirals Club (new, Terminal C):** Approximately 37,000 square feet near the Skylink station — described by American as its largest Admirals Club to date.
- **Terminal F lounge posture (as announced):** A Provisions by Admirals Club grab-and-go concept — a scaled-down offering rather than a Flagship-tier lounge — is planned for Terminal F. This is a live signal about how American currently conceives Terminal F's role in its premium portfolio and is worth weighing against the run prompt's thesis.

## 7. Traffic and operating environment

- **Total passengers (2025):** 85.7 million, a 2.4% decline from the 2024 record of 87.8 million (secondary source, awaiting DFW official statistics confirmation).
- **Peak month (2025):** July at 8.19 million; June at 7.92 million.
- **Global rank:** Reported as the 6th-busiest airport in the world in early 2026 rankings (secondary source).
- **Hub economics (secondary):** A TCU study cited by regional press estimates American's DFW hub supports up to $71.1 billion in economic output. Cited for magnitude only; not primary.

## 8. Regulatory and external dependencies

- **FAA:** Airfield-side and gate-side changes require FAA design-standard concurrence; PFC-funded elements must clear PFC rules; AIP or FAA-Reauthorization discretionary grant use carries its own compliance stack.
- **TSA:** Checkpoint sizing, layout, and CT/AIT deployment are joint TSA/airport decisions and constrain check-in and Flagship Check-In routing.
- **CBP:** Terminal F is planned to grow international operations; FIS (Federal Inspection Services) sizing, staffing model, and CBP consultation are load-bearing on gate mix and passenger flows.
- **Environmental / NEPA:** Terminal F falls within DFW's existing airport-property envelope; NEPA class-of-action status for the expanded scope is not confirmed in this packet.
- **Procurement and labor:** DFW's delivery-method selection for Terminal F, its DBE goals, and the contract structure for the modular-gate manufacturer are not publicly detailed in the sources reviewed. Flagged as a gap.
