## Current argument

Chris,
 
Since we last connected on the proposed data center at IAD, I was copied on a project email allowing me to review the project. This proposal has not been coordinated with the FAA ADO, so I will try to frame my comments from an airport planning and airspace analysis perspective.
 
I recommend that MWAA does not move forward with construction of a data center in the currently proposed location.
 
Airport Planning Concerns:
The proposed data center will be located within the RWY 19R departure and RWY 1L approach surfaces. Even if the project doesn’t penetrate these surfaces, this land should be considered some of the most important and strategic on the airport property. It is prudent to protect this area for both existing and future capacity. We don’t want to preclude our ability to extend the runway or not meet FAA airport design standards per AC 150/5300-13 in the future.
The proposal is located very close to the approach lighting system at the RWY 19R end. Data centers generate bright lights which can interfere with a pilot’s ability to identify the approach light pattern. We also don’t know if there will be impacts to navigational aids, communications, radar or any other electronic interference with FAA equipment.
This proposal is not meet AC 150/5190-4B from a land use compatibility perspective, it is critical to maintain unobstructed space for aircraft to maneuver above ground. Further, limiting atmospheric interference (such as the air turbulence from thermal plumes) near airports is critical to maintaining aircraft control. Data centers are energy intensive sometimes requiring on-site power generation. Airspace can be atmospherically disrupted by thermal plumes emitted from this power.
There is a stormwater retention facility proposed next to the development. These types of facilities create a wildlife attractant within the landing and departure airspace and should be avoided according to FAA AC 150/5200-33.
This area was not designated as non-aeronautical development during the most recent master planning process.
 
Outside of planning, I believe we are at risk of violating the following AIP Grant Assurances as a result of this proposal:
#19 Operation and Maintenance (does not permit activity that would interfere with operations),
#20 Hazard Removal and Mitigation (prevents the establishment of future airport hazards),
#21 Compatible land uses (restricts the use of land adjacent to or in the immediate vicinity of the airport to activities and purposes compatible with normal airport operations, including landing and takeoff of aircraft)
#22 Economic Nondiscrimination (need to make the airport available for aeronautical activities)
#29 Airport Layout Plan (needs to be on an Approved ALP),
 
It appears Planning did review this back in 2025, but it appears to be more of a land use review primarily focused on height and some preliminary environmental considerations. For something to be added to the ALP, it should be considered during the master planning process. If we are outside of that time period, it should be evaluated using the same principles using FAA guidance (ACs, ALP SOP 2.00). Review and approval of the ALP is to ensure we don’t adversely affect the safety, utility, or efficiency of aircraft operations:
 
(A) materially impact the safe and efficient operation of aircraft at, to, or from the airport;
(B) adversely affect the safety of people or property on the ground as a result of aircraft operations; or
(C) adversely affect the value of prior Federal investments to a significant extent.
 
Let me know if you have any questions. Thank you.
