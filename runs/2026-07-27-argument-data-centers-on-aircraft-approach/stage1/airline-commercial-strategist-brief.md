# Airline Commercial Strategist Brief — Data Center in the RWY 19R/1L Surfaces at IAD

**Run:** data-centers-on-aircraft-approach
**Lens:** carrier behavior and hub economics
**Bottom line:** The planning memo is right, and it is *under*-argued. Its case rests on FAA design standards and grant assurances. The stronger case is commercial: IAD is a single-carrier hub whose dominant tenant has already proven it will pull capacity when the airport's cost and constraint profile deteriorates. Sterilizing protected airfield land for a fixed-term data-center lease trades an irreplaceable option on future carrier capacity for a revenue stream MWAA could earn on non-airfield parcels — at the one hub where the carrier has the least tolerance for cost and constraint. Any decision to build here that does not model United's response is incomplete.

---

## 1. Key findings (numerically grounded)

- **IAD is a single-carrier hub.** United operates ~68% of flights; Delta, the next largest, ~4%. IAD set a record 27.25M passengers in 2024. The airport's commercial fate is a United decision, not an MWAA decision. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/washington-dulles-5-largest-airlines-market-share/] [Source: https://www.ffxnow.com/2025/03/03/dulles-airports-2024-passenger-total-set-new-all-time-record/]

- **IAD's structural vulnerability is cost.** It is repeatedly ranked the most expensive major US airport by average fare (~$475–$490 in 2025). Cost per enplanement (CPE) peaked at **$26.55 in 2014**; United called ~$26 "impossible to manage." [Source: https://www.foxnews.com/travel/americas-airport-affordability-gap-cities-where-travel-costs-crushing-families] [Source: https://simpleflying.com/washington-dc-airport-new-records-2024/]

- **The carrier response to that cost is documented, not theoretical.** United cut seats at IAD by nearly **18% from 2010 to 2015** as CPE climbed. This is the closest thing IAD has to a dehubbing near-miss, and it happened within the last decade. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/washington-dc-airport-new-records-2024/]

- **It took a bailout to reverse it.** MWAA restored United growth only via a new airline use agreement plus a **$50M grant from the Commonwealth of Virginia**, which pulled CPE down to roughly **$17**. United's return (Concourse E built for United, ~10 new routes in 2024) is a direct function of that cost relief. [Source: https://airlineweekly.com/2021/07/united-airlines-may-finally-get-a-new-concourse-in-washington/] [Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/tedreed/2025/03/31/dulles-airport-wants-more-cargo-and-united-airlines-is-ready/]

- **The land in question protects a *recent* capital investment.** RWY 1L/19R is the **fourth runway** (9,400 ft), opened **2008**. The data center sits in that runway's approach/departure surfaces. This is not idle acreage — it protects the newest airfield asset MWAA built. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/faa-reviewing-washington-dulles-plan-5th-runway/]

- **The parcel also sits inside a live, FAA-approved growth corridor.** The **fifth runway** has been in Dulles plans since the 1950s, was in the 1985 master plan, received FAA approval alongside the fourth runway in the mid-2000s, and was **reaffirmed by the MWAA Board on July 16, 2025** as part of a build-out toward ~90M annual passengers by the 2080s. MWAA is actively protecting airfield land for capacity it has not yet needed. Committing part of that land to a 30–50 year non-aeronautical lease contradicts a decision the same board made weeks earlier. [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/dulles-master-plan-update-sets-framework-decades-airport-development] [Source: https://virginiamercury.com/2025/07/23/fifth-runway-at-dulles-draws-attention-as-expansion-plans-take-off/]

- **The counter-case is real and must be met head-on:** data-center revenue is precisely the non-aeronautical income that lowers CPE, and CPE is United's single largest grievance at IAD. Loudoun County data centers already fund roughly **one-third of a $3B county budget**. The temptation is not irrational — it is the correct instinct pointed at the wrong parcel. [Source: https://www.city-journal.org/article/loudoun-county-virginia-data-centers-construction]

## 2. The mechanism a strategist should lead with

**Single-carrier hubs concentrate risk in one negotiating counterpart.** With United at ~68% of flights, IAD has almost no ability to backfill lost United capacity with another major. There is no Delta or American hub waiting to absorb slack; Delta is at 4%. When the dominant carrier at a concentrated hub reprices its network, the airport has no diversification to fall back on. That is the structural fact that makes IAD's cost position load-bearing.

**IAD's cost position is already the worst among major US hubs.** The airport competes for United's marginal growth dollar against United's other hubs — Houston (IAH), Chicago (ORD), Newark (EWR), Denver (DEN) — all with materially lower CPE. Carriers benchmark CPE across their own network and divert growth to the cheaper node. IAD sits at the top of the cost table nationally, which means every incremental cost or constraint is evaluated against an already-thin margin of tolerance.

**United has already run the play.** The 2010–2015 18% seat reduction is the tell. It shows the carrier will act on cost at IAD specifically, and it shows the airport cannot self-correct without outside money (the $50M Virginia grant). An airport that needed a state bailout to keep its only hub carrier growing is not an airport with the leverage to spend down strategic airfield land.

**The veto question:** *What does United do if this data center degrades approach reliability or forecloses future airfield capacity?* The honest answer, based on the 2010–2015 record, is that United diverts marginal growth to lower-cost, less-constrained hubs. The data center would have to generate enough net benefit to United to offset that — but United is not a party to the ground lease and captures none of its revenue directly. The revenue lands on MWAA's non-aero line; the operational risk lands on United's schedule. That asymmetry is the problem.

## 3. The land is an option, not a vacant lot

The correct commercial framing of the disputed parcel is **an option on future carrier capacity**, and options are worth the most precisely when the underlying asset is appreciating. United is growing at IAD again — Concourse E, record traffic, new routes. That is exactly when the value of protected airfield land rises, not falls.

A data-center ground lease is long-dated and effectively irreversible: purpose-built facilities with substation-scale power and cooling are not relocated once built. It converts an appreciating capacity option into a fixed rent and forecloses:

- protection of the **existing** fourth-runway (1L/19R) approach and departure surfaces;
- the ability to extend or reconfigure that runway to meet AC 150/5300-13 standards; and
- part of the corridor MWAA is actively protecting for the **fifth runway** and a 90M-passenger build-out.

**Skeptic's objection, answered:** *IAD moves 27M passengers on four runways and 500k+ movements — it will never need the fifth runway, so the land is free.* Two responses. First, the fifth runway is a 2080s-horizon asset; the *fourth runway's* approach surfaces must be protected now regardless of fifth-runway timing. Second, land-banking is cheap insurance against an irreversible 30–50 year commitment. The asymmetry favors patience: if MWAA is wrong to hold the land, it forgoes some lease income; if MWAA is wrong to lease it, it cannot get the capacity back at any price.

## 4. The strongest counter-case, and why it still points away from this site

**Counter-case (state it fairly):** Non-aeronautical revenue is the cleanest tool MWAA has to lower CPE. CPE is what nearly cost IAD its hub. A data-center lease could throw off enough revenue to structurally reduce airline costs, making United *more* likely to stay and grow — arguably doing more for the hub than an unused runway reservation ever will. Given IAD's cost problem and the once-in-a-generation data-center demand on its doorstep, refusing the revenue outright is its own commercial risk.

**Rebuttal:** The benefit is real but **the location is severable from the benefit.** Data-center ground rent is fungible; approach-surface and future-runway land is not. IAD sits on ~13,000 acres. MWAA can capture essentially the same non-aero revenue on parcels that are not inside an active approach surface, not adjacent to the RWY 19R approach lighting system, and not inside the fifth-runway corridor. Siting the facility in the protected airfield surfaces pays for a revenue stream MWAA could earn elsewhere with an irreplaceable capacity option and a set of operational risks a carrier will price:

- **Approach-light and NAVAID interference** near the 19R end degrades approach reliability. At a single-carrier hub, ILS/approach degradation on a primary approach is exactly the kind of schedule-reliability cost the dominant carrier penalizes — reduced weather minimums mean more diversions and cancellations, which United books as a hub-reliability problem.
- **On-site power generation and thermal plumes** are a live risk here specifically: Dominion's transmission into Loudoun is already constrained (PJM flagged emergency build-out in 2022), which makes behind-the-meter generation — and its plume — more likely, not less. [Source: https://www.bayjournal.com/news/energy/energy-demands-for-northern-virginia-data-centers-almost-too-big-to-compute/article_d64ab388-2cb7-11ef-a753-4ffcc056f619.html]
- **The adjacent stormwater pond** is a wildlife attractant in the approach/departure path (AC 150/5200-33) — a bird-strike exposure the carrier bears.

Net: the counter-case argues for *doing the deal on a compatible parcel*, not for doing it in the approach surfaces. That distinction is the whole decision.

## 5. Named case for the writer to cite

**United's 2010–2015 retrenchment at IAD.** As CPE climbed toward its $26.55 (2014) peak, United cut IAD seats ~18%. The hub was carrying disproportionate debt service, and the carrier said the cost was unmanageable. MWAA reversed the decline only with a new airline agreement and a **$50M Commonwealth of Virginia grant** that cut CPE to ~$17, after which United resumed growth (Concourse E built specifically for United; record 2024 traffic). **The lesson for this decision:** IAD's hub is not a birthright — it was nearly lost to cost and constraint within the last 15 years and recovered only with outside money. That is the airport's demonstrated risk tolerance for airfield give-backs: near zero. [Source: https://simpleflying.com/washington-dc-airport-new-records-2024/] [Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/tedreed/2025/03/31/dulles-airport-wants-more-cargo-and-united-airlines-is-ready/]

## 7. Caveats and what would change the conclusion

- **Fare ≠ CPE.** The $475–$490 figures are average *passenger fares*, not CPE (which is an airline unit cost, ~$17 recently). Keep them distinct; use fares only as a proxy for IAD's overall cost reputation, not as the airline cost input.
- **Several CPE data points are from secondary trade/press sources**, not MWAA CAFRs or the airline use-and-lease agreement directly. Confidence on the *direction* (peak ~$26.55, trough ~$17, United retrenchment) is high; exact-dollar precision should be confirmed against MWAA financial statements before a number is quoted as authoritative.
- **The conclusion flips if — and only if — the parcel is genuinely severable-proof.** If MWAA can show no other parcel on ~13,000 acres captures comparable data-center revenue AND that the approach/fifth-runway surfaces are formally released, the trade could be defensible. Nothing in the record suggests that; the 2025 master plan did *not* designate this area as non-aeronautical.
- **The commercial argument reinforces, it does not replace, the FAA/grant-assurance case.** Grant assurances #19, #20, #21, #22, and #29 are the enforceable floor. The carrier-response argument is what makes the case persuasive to a board weighing revenue against risk: the revenue is available elsewhere; the capacity and the carrier are not replaceable.
