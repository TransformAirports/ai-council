# Airport CEO Brief — Data Center in the RWY 19R/1L Surfaces at IAD

*From the chief executive's chair: what this deal costs and what it buys, measured in bond covenants, board votes, the airline use-and-lease, and 30-year option value.*

## The reframe

The planner's memo is right, but it argues on the wrong ledger. It makes a **safety-and-compliance** case (Part 77 surfaces, approach lighting, thermal plume, wildlife attractant, grant assurances). Those are real, and a CEO should bank them. But the memo undersells the argument by treating it as an airfield-engineering question. The stronger case — the one that survives a board vote and a rating-agency call — is that **this parcel is the wrong asset to monetize, at the wrong moment, in the wrong currency.**

MWAA does not own Dulles. It **leases** IAD and DCA from the U.S. Department of Transportation under a federal lease that began in 1987 and now runs to 2100 [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/secretary-transportation-and-airports-authority-sign-lease-extension-operation-washingtons]. That single fact governs everything: the landlord is also the safety regulator and the grant-maker. A data center that triggers an FAA obstruction finding or a grant-assurance dispute is not a permitting nuisance — it is a fight with your landlord, your regulator, and your capital funder simultaneously, three roles the FAA/DOT hold at once. That is why the CEO's answer is *no to this parcel* even though the CEO's answer to "should we earn non-aeronautical data-center revenue" is *yes*.

The clinching context: MWAA is, right now, preparing to unveil a **$20B+ expansion of Dulles to ~90 million passengers — roughly triple today — that federal officials (the Trump administration) have prodded it to accelerate, and that United has concurred with** [Source: https://www.ffxnow.com/2026/07/17/mwaa-almost-ready-to-unveil-dulles-expansion-plan-sought-by-trump/]. Building a data center into the departure/approach surfaces of a runway you may need to lengthen or complement, in the same season you are selling Washington a capacity-expansion story, is a strategic contradiction the board will not want to defend.

## Key findings (board-chair read)

- **This is a landlord problem, not a permitting problem.** IAD sits on a federal lease to 2100; the FAA is landlord, safety regulator, and grant-maker at once [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/secretary-transportation-and-airports-authority-sign-lease-extension-operation-washingtons]. A grant-assurance or obstruction dispute is a fight with all three roles simultaneously.

- **The trade is asymmetric.** Even at Data Center Alley's record land values (~$3M/acre, a Nov-2025 sale at >$6M/acre) [Source: https://www.loudounnow.com/business/data-center-sale-sets-new-loudoun-price-record/article_d392a250-e672-46c2-b2d2-f0ad3424a100.html], a single parcel's proceeds are a rounding error against a $20B+ capital program — yet the airspace and expansion foreclosure it buys is **permanent**.

- **Under a hybrid-residual lease, the airlines capture much of the upside anyway.** IAD is a hybrid-residual airport with an airline revenue-sharing formula [Source: https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/airline-use-agreements]. Non-aero income lowers airline cost — good — but it means **the airport bears 100% of the airspace and political risk to share the reward.**

- **Sequencing is bad.** The United long-term lease last ran through 2024 [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-and-united-airlines-execute-long-term-lease-dulles-international]; the use-and-lease renegotiation window is live now. Handing your dominant hub carrier a grievance over strategic airfield land during a rate negotiation is an unforced error.

- **It contradicts the federally-backed growth narrative.** MWAA is about to ask Washington and the rating agencies to fund a tripling of Dulles capacity [Source: https://www.ffxnow.com/2026/07/17/mwaa-almost-ready-to-unveil-dulles-expansion-plan-sought-by-trump/]. Foreclosing runway-end land undercuts the story the same board is telling.

- **There is a clean, board-blessed alternative — and MWAA is already using it.** In 2018 MWAA sold Digital Realty a 424-acre parcel for $236.5M and applied proceeds to reduce airline costs [Source: https://baxtel.com/data-center/digital-realty-dulles-expansion]. Data-center revenue is legitimate MWAA strategy — **on non-surface land, not in the RWY 19R/1L surfaces.**

- **The FAA has a specific, citable safety test the planner only gestured at.** FAA's thermal-exhaust-plume guidance treats vertical plume velocities above **4.3 m/s** as a potential hazard to aircraft [Source: https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/airports/environmental/land_use/Technical-Guidance-Assessment-Tool-Thermal-Exhaust-Plume-Impact.pdf]. An energy-intensive data center with on-site generation is exactly the plume source that guidance exists for.

- **The credit is strong today and financed on that strength.** MWAA airport-system revenue bonds carry AA-/AA-/Aa3 (S&P/Fitch/Moody's), stable, with Fitch citing dual-hub resilience, balanced carrier mix, and *capital-program progression* [Source: https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/mwaa-goes-to-market]. A self-inflicted federal-compliance overhang is the kind of governance signal that erodes exactly those attributes.

## The federal stack (where the thesis depends on authorizations not yet secured)

- **Grant assurances bite because MWAA takes AIP.** The planner's cited assurances — #19 Operation & Maintenance, #20 Hazard Removal, #21 Compatible Land Use, #22 Economic Nondiscrimination, #29 ALP — are the right ones. Add **#25 (Airport Revenue)**: proceeds from any airport land must be reinvested in the airport, or it is revenue diversion under 49 USC 47107/47133 [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/resources/publications/orders/compliance_5190_6/order-5190-6c-compliance-chapter-22].

- **ALP + Part 77, not a land-use review.** Adding a structure to the Airport Layout Plan requires FAA action, and construction in approach/departure surfaces triggers 14 CFR Part 77 obstruction evaluation (Form 7460). The 2025 review the memo describes was a height/environmental screen — it is **not** the federal action required, and it did not go to the FAA Airports District Office.

- **Land release is a high bar.** FAA may release federally-obligated land only on a finding that the release **benefits the airport** and creates no incompatible land use; "the non-aviation interest of the sponsor or community does not constitute an airport benefit" [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/resources/publications/orders/compliance_5190_6/order-5190-6c-compliance-chapter-22]. A data center in an approach surface is close to the textbook case the release standard is designed to stop.

## Governance — why this is politically survivable to *stop* and dangerous to *force*

MWAA's 17-member board is appointed **7 by the Virginia governor, 4 by the D.C. mayor, 3 by the Maryland governor, and 3 by the President of the United States** [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/about-airports-authority]. Two consequences for this decision:

1. **The federal government sits in the boardroom and holds the lease.** Three presidential appointees, plus a DOT landlord, plus (per the July 2026 reporting) an administration actively pushing expansion. A staff-level data-center deal that runs crosswise to FAA compliance is the kind of item that becomes a presidential-appointee's floor speech.
2. **No single jurisdiction controls the board, so durable strategy must be defensible on the merits, not the votes.** A land monetization that depends on airspace risk is fragile across a chair transition; a decision to protect strategic airfield land is defensible to any future chair regardless of who appointed them. Long-tenure survivability favors *preserving option value.*

## Long-tenure strategic pattern

The recurring large-hub error of 2000–2025 is trading **long-dated option value for near-term cash** — building over the land that turns out to be the only place a future runway, taxiway, or gate complex can go. Dulles was deliberately sited on ~11,000+ acres precisely to preserve that room; that founding premise is the asset. The hubs that banked land and expanded when demand arrived look prescient; the ones that foreclosed their own airfield (perimeter- and geometry-constrained hubs) spent the next 30 years paying for it. A data center in the RWY 19R/1L surfaces is that mistake in its purest, most irreversible form: **you cannot un-build it, and you cannot lengthen a runway through it.**

## The case FOR (steelman the CEO must answer)

- Cost-per-enplanement at IAD has a bad history (peaked at $26.47 in 2013; kept under $20 only with state grants and DCA transfers) and could climb steeply under a $20B+ CIP [Source: https://crankyflier.com/2018/08/28/it-may-be-a-mistake-for-united-to-grow-dulles/]. Non-aeronautical revenue is precisely the CPE shock-absorber that protects the airline relationship — and rating agencies *reward* non-aero diversification.
- MWAA already treats data-center land as a tool and is actively marketing undeveloped parcels for that use (widely reported; the 2018 Digital Realty sale is the documented precedent) [Source: https://baxtel.com/data-center/digital-realty-dulles-expansion].
- IAD already has four runways; if a fifth is never built, protecting this land "forever" is option-value hoarding that leaves real money on the table.

**The CEO's answer:** none of that requires *this* parcel. The steelman argues for the *strategy* (data-center revenue), not the *site* (the approach/departure surfaces). Accept the strategy; reject the location.

## The case AGAINST (what the CEO carries to the board)

Approve the strategy, deny the site. This parcel puts a permanent structure in a Part 77 surface next to the RWY 19R approach lighting, introduces a plume source the FAA has a 4.3-m/s hazard test for, adds a wildlife-attractant stormwater facility under AC 150/5200-33, risks findings on five-plus grant assurances, requires an ALP action and FAA release the deal likely cannot earn, forecloses the capacity the administration and United are counting on, and hands United a grievance mid-renegotiation — all to capture a one-time land rent that a non-surface parcel would capture without any of the risk. In the CEO's currency, that is a losing trade.

## Data points / precedents a strategist can use

1. **The MWAA precedent cuts both ways:** 424 acres to Digital Realty, $236.5M, proceeds pledged to lower airline costs, 2018 — proof the *strategy* is legitimate and airline-accretive, and proof the *right site* is non-surface land [Source: https://baxtel.com/data-center/digital-realty-dulles-expansion].
2. **The FAA's own number:** vertical thermal-plume velocity >4.3 m/s = potential aircraft hazard, per FAA thermal-exhaust-plume assessment guidance [Source: https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/airports/environmental/land_use/Technical-Guidance-Assessment-Tool-Thermal-Exhaust-Plume-Impact.pdf].
3. **The scale mismatch:** record NoVa data-center land ~$6M/acre (Nov 2025) vs. a $20B+ Dulles CIP — the parcel's proceeds are <1–3% of the program it would jeopardize [Source: https://www.loudounnow.com/business/data-center-sale-sets-new-loudoun-price-record/article_d392a250-e672-46c2-b2d2-f0ad3424a100.html; https://www.ffxnow.com/2026/07/17/mwaa-almost-ready-to-unveil-dulles-expansion-plan-sought-by-trump/].
4. **The credit MWAA is spending:** AA-/AA-/Aa3 stable, financed on "capital-program progression" and system resilience — attributes a compliance fight erodes [Source: https://www.bondbuyer.com/news/mwaa-goes-to-market].
5. **The political counter-precedent:** data centers are drawing organized local opposition (Prince William's rejection of a 2,000-acre project) — the reputational tailwind is turning into a headwind [Source: https://wjla.com/news/local/prince-william-county-dulles-south-data-center-centers-northern-virginia-board-of-supervisors-woodbridge-rezoning-western-digital-gateway-amazon-web-services-community-protest-comprehensive-plan-industrial-zoning-tax-revenue].
