# Regulatory & Political Brief — Data Center Within IAD RWY 19R/1L Surfaces

**Scope:** What actually binds MWAA on a proposed data center inside Dulles's runway approach/departure surfaces — separating "the FAA requires this" (regulation) from "MWAA wants this" (institutional revenue politics). Written to strengthen the author's recommendation to not build.

---

## Bottom line for the writer

The author's instinct is right, but the strongest version of the argument is narrower and harder than the memo currently makes it. Three of the memo's five planning points are firm regulatory ground; two (thermal plume, "strategic land") are softer and should be repositioned so a project sponsor cannot pick them off. The decisive facts are structural and non-negotiable:

1. **Dulles land is federally owned.** DOT holds title; MWAA merely leases it (through 2100). A data center is a *land-use change on federal land*, not a private real-estate decision.
2. **Section 163 (FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018) already reduced the fight to exactly the three prongs the author quotes** — and a structure inside Part 77 surfaces, next to the approach lighting system, sits squarely inside two of them. This is the author's best legal weapon, and the memo doesn't name it.
3. **A Form 7460-1 airspace study is mandatory regardless of what MWAA planning decides** — the obstruction evaluation is an independent federal gate, not a courtesy coordination.

The real adversary is not the FAA. It is MWAA's own revenue incentive: MWAA has already monetized Dulles land for data centers once, and is simultaneously selling a $20B expansion. Frame the argument as *protecting the expansion MWAA just committed to*, not as blocking a data center.

## What is regulation (hard to move) vs. politics (MWAA's choice)

### Regulation — these bind and are not discretionary

**1. The airspace study is a federal gate that MWAA cannot waive.** Any construction within a runway approach surface requires notice to the FAA under **14 CFR § 77.9** on **FAA Form 7460-1**, triggering an Obstruction Evaluation/Airport Airspace Analysis. The output is a *Determination of No Hazard* or *of Hazard* — issued by the FAA, not MWAA. The memo's observation that the project "has not been coordinated with the FAA ADO" understates this: it is not merely uncoordinated, it is **missing a mandatory federal filing**. [Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-E/part-77]

**2. Section 163 has already defined the battlefield — and it favors the author here.** Section 163 of the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018 stripped the FAA of general authority to review on-airport land use and confined its remaining authority to development that: **(A) materially impacts the safe and efficient operation of aircraft; (B) adversely affects the safety of people or property on the ground; or (C) adversely affects the value of prior Federal investments to a significant extent.** These are the exact (A)/(B)/(C) criteria the author quotes. [Source: https://www.kaplankirsch.com/resources-and-news/faa-releases-guidance-on-section-163-and-limited-federal-control-over-airport-property/]

   - **This cuts both ways, and the writer must own the double edge.** Section 163 was *written to let airports build non-aeronautical revenue projects with less FAA friction.* A sponsor will argue the data center is precisely the kind of revenue development Congress meant to free from FAA second-guessing. The author's counter is that this project is not a neutral parcel: it sits **inside Part 77 approach/departure surfaces and adjacent to the RWY 19R approach lighting system**, so it trips prong **(A)** (impact on safe/efficient operation) and prong **(C)** (value of the runway and federally funded ALS/NAVAIDs). Once a prong is tripped, full FAA review and ALP approval revive. The argument should be built on prongs (A) and (C) explicitly — not on "compatible planning" generally, which Section 163 removed from FAA's reach.

**3. Federal ownership makes this stronger at Dulles than almost anywhere else.** DOT holds title to Dulles; MWAA operates under a federal lease (Metropolitan Washington Airports Act of 1986; transferred June 7, 1987; lease now extended through 2100). The FAA's **December 8, 2023 policy on "Processing Land Use Changes on Federally Acquired or Federally Conveyed Airport Land"** governs precisely this situation. Section 163's carve-out preserving FAA authority over *federally conveyed* land applies with full force. A data center inside the airfield surfaces on **federally owned land** is the strong-authority case, not the weak one. [Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/08/2023-27017/policy-regarding-processing-land-use-changes-on-federally-acquired-or-federally-conveyed-airport]

**4. Grant Assurance 20 is the author's cleanest hook — and it names the 7460 itself.** GA 20 (Hazard Removal and Mitigation), based on **49 U.S.C. § 47107(a)(9)**, obligates the sponsor to mitigate hazards *identified through the FAA Form 7460-1 obstruction evaluation.* GA 20 and § 47107(a)(9) require the sponsor to "clear and protect" the terminal airspace "required to protect instrument and visual operations." A structure the sponsor knowingly places inside the approach surface is the paradigm case GA 20 was written to prevent. [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/resources/publications/orders/compliance_5190_6/order-5190-6c-compliance-appendix-a]

**5. The wildlife/stormwater point is solid — cite the number.** FAA **AC 150/5200-33C** recommends that hazardous-wildlife attractants be sited **at least 10,000 feet** from any runway used by turbine aircraft, and that stormwater detention basins **remain totally dry between rainfalls** (or be paved to prevent vegetation/nesting). A wet retention pond inside the landing/departure corridor is a textbook attractant. This point is defensible; strengthen it with the 10,000-foot standard and the "dry basin" design rule rather than a general assertion. [Source: https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/150-5200-33C.pdf]

### Softer ground — reposition, don't lead with these

**6. Thermal plume: real FAA concept, weak as a standalone.** The FAA does treat thermal exhaust plumes as a potential hazard and has published a **"Thermal Exhaust Plume Impact on Airport Operations: Technical Guidance and Assessment Tool"** built on MITRE's *Exhaust-Plume-Analyzer* model. But the FAA's own characterization is that the **overall risk of flight disruption from thermal plumes is "low,"** significant mainly to light aircraft in calm/cold/unstable conditions. The frequently cited **4.3 m/s vertical-velocity threshold is an Australian CASA figure, not an FAA standard** — do not attribute it to the FAA. Use the plume point to argue that on-site generation **requires a plume assessment that has not been done**, not that a hazard is proven. Framed as "unassessed unknown," it reinforces the 7460 gap; framed as "proven hazard," a sponsor's consultant will dismantle it. [Source: https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/airports/environmental/land_use/Technical-Guidance-Assessment-Tool-Thermal-Exhaust-Plume-Impact.pdf]

**7. "Strategic land / future runway": true but not a Section 163 trigger by itself.** "We may want to extend the runway someday" and "it wasn't designated non-aeronautical in the master plan" are *planning* arguments, and Section 163 specifically removed generic planning from FAA's veto. They matter, but as **grant-assurance and ALP-process** arguments (GA 29, ALP currency), not as FAA-hazard arguments. Tie them to the concrete July 2025 master plan (below), which gives them force.

## The political economy — the actual force pushing the project

The regulatory case is winnable. The institutional case is where the project gets built anyway. The writer should name the incentive plainly:

- **MWAA has already sold Dulles land for data centers — successfully and profitably.** In 2018–19 MWAA sold **424 acres of "Western Lands" to Digital Realty for $236.5 million (~$558K/acre)**, now the "Digital Dulles" campus. MWAA's own press framing: the proceeds were "instrumental in the mission to hold down the future cost per enplaned passenger at Dulles." Data-center land is not a hypothetical revenue idea at MWAA; it is a proven playbook with a nine-figure precedent and an explicit airline-cost rationale. [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-announces-sale-424-acres-western-lands-dulles-international] [Source: https://www.bisnow.com/washington-dc/news/data-center/data-center-developer-to-buy-424-acre-site-next-to-dulles-airport-92924]

- **The distinguishing fact the author should press: Western Lands were peripheral fee parcels; this site is inside the airfield surfaces.** The precedent is not a precedent. Selling land *outside* Part 77 surfaces to lower CPE is exactly the Section 163 revenue play Congress blessed. Placing a structure *inside* the 19R/1L surfaces next to the ALS is the opposite. Do not let anyone equate the two.

- **The project contradicts MWAA's own live expansion commitment.** MWAA **adopted a new Dulles master plan in July 2025**; airlines **formally concurred on July 15, 2026**; and MWAA is preparing a **~$20 billion expansion to roughly 90 million annual passengers** — more than triple current volume — under active pressure from the Trump DOT (Secretary Duffy's Dulles "revitalization" initiative and RFI). Sterilizing prime airfield land for a data center is inconsistent with the capacity thesis MWAA just sold to its airlines and to Washington. **That inconsistency is the wedge.** [Source: https://www.ffxnow.com/2026/07/17/mwaa-almost-ready-to-unveil-dulles-expansion-plan-sought-by-trump/] [Source: https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/trumps-transportation-secretary-sean-p-duffy-launches-new-initiative-revitalize-dulles]

## Decision-calendar anchors (attach every argument to one)

- **The July 2025 master plan is the author's strongest procedural fact.** The site "was not designated as non-aeronautical development during the most recent master planning process" — and that process concluded *last year*, then was reaffirmed by airline concurrence on July 15, 2026. Adding this parcel now means amending a plan the board adopted twelve months ago and the airlines endorsed weeks ago. That is a board-visible reversal, not a staff-level land review.
- **The ALP is the gate.** GA 29 requires the ALP be kept current and that no modification inconsistent with the ALP proceed without FAA approval. A non-aeronautical structure inside the surfaces is an ALP change; on federally owned land inside Part 77 surfaces, FAA ALP review is not optional. The 2025 "Planning review" the author describes — "primarily focused on height and preliminary environmental considerations" — is not an ALP approval and cannot substitute for one.
- **The 7460 clock has not started.** Until Form 7460-1 is filed and a Determination issued, there is no federal airspace finding — and MWAA would be committing capital ahead of a mandatory federal determination that could come back "Hazard."

## Counter-case the writer must pre-empt (steelman)

1. **"Section 163 frees us to build revenue projects."** True for neutral parcels; false inside Part 77 surfaces adjacent to the ALS, which trips prongs (A) and (C) and revives full FAA authority.
2. **"We already did Digital Dulles."** Those were peripheral fee lands outside the surfaces; this is airfield-critical, federally owned land. Different legal category.
3. **"The plume/lighting risks are speculative."** Correct that they are *unassessed*, which is the point — the mandatory 7460 study and a plume assessment have not been done. Argue process gap, not proven hazard.
4. **"This lowers CPE."** So does not sterilizing land the $20B/90M-passenger plan may need. Revenue that undercuts capacity the airlines just endorsed is a bad trade the board should see.

## What can and cannot be moved, and on what timeframe

- **Cannot be moved on project timelines:** the Part 77 / 7460 airspace-study requirement; FAA's retained authority over federally owned Dulles land; grant assurances 19/20/21/29 (statutory, tied to accepted AIP funds — years-long compliance exposure, enforceable via FAA Part 16). These are federal law, not MWAA policy.
- **Can be moved — and is the real risk:** MWAA's own decision to pursue the site. The revenue incentive is durable and precedented. The counter-lever is equally institutional: the board's fresh July 2025 master plan and the airlines' July 2026 concurrence give the author a live, on-the-record commitment to protect airfield land. Fight this at the board/ALP level as a contradiction of MWAA's stated capacity strategy — not as a technical dispute the FAA will settle, because Section 163 may mean the FAA doesn't reach it unless prongs (A)/(C) are proven first.
