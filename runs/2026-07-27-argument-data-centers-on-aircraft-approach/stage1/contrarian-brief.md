# Contrarian Brief — Data Center at IAD: The Case Against the "Do Not Build" Recommendation

**Active thesis (what I am opposing):** An MWAA planner recommends that the Authority *not* move forward with the proposed data center because it sits within the RWY 19R departure / RWY 1L approach surfaces, risks light/plume/NAVAID/wildlife impacts, is not designated for non-aeronautical use in the current master plan, and would put five AIP grant assurances (#19, #20, #21, #22, #29) at risk.

**My position:** The planner has written a strong *conditions* memo and mislabeled it a *kill* memo. Every technical objection is a testable, mitigable, standard-FAA-process question — not a bright-line prohibition. The grant-assurance argument is not just weak; it points the other way, because revenue-generating non-aeronautical development is exactly what Assurance 24 (self-sustainability) rewards. And MWAA has *already done this at this airport*: in 2018 it sold 424 acres of Dulles "Western Lands" to Digital Realty for data centers and banked ~$207M to lower airline costs. The correct recommendation is "process it through the FAA and the ALP with binding mitigation conditions," not "don't build."

---

## 1. The strongest version of the counterargument (bullets)

- **The memo conflates "requires FAA adjudication" with "must be rejected."** Siting inside Part 77 approach/departure surfaces triggers a Form 7460-1 Notice of Proposed Construction and an FAA aeronautical study. That process exists precisely to answer these questions with data. Pre-empting it with a planner's veto substitutes precaution for evidence.

- **Every named hazard has a quantitative FAA screening standard and an established mitigation path.** Glare → FAA solar glare policy and the Sandia Solar Glare Hazard Analysis Tool. Thermal plume → FAA's *Thermal Exhaust Plume Impact* assessment tool and the 4.3 m/s vertical-velocity screen. Lights → shielding/directional-lighting conditions. NAVAID/EMI → 7460 electromagnetic study and setbacks. Wildlife/stormwater → AC 150/5200-33B-compliant subsurface or steep-sided detention. None is an inherent disqualifier; each is a design condition.

- **The grant-assurance case is backwards.** Assurance 24 obligates the sponsor to make the airport "as self-sustaining as possible." Non-aeronautical land that pays fair market value and lowers airline cost per enplanement *advances* the assurances the memo cites, provided operations are not impaired. FAA routinely approves non-aeronautical use via ALP amendment; it is a governed, ordinary transaction, not a violation.

- **MWAA has already run this exact play at this exact airport — successfully.** The 2018 Western Lands sale to Digital Realty ($236.5M gross; ~$207M netted into a fund to hold down Dulles CPE) is a direct, on-property precedent that the FAA, the ALP, and the grant-assurance framework accommodated. The burden is now on the "don't build" side to explain why the mechanism that worked in 2018 is impossible in 2026.

- **The opportunity cost is not hypothetical — it is the largest data-center market on Earth.** Loudoun/Ashburn ("Data Center Alley") carries an estimated 70% of global internet traffic and returns roughly $13 per dollar and ~30% of county tax revenue. Airport-adjacent, power- and fiber-served land here is among the most valuable non-aeronautical acreage in U.S. aviation. "Protect it just in case" has a nine-figure price tag that the memo never quantifies.

- **The "future runway extension" reservation is asserted, not demonstrated.** MWAA acquired the 854-acre Western Lands in 2005–2007 specifically to build a fourth runway *and then sold the surplus once the runway and support area were built.* A generic "we might extend 19R/1L someday" claim needs a capacity forecast and a master-plan trigger, not a reflex to bank every strategic acre indefinitely at IAD — an airport already famous for land abundance.

- **"Not designated in the last master plan" is a curable procedural gap, not a substantive bar.** ALPs are amended between master-plan cycles all the time. The remedy is an ALP amendment and (if needed) a land-use change evaluated under the same FAA principles — which is what the memo itself calls for — not abandonment of the project.

## 2. Evidence

**The Dulles precedent — MWAA already monetized non-aeronautical land for data centers and used it to cut airline costs.** In September 2018 MWAA sold 424 acres of Dulles "Western Lands" to Digital Realty for $236.5M; after transaction costs "just over $200 million will be put into a special fund dedicated to controlling Dulles International's cost per enplaned passenger," netting an estimated $207M "earmarked to reduce the costs it includes when calculating rentals, fees and charges it assesses" [Source: https://loudounnow.com/2018/09/18/424-acres-at-airport-sold-for-data-centers/] [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-announces-sale-424-acres-western-lands-dulles-international]. Critically, the same reporting notes the Western Lands were part of an 854-acre tract "acquired by the Airports Authority between 2005 and 2007 to construct a fourth runway and additional facilities," and that "after building the runway and support area, the Western Lands portion … remained undeveloped" — i.e., the future-capacity reservation was satisfied first, then the surplus was sold [Source: https://wtop.com/business-finance/2018/09/data-center-operator-to-pay-237m-for-land-near-dulles-airport/].

**Grant assurances reward self-sustainability, not aeronautical purity.** Assurances 24/25 require a rate structure that makes the airport "as self-sustaining as possible" and that airport revenue be spent on the airport — the exact use MWAA made of the 2018 proceeds [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/aip/grant_assurances/assurances-airport-sponsors-2025]. The FAA's own compliance framework treats non-aeronautical development as legitimate so long as it is at fair market value and doesn't impair aeronautical use; charging *below* FMV is the diversion risk, not the development itself [Source: https://www.faa.gov/airports/resources/publications/orders/compliance_5190_6/order-5190-6c-compliance-chapter-12]. Industry practice is unambiguous that airports are expected to grow non-aeronautical income to stay financially healthy [Source: https://www.aviationpros.com/airports/article/53082175/navigating-airport-land-use-regulations-to-grow-nonaeronautical-revenue].

**The hazards the memo lists are governed by quantitative, mitigable FAA screens — not prohibitions.**
- *Thermal plume:* The FAA publishes a *Thermal Exhaust Plume Impact on Airport Operations: Technical Guidance and Assessment Tool* [Source: https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/airports/environmental/land_use/Technical-Guidance-Assessment-Tool-Thermal-Exhaust-Plume-Impact.pdf]. The screening threshold in wide use is a **4.3 m/s (~850 fpm) critical vertical velocity**; plumes below it are not treated as a hazard, and above it a site-specific meteorological analysis — not automatic denial — is the prescribed step [Source: https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/GetDocument.aspx?tn=242934&DocumentContentId=76519]. FAA itself frames plume turbulence as occurring "only in a limited area above the stacks" [Source: https://www.aviationairportdevelopmentlaw.com/2012/11/articles/federal-aviation-administration-faa/faa-finally-issues-guidance-on-plume-hazards-to-aircraft-from-power-plants-or-does-it/]. A data center's mitigation levers are real: grid power instead of on-site generation, stack height/placement, and closed-loop cooling.
- *Glare/lights:* The FAA has a formal review policy for solar projects on federally obligated airports and adopted the Sandia Solar Glare Hazard Analysis Tool as the standard; approval turns on demonstrating "no potential for glint or glare" in the tower cab and no/low after-image on approach — a testable condition, routinely satisfied with siting, setbacks, and anti-reflective treatment [Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/05/11/2021-09862/federal-aviation-administration-policy-review-of-solar-energy-system-projects-on-federally-obligated]. Directional-lighting and shielding conditions are the analog for data-center fixtures.
- *Obstruction/NAVAID/EMI:* The Form 7460-1 aeronautical study is the designed vehicle to test surface penetration, NAVAID interference, and EMI, and to attach conditions [Source: https://www.airspaceconsultants.com/faa-7460-form] [Source: https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/pg-apch-dep-surface-protection-2022.pdf].

**The market context makes forgone value enormous.** Ashburn is "the densest data center market in the world," with "an estimated 70% of global internet traffic" passing through it, and Dulles sits directly behind it; data centers already generate ~30% of Loudoun's tax revenue and return roughly $13 per dollar [Source: https://lightyear.ai/blogs/ashburn-colocation-data-center-alley] [Source: https://virginiamercury.com/2026/07/06/how-virginia-became-the-worlds-data-center-capital-and-how-its-going/].

**Airport data-center leasing is now a national pattern, not an aberration.** Named recent examples include Buckeye Municipal Airport (2,069 acres, up to 20M sq ft, 1.8 GW), Eastern Iowa/Cedar Rapids ($750M project, ~$1B in 20-year property tax), Manassas Regional (Amazon, 20 acres, $27.7M, projected $4–6M/yr), and Colorado Springs' Peak Innovation Park — all leveraging airports' existing power and fiber [Source: https://www.landapp.com/post/maximizing-airport-land-with-data-centers].

## 3. Specific scenarios where the "do not build" thesis is wrong

1. **The 7460 study comes back clean or conditionable.** If the FAA aeronautical study finds no surface penetration, no NAVAID/EMI impact, a sub-4.3 m/s plume (grid-powered, no on-site generation), and a passing glare analysis, then the memo has recommended forgoing nine figures of revenue to avoid a risk the FAA itself concluded is manageable. In that world "protect the land anyway" is pure loss.

2. **A CPE crisis makes the revenue decisive.** If Dulles faces airline pushback on cost per enplanement (the very metric the 2018 sale was structured to protect), a fair-market data-center ground lease is one of the few levers that lowers airline charges *and* satisfies Assurance 24. Rejecting it can itself become the airline-relations and self-sustainability problem.

3. **The "future runway" never materializes.** IAD already has four runways and abundant land; if no master-plan capacity trigger is ever hit for a 19R/1L extension, the parcel sits idle for decades while the region's data-center demand peaks and moves elsewhere. The reservation cost is paid in full; the insurance is never claimed.

4. **A rival jurisdiction captures the load.** Ashburn power and land are constrained; Loudoun approvals are tightening. If MWAA passes, the tenant builds in Prince William, Fauquier, or out of state. MWAA keeps the airspace-surface purity and loses the revenue, the jobs, and the tax base to a neighbor — with zero safety benefit, because the data center gets built somewhere anyway.

5. **The mitigation set is cheaper than assumed.** On-site generation is the only element that meaningfully drives the plume risk; if the tenant is contractually bound to grid/utility power (standard in Data Center Alley), the plume objection largely evaporates, and with it much of the memo's airspace case.

## 4. Direct data points / quotes a strategist must address or concede

1. **"After transaction costs, just over $200 million will be put into a special fund dedicated to controlling Dulles International's cost per enplaned passenger."** [Source: https://loudounnow.com/2018/09/18/424-acres-at-airport-sold-for-data-centers/] — MWAA has already used data-center land at IAD to *lower* airline costs. The "grant-assurance violation" framing must reconcile with the Authority's own precedent that treated the same move as compliant and revenue-positive.

2. **The Western Lands were "acquired … between 2005 and 2007 to construct a fourth runway," and were sold only "after building the runway and support area."** [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-announces-sale-424-acres-western-lands-dulles-international] — the "protect for future runway capacity" argument was already tested at IAD and resolved by building the capacity first, then monetizing the surplus. A strategist must show why *this* parcel is different, with a forecast, not a reflex.

3. **"Plume velocities in excess of 4.3 m/s [are] considered a potential hazard … a more rigorous analysis including site-specific meteorology should be used if screening thresholds are exceeded."** [Source: https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/GetDocument.aspx?tn=242934&DocumentContentId=76519] — the thermal-plume concern is a *screen with a number and a next step*, not a veto. The memo asserts a hazard where the governing method asks for a measurement.

4. **Ashburn is "the densest data center market in the world," carrying "an estimated 70% of global internet traffic," with Dulles directly behind it.** [Source: https://lightyear.ai/blogs/ashburn-colocation-data-center-alley] — the opportunity cost of "protect it just in case" is set by the single most valuable data-center location in the world. The memo never puts a dollar figure against the risk it wants to avoid.

5. **FAA solar policy requires demonstrating "no potential for glint or glare" in the tower cab and no/low after-image on final approach — a case-by-case standard the FAA approves routinely.** [Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/05/11/2021-09862/federal-aviation-administration-policy-review-of-solar-energy-system-projects-on-federally-obligated] — the light-interference objection has a mature analytical and mitigation regime; "data centers make bright lights" is not the end of the analysis.

## 5. Most vulnerable causal assumption in the thesis

The memo's load-bearing assumption is **"proximity to sensitive airspace ⇒ incompatible use ⇒ reject."** The missing middle term is the FAA adjudication that converts proximity into a *measured* impact and a *conditioned* approval. Strip that step out and any near-airfield development — solar, warehousing, the 2018 Digital Realty campus — would be un-buildable. The thesis proves too much.

## 6. Strongest alternative course of action

**Route the proposal through the full FAA/ALP process with binding conditions, and negotiate a fair-market ground lease structured to hold down CPE — do not reject it.** Concretely: file the 7460-1; commission glare, plume (assuming utility power, no on-site generation), and EMI studies; require a compliant subsurface/steep-sided stormwater design under AC 150/5200-33B; and amend the ALP. Approve if and only if the studies clear — which is a very different memo than "do not move forward."

## 7. The concession that makes the original position more defensible

The planner is **right that this cannot proceed on a "project email," and right that the specific parcel — inside the approach/departure surfaces and near the RWY 19R ALS — is more sensitive than the 2018 Western Lands site.** The defensible version of the memo is: *"Do not approve the current, uncoordinated proposal as drawn; require FAA ADO coordination, a 7460 aeronautical study, glare/plume/EMI analyses, a compliant stormwater design, and an ALP amendment; and impose a no-on-site-generation lease condition. Reject only if those studies fail."* That is a gate, not a wall — and it is both safer and far more defensible than an outright veto that ignores MWAA's own precedent and the largest data-center market on Earth sitting on the other side of the fence.
