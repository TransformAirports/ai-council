# Proposed Data Center at IAD — RWY 19R/1L Approach Zone: Recommend Do Not Proceed

**To:** MWAA Executive Leadership
**From:** Jennifer I. Kandel, Airspace/Airfield Planner, Planning Department (MA-32)
**Date:** July 29, 2026

## Bottom line

Do not advance this project at the currently proposed location. The site sits inside the RWY 1L approach and RWY 19R departure surfaces of a Category II/III ILS runway with ALSF-2 approach lighting.[^1] Four independent federal obligations attach, each grounds for FAA denial on its own. The project has not been coordinated with the FAA Airport District Office, is not on the approved Airport Layout Plan, and rests on a 2025 review that treated it as a height-and-land-use question. It fails all three tests Section 743 of the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 preserves for FAA ALP jurisdiction.[^2]

## Why it holds

**Thermal plumes.** AIM § 7-6-16 names cooling-tower exhaust as a hazard producing airframe damage, aircraft upset, and possible engine failure, with turbulence "over 1,000 feet above the top of the stack or cooling tower," worst "in and around approach and departure corridors."[^3] FAA's 2015 Technical Guidance Memorandum classifies such plumes as "incompatible with airport operations,"[^4] and FAA applied the same finding to a 200-MW facility 2.6 miles from Long Beach Airport in CEC Docket 15-AFC-01.[^5] This proposal places the same cooling hardware inside 0.5 miles of the RWY 19R threshold.

**Wildlife attractant.** AC 150/5200-33C sets a 10,000-foot exclusion for open-water hazardous wildlife attractants around runways serving turbojet aircraft.[^6] The proposed stormwater retention basin sits inside 2,500 feet of the threshold — a 7,500-foot shortfall against a numeric standard.

**ILS integrity.** FAA Order 6750.16E is explicit: "Placing an object outside the critical area does not guarantee non-interference with the ILS signal in space."[^7] Data centers combine large metallic mass, UPS and generator emissions, and thermal turbulence — three interference vectors against a Cat II/III localizer and glideslope. FAA Technical Operations owns that certification and is not a party to any lease.

**Grant Assurance exposure.** Assurances 19 (Operation and Maintenance), 20 (Hazard Removal and Mitigation), and 29 (Airport Layout Plan) each attach independently.[^8] Enforcement under 14 CFR Part 16 can order corrective action, withhold future AIP grants, and impose civil penalties up to three times any illegally diverted revenue.[^9]

## Strongest objection

The strongest counter is MWAA's own precedent: the 2018 sale of 424 acres of Western Lands to Digital Realty for $236.5 million, cleared through NEPA and an ALP amendment.[^10] That transaction proves data center use is achievable at IAD — sited outside the approach and departure surfaces. It argues for a different parcel, not for this one. Section 743 is often read as narrowing FAA authority, but the statute preserves jurisdiction where a project (a) materially impacts safe aircraft operations, (b) endangers people or property on the ground from those operations, or (c) devalues prior federal investment.[^2] This site triggers all three. FAA will assert authority within the 45-day Notice of Intent window.

## What to do now

1. **Halt project advancement** pending formal FAA ADO coordination under ARP SOP 2.00.
2. **File FAA Form 7460-1** and require a Determination of No Hazard covering height, exterior lighting, plume dispersion, and NAVAID compatibility.[^11]
3. **Redirect the deal, not the developer.** Direct Commercial Real Estate to identify alternative parcels within the 2025 Master Plan's designated non-aeronautical zones; the Western Lands envelope is a working precedent.
4. **Route Grant Assurance exposure to General Counsel.** Any ALP amendment for this parcel should re-enter the master-plan process — not a project-level review.
