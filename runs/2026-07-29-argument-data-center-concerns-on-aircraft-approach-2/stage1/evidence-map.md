# Evidence Map — Data Center Concerns on Aircraft Approach

**Runway of concern:** RWY 01L/19R at IAD, 9,400 ft × 150 ft, ILS/DME, ALSF-2 approach lights, Cat I/II/III. Highest-criticality approach lighting in the FAA inventory.

## Load-bearing facts (ranked by strength for the memo)

1. **AIM § 7-6-16 names cooling towers as a plume hazard.** Verbatim: plume "significant turbulent effects … can extend to heights of over 1,000 feet above the height of the top of the stack or cooling tower"; hazards "most critical during low altitude flight in calm and cold air, especially in and around approach and departure corridors." Change 3, September 5, 2024. Primary FAA source. Load-bearing on thermal plume.
2. **FAA September 24, 2015 Technical Guidance Memorandum on Thermal Exhaust Plume Impact.** Verbatim: plumes are "incompatible with airport operations." Same document family the FAA used at Long Beach (CEC Docket 15-AFC-01, Puente Power Project). Direct precedent — same regulatory framework, same class of thermal source.
3. **AC 150/5200-33C wildlife-attractant standard.** 10,000-foot separation from turbojet runway ends for hazardous wildlife attractants; open-water retention basins are the named case. Proposed basin sits inside 2,500 ft of threshold. Numeric shortfall of 7,500 ft against a specific dimensional standard. Strongest quantitative anchor in the entire record.
4. **Section 743, FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024, preserves three FAA veto grounds.** Verbatim tests: (a) material impact on safe and efficient aircraft operations; (b) safety of people/property on the ground from aircraft operations; (c) adverse effect on value of prior federal investments. All three apply. FAA October 3, 2024 Preliminary Instructions Memorandum confirms 45-day NOI window is procedural, not substantive.
5. **FAA Order 6750.16E (ILS siting).** Verbatim: "Placing an object outside the critical area does not guarantee non-interference with the ILS signal in space." Data center combines metallic mass, UPS/generator emissions, and thermal disturbance — three interference vectors against a Cat II/III localizer/glideslope. FAA Technical Operations owns NAVAID certification and is not a lease party.
6. **Grant Assurances 19, 20, 29 — enforceable contractual obligations.** 20-year run-with-grant. Part 16 enforcement can order corrective action, withhold AIP grants, and impose civil penalties up to 3× illegally diverted revenue (49 U.S.C. § 46301(a)(3)).
7. **MWAA precedent supports use, not this site.** 2018 Western Lands sale, 424 acres to Digital Realty, $236.5M — cleared through NEPA + ALP amendment, sited outside approach/departure surfaces. Precedent argues for a different parcel, not for the proposed one.
8. **Loudoun County density is real but geometrically distinct.** ~26M sq ft of operating data centers exist near IAD; contrarian correctly notes no documented NTSB/ASRS/FAA enforcement action tied to them. None sit inside an ILS approach corridor. The absence of an accident is not permission.

## Duplication and disagreement (reconciled)

- **AC 150/5200-33 version.** QA cites 33B; virtual-chris and regulatory-political cite 33C. Use **33C** (current). The 10,000-ft standard for turbojet runways is consistent across both.
- **RPZ-prohibition language.** Airport-COO brief says AC 150/5300-13B "categorically prohibits enclosed occupied structures." Contrarian correctly notes the AC's enumerated prohibitions are narrower (residences, public assembly, fuel storage), with occupied structures "discouraged," not banned. **The memo must NOT overclaim a categorical RPZ prohibition.** Rely instead on the approach/departure surface protection framework (Sept. 2022 FAA Policy Guidance), which does not require penetration.
- **Wildlife AC distance.** Chief-engineer cites 5,000 ft; QA/virtual-chris cite 10,000 ft. AC 150/5200-33C uses **10,000 ft for turbojet-serving airports** (IAD qualifies) and 5,000 ft for piston-only. Use 10,000 ft.
- **Grant Assurance 21 scope.** Contrarian argues #21 governs off-airport land only. The memo should **not lean on #21 or #22** — safer to rely on #19 (operation and maintenance), #20 (hazard removal and mitigation), and #29 (ALP compliance), which are uncontested on this fact pattern.
- **Section 743.** Contrarian frames it as narrowing FAA authority; regulatory-political and virtual-chris confirm the three preserved veto grounds. Both are true; the resolution is that Section 743 codifies the exact criteria Jen already cites — it strengthens the position rather than weakening it.

## What the memo must NOT claim

- **No claim of "categorical" RPZ prohibition of any structure.** The FAA discourages, and Order 5190.6C/AC 150/5190-4B treat compatibility case-by-case.
- **No claim of documented data center–caused aircraft incidents at IAD or Loudoun County.** None exist in the public record.
- **No reliance on Grant Assurances #21 or #22** — both are contested and unnecessary given #19/20/29.
- **No claim that Section 743 forbids this project outright** — it preserves FAA jurisdiction; FAA still has to assert it within the 45-day window.
- **No claim that AIM § 7-6-16 is unambiguously "settled law" for data center HVAC.** The AIM names cooling towers by hardware class; it is the strongest applicable authority but not a bright-line rule for every HVAC configuration. Frame accordingly.
- **No specific dollar estimate of AIP grants at risk** unless supplied by MWAA Finance; virtual-chris flagged this as unquantified.
- **No claim that the 2018 Digital Realty transaction site was inside approach surfaces** — it was expressly outside them.

## Decision anchor for the memo

The regulatory event that governs the decision is not a board vote or a lease signature — it is the Form 7460-1 filing and the FAA aeronautical study that follows. Every commitment made before that study returns is made against an undefined regulatory outcome. The correct posture is: complete the FAA processes; site the deal on the Western Lands–type envelope where MWAA has already demonstrated a workable path; do not build inside the approach and departure surfaces of a Cat II/III ILS runway.
