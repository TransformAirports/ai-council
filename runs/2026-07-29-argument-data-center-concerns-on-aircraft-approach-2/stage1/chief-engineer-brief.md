# Chief Engineer Brief: Proposed Data Center at IAD — Airfield and Regulatory Assessment

**To:** Leadership  
**From:** Airspace/Airfield Planning (MA-32), supported by Engineering Review  
**Re:** Proposed data center sited within RWY 19R/1L approach and departure surfaces  
**Date:** July 2026

---

## Bottom line

Do not move forward with the proposed data center in the currently described location. The site sits within the approach and departure surfaces of RWY 19R and RWY 1L — land MWAA is federally obligated to protect from incompatible development. At least five AIP Grant Assurances are at risk, FAA ADO has not been consulted, and four independent safety mechanisms — approach surface encroachment, approach light interference, thermal plume hazard, and wildlife attractant — each stand alone as grounds for rejection under current FAA standards.

---

## Why it holds

**Strategic land protection.** Being within an approach or departure surface without physically penetrating it does not remove the site from FAA scrutiny. AC 150/5300-13B (Airport Design, March 2022, Tables 3-2 through 3-5) and FAA's September 2022 Policy Guidance on Approach and Departure Surface Protection together establish that airport sponsors must protect these corridors from development that forecloses future runway extension, instrument procedure modification, or threshold repositioning. Once a permanent structure occupies this land, those options close — often for the 50-year life of the building. This area was not designated for non-aeronautical development in the current Master Plan. Per FAA ARP SOP 2.00, placing any new use on the ALP requires ADO review against three explicit criteria: (A) material impact on safe and efficient aircraft operations; (B) adverse effect on safety of people or property on the ground from aircraft operations; (C) adverse effect on the value of prior federal investments. This proposal triggers all three.

**Approach light interference.** 14 CFR §139.311 requires all airport building and area lighting to be "adequately adjusted or shielded to prevent lighting interference." A large-scale data center in this corridor — with server room status lighting, cooling equipment panels, perimeter security arrays, and generator warning lights — generates diffuse, broad-spectrum illumination exactly where pilots are transitioning from instrument to visual reference. No engineering study addressing this specific risk has been submitted for review.

**Thermal plume hazard.** FAA Aeronautical Information Manual §7-6-16 warns that cooling tower exhaust plumes can extend more than 1,000 feet above the structure and that encounters "may include airframe damage, aircraft upset, and/or engine damage/failure." These effects are most pronounced in calm, cold, stable air — precisely the conditions that produce IAD's low-visibility instrument approaches. Large-scale data centers require substantial cooling infrastructure. The FAA's Technical Guidance Assessment Tool for Thermal Exhaust Plume Impact treats power-generation and cooling tower exhaust as within scope of this hazard category.

**Wildlife attractant.** AC 150/5200-33C prohibits new water impoundments of one-quarter acre or larger within an approach corridor and within 5,000 feet of the runway end. The stormwater retention facility proposed alongside the data center falls within that zone relative to RWY 19R. The AC's separate 5-statute-mile buffer — applied when an attractant could draw wildlife into approach or departure airspace — may impose a further constraint on site design.

**Grant Assurance exposure.** Proceeding without FAA ADO concurrence exposes MWAA to documented violations of Assurances #19 (no interference with operations), #20 (no establishment of new hazards), #21 (compatible land uses), #22 (airport available for aeronautical activities), and #29 (ALP maintained and approved). Under 14 CFR Part 16, any airline certificated at IAD can file a formal complaint. Sanctions include corrective-action orders, withholding of future AIP grants, and grant repayment.

---

## Strongest objection

The developer will argue that the structure does not penetrate the approach or departure surface, that modern data centers can shield EMI and direct lighting downward, and that MWAA's own 2018 sale of 424 acres of Western Lands to Digital Realty for $236.5 million proves data center development is compatible with IAD. Each point is answerable. Non-penetration is not the FAA standard for approach surface protection — the 2022 policy guidance goes well beyond physical height clearance. EMI shielding and lighting mitigation require FAA validation through a 7460-1 aeronautical study, not a developer's engineering assurance. And the Western Lands site was expressly positioned outside IAD's approach and departure surfaces, in a corridor already excluded from residential development by Loudoun County noise overlay — a fundamentally different location with a different planning and regulatory history. That precedent supports a compatible data center development sited correctly, not this one.

---

## What to do now

1. **Halt site plan review** and formally notify FAA ADO Eastern Region (Airports) of the proposal for consultation under ARP SOP 2.00 before any permit advances.
2. **Commission a 7460-1 aeronautical study** — scope must include building height, exterior lighting interference with the ALS, thermal plume dispersion modeling, and EMI impact on ILS, VOR, and ATC communications. Require a FAA Determination of No Hazard before construction permits are issued.
3. **Conduct a formal wildlife hazard site assessment** under AC 150/5200-33C to quantify the stormwater retention facility's distance from the RWY 19R runway end and approach corridor; require redesign, burial, or complete elimination of any open water impoundment that fails the 5,000-foot and approach-corridor tests.
4. **Engage MWAA General Counsel** to inventory the five Grant Assurance risks and obtain FAA informal guidance on whether a Land Release or ALP amendment pathway exists for this specific location — and what conditions, if any, would be attached.
