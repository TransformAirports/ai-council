# Contrarian Brief: Against the Categorical Rejection of the IAD Data Center Proposal

**Position being opposed:** Jennifer Kandel's memo recommending MWAA "not move forward with construction of a data center in the currently proposed location" at IAD, on grounds of approach surface risk, approach lighting interference, thermal plumes, wildlife attractants, land use incompatibility, and violations of AIP Grant Assurances #19–22 and #29.

**Contrarian position:** The memo correctly identifies process gaps — ADO coordination not done, no 7460-1 filing, no ALP amendment — but then misuses those gaps to justify a categorical veto. Every substantive safety concern in the memo is either (a) speculative and properly resolved through the FAA's defined evaluation processes, not assumed away by a planner, or (b) a design problem with standard solutions, not a site-disqualifying defect. The correct recommendation is: *complete the required FAA processes before construction*. Not: *do not move forward.*

---

## 1. Strongest Version of the Counterargument (Seven Bullets)

- **MWAA already set the precedent at IAD.** In 2018, MWAA completed an ALP amendment, NEPA Environmental Assessment, and FAA coordination to remove 424 acres of Dulles airport land from aeronautical status and sell it to Digital Realty for $236.5 million in data center development — the "Digital Dulles" campus. The FAA reviewed and approved data center use on IAD-complex property under the exact same grant assurance and ALP framework Jen cites as prohibitive. If the process was adequate in 2018, it remains adequate now. Invoking GA #29 (ALP) as a prohibitive barrier while ignoring that MWAA successfully cleared that barrier for a *larger* data center project at the same airport is a contradiction, not a regulatory argument. [Source: https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-announces-sale-424-acres-western-lands-dulles-international]

- **The 7460-1 / OE/AAA process exists precisely to answer Jen's "we don't know" questions.** Jen writes: "We also don't know if there will be impacts to navigational aids, communications, radar or any other electronic interference with FAA equipment." That is literally what FAA Form 7460-1 and the Obstruction Evaluation / Airport Airspace Analysis (OE/AAA) process is designed to determine. The FAA coordinates the 7460-1 study with local ATCT, TRACON, and facility engineering, then issues either a "No Hazard Determination" with mitigation conditions, or a Determination of Hazard. The absence of that filing is the problem — but filing it is the solution. Citing uncertainty as a reason to reject the project before asking the FAA to evaluate it substitutes a planner's instinct for the FAA's statutory evaluation authority. [Source: https://oeaaa.faa.gov/; 14 CFR Part 77]

- **AIM § 7-6-16 does not apply to data center HVAC.** The FAA's own exhaust plume hazard guidance defines exhaust plumes as "visible or invisible emissions from power plants, industrial production facilities, or other industrial systems that release large amounts of vertically directed unstable gases." Data centers generate waste heat through rooftop HVAC and cooling towers — not industrial stacks releasing combustion gases under pressure. The documented hazards in AIM § 7-6-16 (oxygen depletion, particulate contamination, gaseous oxides, high-velocity vertical flow) are attributes of industrial combustion exhaust, not commercial building mechanical systems. The memo cites no FAA guidance, AOSC finding, or incident report linking data center HVAC to aircraft upset. This claim requires evidence, not assertion. [Source: FAA AIM § 7-6-16, https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap7_section_6.html]

- **Grant Assurance #21 governs the surrounding community, not the airport's own property.** The exact text of GA #21 reads: "The Airport Sponsor will take appropriate action, to the extent reasonable, to restrict the use of land *in the vicinity of the airport* to activities compatible with normal airport activities." This assurance obligates MWAA to constrain incompatible development in the surrounding community (the source of the on-airport noise complaint, encroachment, and hazard problems the assurance was designed to address). It does not restrict what MWAA builds on its own obligated property — that is governed separately by GA #19, which applies to airport facilities funded with federal AIP grants, not to every acre MWAA owns. Applying GA #21 to an on-airport data center inverts its purpose. [Source: FAA AIP Sponsor Assurances, https://mm.nh.gov/files/uploads/dot/remote-docs/grant-assurance-21.pdf]

- **Not penetrating the approach surface is the compliance standard, not mere proximity.** Jen acknowledges the project "doesn't penetrate these surfaces." Under 14 CFR Part 77 and AC 150/5300-13, the threshold that triggers a finding of hazard — and requires a Notice of Presumed Hazard through the 7460-1 process — is actual penetration of the imaginary surface, not location beneath it. The FAA's Approach/Departure Surface Protection Policy (2022) is explicit: structures trigger heightened review when they penetrate the surface. A structure within but not penetrating the surface requires a 7460-1 study to evaluate aeronautical effect, not a categorical rejection. The argument that MWAA should decline the project to protect its speculative *future* ability to extend the runway is a master planning consideration — not a current regulatory violation. [Source: 14 CFR § 77.19; FAA Policy Guidance: Approach and Departure Surface Protection, 09/19/2022]

- **The FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 narrowed FAA's ALP review authority over non-aeronautical projects.** Section 743 of the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 amended FAA's ALP approval authority to cover only changes that (1) materially impact safe and efficient aircraft operations, (2) adversely affect the safety of people or property on the ground, or (3) adversely affect the value of prior federal investments. For non-aeronautical development on sponsor-acquired land, a new Notice of Intent (NOI) process was established — a simplified pathway that limits FAA's regulatory reach over how airports develop their non-aeronautical property. The memo cites GA #29 as a prohibitive barrier without acknowledging this updated framework, which explicitly facilitates exactly the kind of development being proposed. [Source: https://alerts.aaae.org/latest/regulatory_100524]

- **Loudoun County surrounds IAD with 26 million square feet of data centers, and the FAA has not found an incompatibility.** Loudoun County — which abuts Dulles on three sides and sits directly under IAD's approach and departure corridors — already hosts more than 26 million square feet of operating data centers, with plans for tens of millions more. These facilities operate within and adjacent to the airport traffic environment daily. No documented NTSB investigation, ASRS report, or FAA enforcement action attributable to data center operations (lights, HVAC, electromagnetic emissions) on IAD approach paths has been identified. The claimed hazards, if real at commercial HVAC scale, would already have materialized. [Source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/loudoun-county-is-home-to-26-million-sq-ft-of-data-centers-dulles-cloud-south/; Loudoun County Economic Development]

## 2. Evidence Section

**MWAA's 2018 Western Lands Data Center Transaction**
In September 2018, MWAA's Board of Directors approved the sale of 424 acres of Dulles airport property to Digital Realty Trust for $236.5 million, with the explicit intent of developing a large-scale data center campus. The transaction required an ALP amendment subject to NEPA review and FAA approval — the same process Jen frames as an insurmountable barrier. The Environmental Assessment was prepared per FAA policies and procedures. Revenue was restricted to aeronautical purposes per grant assurance obligations. The project proceeded.
[Source: MWAA Press Release, https://www.mwaa.com/news/airports-authority-announces-sale-424-acres-western-lands-dulles-international; Data Center Dynamics, https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/digital-realty-submits-plans-large-dulles-data-center-campus/]

**FAA OE/AAA Process as the Correct Mechanism**
The FAA's Obstruction Evaluation / Airport Airspace Analysis system at oeaaa.faa.gov exists to evaluate proposed construction for aeronautical effect, including electromagnetic interference with navaids, radar, and comms. Structures filed through the 7460-1 process receive a formal Determination — either No Hazard (often with mitigation conditions such as marking and lighting) or Hazard. The process takes 45–90 days. A No Hazard Determination is valid for 18 months and is renewable. This is the FAA's designated process for resolving exactly the unknowns Jen lists.
[Source: FAA OE/AAA, https://oeaaa.faa.gov/; AOPA Tall Towers Brief, https://www.aopa.org/advocacy/advocacy-briefs/air-traffic-services-process-brief-tall-towers-the-faas-obstruction-evaluation-process]

**AIM § 7-6-16 Scope Limitation**
AIM § 7-6-16 defines exhaust plumes as emissions from "power plants, industrial production facilities, or other industrial systems that release large amounts of vertically directed unstable gases." Data centers and commercial buildings are not mentioned. The documented risks — oxygen depletion, gaseous oxide exposure, particulate engine contamination, high-temperature buoyancy effects — are characteristics of industrial combustion, not commercial HVAC. The FAA's Technical Guidance Assessment Tool for Thermal Exhaust Plume Impact was developed for industrial facilities near airports, not commercial buildings.
[Source: FAA AIM § 7-6-16, https://faraim.org/faa/aim/chapter-7/section-7-6-16.html; FAA Technical Guidance Assessment Tool for Thermal Exhaust Plume Impact, https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/airports/environmental/land_use/Technical-Guidance-Assessment-Tool-Thermal-Exhaust-Plume-Impact.pdf]

**Grant Assurance #21 Exact Text**
"The Airport Sponsor will take appropriate action, to the extent reasonable to restrict the use of land in the vicinity of the airport to activities compatible with normal airport activities." This assurance imposes obligations on surrounding off-airport land use, not on the sponsor's own on-airport development decisions. The assurance originates in 49 U.S.C. §§ 47107(a)(10) and 47141, which were enacted to address community encroachment problems, not to restrict airport development on airport land.
[Source: FAA Airport Sponsor Assurances, Grant Assurance 21, https://mm.nh.gov/files/uploads/dot/remote-docs/grant-assurance-21.pdf]

**RPZ Prohibited Uses Inventory**
The FAA's actual list of prohibited uses within Runway Protection Zones includes: residences, places of public assembly, and fuel storage facilities. The FAA discourages — but does not categorically prohibit — structures within the RPZ when they are outside the Runway OFA and do not attract wildlife or interfere with navaids. Automobile parking (under conditions), agricultural uses, golf courses, irrigation channels, airport service roads, and unstaffed navigational aids are all listed as permitted or conditionally permitted.
[Source: FAA Interim Guidance on Land Uses Within a Runway Protection Zone, https://crp.trb.org/acrpwebresource13/faa-interim-guidance-on-land-uses-within-a-runway-protection-zone/; ACRP RPZ Risk Assessment Tool, https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/24662/chapter/2]

**FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024, Section 743**
Section 743 narrowed FAA's ALP review authority for non-aeronautical projects, establishing an NOI (Notice of Intent) process for non-aeronautical development on sponsor-acquired land. FAA's jurisdiction over non-aeronautical portions of a project is explicitly limited. This is a 2024 change that postdates most of the guidance Jen cites and reduces the regulatory friction associated with non-aeronautical development on airport property.
[Source: AAAE Regulatory Alert, https://alerts.aaae.org/latest/regulatory_100524]

**Loudoun County Data Center Density**
Loudoun County, Virginia — which surrounds Dulles International Airport and sits under its major approach and departure corridors — hosts more than 26 million square feet of operating data centers as of 2026, with proposals for an additional 56 million square feet in the "Dulles Cloud South" corridor directly south of the airport. No FAA enforcement action, NTSB investigation, or documented incident linking data center HVAC plumes, lighting, or electromagnetic emissions to aircraft operations on IAD approaches has been publicly identified.
[Source: Data Center Dynamics, https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/loudoun-county-is-home-to-26-million-sq-ft-of-data-centers-dulles-cloud-south/; Virginia Mercury, https://virginiamercury.com/2026/07/06/how-virginia-became-the-worlds-data-center-capital-and-how-its-going/]

## 3. Specific Scenarios Where the Thesis Is Wrong

**Scenario A: The 7460-1 returns a No Hazard Determination.**
The FAA's OE/AAA study, which has not been filed, evaluates navaid interference, radar shadowing, and obstruction effect. If it returns a No Hazard Determination — as it does for thousands of structures near airports annually — every "unknown" Jen raises is resolved by the FAA's own determination. The memo pre-empts that determination without evidence. A No Hazard with lighting/marking conditions is the most probable outcome for a structure that does not penetrate approach surfaces.

**Scenario B: The data center uses liquid cooling.**
Modern hyperscale and edge data centers increasingly use direct liquid cooling (DLC) or immersion cooling systems that eliminate rooftop heat rejection entirely. If the proposed facility uses liquid cooling, the thermal plume concern is architecturally moot. The memo was written without knowledge of the cooling technology. A planner cannot reject a project on thermal plume grounds when the design may preclude plumes.

**Scenario C: Exterior lighting is shielded and aviation-compliant.**
FAA AC 70/7460-1L governs obstruction marking and lighting for structures. If the data center uses downward-facing, full-cutoff exterior fixtures with aviation-compliant design, the approach light pattern interference claim is designed away. Data centers do not inherently require upward-facing or high-intensity lighting. This is an engineering specification, not a categorical incompatibility.

**Scenario D: The stormwater facility is redesigned as a covered cistern.**
AC 150/5200-33 concerns about wildlife attractants from open-water retention facilities are standard in airport planning. The solution is not to reject the site but to require a covered detention system or underground cistern as a design condition. This is a routine mitigation used at airports nationwide — it is not a site-disqualifying defect.

**Scenario E: The master plan is updated through standard ALP amendment.**
Jen notes the area was not designated for non-aeronautical development in the most recent master plan. But master plans are documents, not legislation. MWAA has already completed an ALP amendment for data center development at IAD (Western Lands, 2018). The process — Environmental Assessment, FAA coordination, ALP revision — exists precisely to add new uses not originally contemplated. "It's not on the current ALP" is not a prohibition; it is the starting condition for the amendment process.

## 5. The Concession That Makes the Original Position Defensible

Jen's memo is accurate on procedure and wrong on substance. The accurate version of the recommendation is:

> *MWAA should not begin construction of the data center until the following FAA processes are completed: (1) Form 7460-1 is filed and an OE/AAA aeronautical study is obtained; (2) FAA ADO coordination on approach lighting, navaid, and radar interference is documented; (3) an Environmental Assessment is prepared per FAA/NEPA requirements; and (4) an ALP amendment is submitted and approved. Each of these processes may yield a pathway to approval. None has been completed. Proceeding to construction before they are completed exposes MWAA to grant assurance violations — specifically GA #20 (establishing a hazard without FAA evaluation) and GA #29 (building outside the approved ALP). But the correct order is: run the processes, then decide. Not: decide without running the processes.*

The current memo skips the evaluation and issues a verdict. That substitutes planning judgment for regulatory determination. It also leaves MWAA without the FAA-documented basis to reject the project if the sponsor pushes back — because MWAA hasn't asked the FAA yet.
