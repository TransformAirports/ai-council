# Run: Infrastructure vs. Operational Intelligence

## Thesis

Airports are over-investing in infrastructure and under-investing in operational intelligence. The next billion dollars of CapEx at major US hubs should be pressure-tested against a much smaller bet on software, data, and coordination systems that make existing assets meaningfully more productive.

## Audience

MWAA leadership, airport planners across the industry, and policy readers. Assume sophistication. Assume skepticism. Assume they've read McKinsey decks and are tired of them.

## Tone

Direct. Evidence-dense. Intellectually honest about the counter-argument. Slightly provocative but not polemical. Think Matt Levine on aviation, not a consultant thought leadership piece.

## Length

Final document: 8,000-10,000 words. This is a substantive piece, not a blog post.

## Output format

report

## What this is NOT

- A vendor pitch for any specific technology
- A criticism of MWAA's capital program specifically (use MWAA examples where relevant but the argument is industry-wide)
- A call for zero infrastructure investment (that position is indefensible)
- A piece that ignores the genuine reasons airports build things

## What this IS

- A rigorous argument that the *ratio* of infrastructure to operational intelligence investment is wrong
- A piece that forces engaged planners to defend their capital programs on new ground
- A demonstration that the counter-argument has been taken seriously and still loses on the specifics

## Success criteria

- Put comparable infrastructure and operational-intelligence investments on a common economic and operational basis, with assumptions and uncertainty made explicit.
- Present the strongest infrastructure-first counter-case fairly enough that an airport CFO, COO, airline partner, and chief engineer would recognize it.
- Translate the thesis into a sequenced airport decision: what to test first, who owns it, what approvals are required, and which measures determine whether a capital project should proceed.
- Trace every material number and attributed claim to a primary or clearly identified authoritative source.

## Research agent overrides

<!-- Deliberately broad: the thesis crosses capital economics, operations, airlines, governance, delivery, and organizational behavior. -->

**Excluded from this run:** director-of-public-safety, airport-emergency-management-director, virtual-christian, architectural-historian, deep-research

- **infrastructure-economist:** Compare capital expansion with operational-intelligence alternatives using lifecycle economics, debt effects, and cost per enplanement.
- **operations-analyst:** Identify where coordination, process design, and real-time decisions—not physical capacity—are the binding constraints.
- **technology-scout:** Separate deployable operational intelligence from vendor claims; emphasize integration, adoption, and measured results.
- **quantitative-analyst:** Build reproducible comparisons and sensitivity ranges for the signature economic argument.
- **contrarian:** Build the strongest evidence-based case that the apparent underinvestment in infrastructure is itself the larger risk.
- **chief-engineer:** Test which bottlenecks software cannot solve and where asset condition, standards, or constructability make capital unavoidable.
- **airline-commercial-strategist:** Explain carrier incentives, cost-per-enplanement sensitivity, hub economics, and airline responses to each investment path.
- **regulatory-political-analyst:** Identify funding, grant, procurement, labor, privacy, governance, and political constraints.
- **aviation-historian:** Test the thesis against prior cycles of capacity optimism, automation, and traffic growth.
- **airport-ceo:** Frame the board, bond-rating, airline-use-agreement, and long-horizon strategic decision.
- **airport-coo:** Test whether the operational-intelligence alternative can function during irregular operations and peak conditions.
- **airport-procurement-expert:** Compare delivery vehicles, acquisition speed, auditability, and vendor-lock-in risk.
- **slacker:** Look for a counterintuitive framing or evidence connection the structured panel may miss.
- **virtual-chris:** Find cross-department alliances and implementation opportunities hidden between capital, operations, technology, and airline programs.
- **virtual-pat:** Identify practical, low-cost experiments that can prove or disprove the thesis within 90 days.
